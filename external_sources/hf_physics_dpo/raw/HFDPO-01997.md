# HFDPO-01997

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a polarimetric sensor to monitor ocean surface roughness for climate modeling, but detects unexpected signal variations during solar noon. The sensor measures the degree of polarization (DoP) of reflected sunlight, expecting a smooth surface to yield low DoP (~0.1), but records DoP values spiking to 0.65 at certain angles. Given the sun’s elevation is 60°, the sensor’s viewing angle is 30° from nadir, and the sea surface has a wind speed of 8 m/s, what physical mechanism explains this anomaly, and how does it relate to the Fresnel reflection coefficients and the microscale wave orientation relative to the sensor's polarization axis?
