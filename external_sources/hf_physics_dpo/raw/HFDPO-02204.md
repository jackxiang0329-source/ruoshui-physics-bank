# HFDPO-02204

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system emits superheated fluid at 350°C into cold ocean water at 2°C. The vent’s effluent flows through a porous rock matrix with a thermal conductivity of 2.1 W/m·K and a cross-sectional area of 0.5 m². Over a 10-minute interval, 120 kg of water is ejected, and temperature measurements show the fluid cools to 90°C by the time it reaches the outer boundary. Estimate the rate of entropy generation within the rock matrix during this process, assuming the system is adiabatic to the external environment.
