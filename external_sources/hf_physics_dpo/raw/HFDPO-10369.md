# HFDPO-10369

During a deep-sea hydrothermal vent survey, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a plume of superheated fluid (350°C, ρ = 920 kg/m³) erupting vertically from a chimney at 1.2 m/s. The surrounding seawater is at 2°C and 1030 kg/m³. As the plume rises, it entrains ambient water and cools, reducing its density. A sensor records that at 15 m above the vent, the plume’s velocity has dropped to 0.8 m/s and its diameter has expanded to 2.4 m. Estimate the time-averaged entrainment coefficient (α) required to sustain this evolution, and discuss how deviations from this α might affect the plume’s trajectory and mixing efficiency.
