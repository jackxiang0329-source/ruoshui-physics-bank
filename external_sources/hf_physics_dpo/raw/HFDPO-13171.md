# HFDPO-13171

A satellite in low Earth orbit detects anomalous fluctuations in the local magnetic field every time it passes over a remote volcanic region. Scientists suspect these are caused by induced currents in the conductive basalt layer beneath the surface, driven by time-varying geomagnetic fields. Given that the vertical component of Earth’s magnetic field changes at a rate of 0.5 nT/s and the conductivity of the basalt is 1.0 S/m, estimate the magnitude of the induced electric field at a depth of 5 km directly beneath the surface. Assume the region is horizontally extensive and the field change is uniform over the area.
