# HFDPO-00344

A satellite in low Earth orbit (LEO) is experiencing unexpected orbital decay due to atmospheric drag, but the drag force isn't constant—density increases with atmospheric tides caused by the Moon’s gravitational pull. The satellite’s altitude drops from 450 km to 440 km over 30 days. Using the Moon’s tidal potential and the variation in atmospheric density with height, estimate the effective gravitational perturbation from the Moon that contributes to this drag-induced decay. Assume the satellite’s cross-sectional area is 2.5 m², mass is 1200 kg, and the atmospheric density at 450 km varies sinusoidally with a 12.4-hour period and amplitude 15% above average.
