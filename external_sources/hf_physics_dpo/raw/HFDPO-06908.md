# HFDPO-06908

A solar-powered water pump in a remote village fails at night, even though the panels charge a battery during the day. The battery voltage drops quickly when the pump starts. Engineers suspect the battery isn't the issue—could the problem lie in the diode used to protect the system from reverse current? The diode’s forward voltage drop is measured at 0.7 V, but the solar panel output drops to 5.5 V under load. If the battery needs at least 12 V to operate the pump efficiently, how much voltage is actually delivered to the battery when the diode is in forward conduction?
