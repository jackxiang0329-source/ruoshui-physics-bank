# HFDPO-00007

During a seismic survey in the Arctic, a research team detects a mysterious low-frequency vibration (1.2 Hz) propagating through permafrost layers beneath a 120-meter-thick ice sheet. The signal appears to originate from a subsurface aquifer, but its waveform shows unexpected phase shifts and amplitude decay inconsistent with standard wave models. Using borehole strain sensors spaced 30 meters apart, they record a 0.04-second delay between wave arrivals. Given that the ice has a Young’s modulus of 10 GPa and density of 917 kg/m³, and the permafrost layer has a shear modulus of 300 MPa and density of 1650 kg/m³, determine the effective wave speed in the composite medium and explain why the observed phase shift suggests the presence of a trapped mode.
