# HFDPO-04297

A quantum dot solar cell prototype exhibits unexpected efficiency drops under high-intensity sunlight. Spectral analysis reveals interference fringes in the emitted photoluminescence, with maxima spaced 1.8 nm apart in the visible spectrum (λ ≈ 550 nm). The device uses a 200-nm-thick indium gallium nitride (InGaN) active layer sandwiched between distributed Bragg reflector (DBR) mirrors. The top DBR has a refractive index of 2.5, while the bottom layer (SiO₂/TiO₂) has an index of 1.7. Determine whether the observed interference is due to Fabry–Pérot resonance or thin-film interference, and evaluate which structure—adjusting the DBR period or the active layer thickness—would more effectively suppress unwanted optical feedback without sacrificing charge carrier collection.
