# HFDPO-03162

A fusion reactor's plasma containment field relies on a toroidal magnetic field generated by current-carrying coils. Engineers observe unexpected electric field spikes near the inner wall of the vacuum chamber, despite the system being designed to maintain electrostatic neutrality. The chamber has a major radius of 5 m and a minor radius of 0.6 m, with charge density varying as ρ(r) = ρ₀(e⁻ʳ/α) inside the toroidal plasma (r measured from centerline). Given ρ₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻⁸ C/m³ and α = 0.3 m, determine the magnitude of the radial electric field at r = 4.8 m (just inside the inner wall) using Gauss’s Law.
