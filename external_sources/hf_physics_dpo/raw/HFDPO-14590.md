# HFDPO-14590

A neutrino detector deep underground in a former mine detects a burst of electron antineutrinos from a distant supernova remnant. The signal arrives over 10 seconds, with a peak rate of 3.2 × 10⁶ antineutrinos per square meter per second. The detector uses inverse beta decay on protons, producing a positron and neutron. Given the cross-section for this interaction is 1.2 × 10⁻⁴⁴ m² at the antineutrino energy of 8 MeV, and the detector contains 1.5 × 10³⁰ free protons, calculate the total number of detectable positron events expected during the burst, accounting for both the time-integrated flux and the detector’s angular acceptance of 0.6 steradians. Assume the supernova is 150,000 light-years away and the burst is isotropic.
