# HFDPO-03963

In a remote Arctic research station, a critical weather sensor array operates via a 120-meter-long copper cable buried underground. During winter, ice forms a 5 cm thick layer around the cable, reducing thermal conductivity. The cable carries 3.5 A and has a resistance per meter of 0.025 Ω/m. Due to a power surge, the cable overheats, risking insulation failure. Engineers must determine the minimum ambient temperature needed to keep the cable below 60°C, considering both resistive heating and heat loss through the ice layer (thermal conductivity 2.2 W/m·K).
