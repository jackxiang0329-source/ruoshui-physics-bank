# HFDPO-09762

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a complex, toroidal magnetic field to confine plasma at 15 million Kelvin, but stray electric fields from uneven charge distribution in the plasma are disrupting stability. Engineers detect an unexpected radial electric field of 2.4 × 10⁶ N/C at a radius of 1.8 m from the central axis, despite the vessel’s symmetric design. Given that the plasma is modeled as a non-uniform cylindrical volume charge density ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 − r/R) for r ≤ R (with R = 2.0 m), determine the total charge enclosed within a Gaussian surface of radius 1.8 m using Gauss’s law—then explain why the measured field does not match the field predicted by assuming uniform charge distribution.
