# HFDPO-01365

A lunar rover's thermal regulator uses a phase-change material (PCM) capsule filled with paraffin wax (melting point 60°C, latent heat 240 kJ/kg) to stabilize internal electronics during the 14-day lunar night. The capsule, initially solid at 30°C, is exposed to a constant heat sink of -170°C at the moon’s surface. Engineers observe that after 48 hours, the internal temperature remains stable at 58°C—just below the wax’s melting point. Estimate the effective thermal conductivity of the capsule’s insulation layer, assuming the PCM melts uniformly and the system behaves as a 1D semi-infinite solid with constant surface temperature.
