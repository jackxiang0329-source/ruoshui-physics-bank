# HFDPO-15329

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a deep borehole (1.2 km deep) to extract heat from hot rock formations. The rock has a uniform thermal conductivity of 2.4 W/m·K and a heat capacity of 800 J/kg·K. The borehole’s walls are insulated, but the rock is initially at 150°C. After 48 hours of continuous heat extraction at a rate of 2.5 MW, the temperature at the borehole wall drops to 112°C. Estimate the effective thermal diffusivity of the rock using a semi-infinite solid model, and discuss whether the 48-hour timeframe is sufficient to achieve steady-state heat transfer.
