# HFDPO-01685

A deep-space satellite network operating near Jupiter must synchronize clocks across three probes orbiting at different altitudes—100,000 km, 150,000 km, and 200,000 km—above the planet’s cloud tops. Due to Jupiter’s strong gravitational field, general relativity predicts clock rates will differ by up to 1.2 seconds per year. The mission team observes a growing time drift in telemetry data but can’t determine whether it’s due to gravitational time dilation or residual clock instability. How should they validate that the observed drift is primarily relativistic, not instrumental?
