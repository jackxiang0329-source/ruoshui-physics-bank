# HFDPO-06150

At a quantum materials lab, researchers observe that electrons passing through a nanoscale gold grating produce an interference pattern on a detector, but only when no path information is recorded. When a detector is placed to determine which slit each electron went through, the pattern fades. If the grating has slits spaced 50 nm apart and the electrons have a de Broglie wavelength of 12.3 nm, what is the angle of the first-order interference maximum? Why does measuring the path destroy the interference?
