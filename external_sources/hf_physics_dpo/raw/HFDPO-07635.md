# HFDPO-07635

A remote Arctic research station uses a triangular lattice of carbon-fiber struts to support a solar array tilted at 40° to maximize winter sun capture. The central joint, subjected to 12,000 N of downward load from the array and wind forces, is secured by three struts made of different alloys—each with unique yield strengths and cross-sectional areas. One strut has a 60° angle with horizontal, another 15°, and the third forms the remaining angle in the triangle. The design must ensure no strut exceeds 80% of its yield strength. What is the minimum required cross-sectional area of the weakest strut to prevent failure, given that the material properties vary and the structure is symmetric?
