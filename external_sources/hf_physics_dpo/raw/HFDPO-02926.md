# HFDPO-02926

A quantum imaging lab is developing a new entanglement-based microscope to visualize live neural activity in brain tissue with sub-diffraction resolution. The system uses correlated photon pairs generated via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC), with one photon probing the sample and the other detected in a background-free coincidence channel. However, the signal-to-noise ratio degrades rapidly as the sample thickness increases beyond 150 μm due to scattering and phase distortion. Design a phase-compensating metasurface array that can correct for the spatially varying wavefront aberrations induced by the tissue, while preserving quantum entanglement. Assume the pump laser is 405 nm (pulse duration 100 fs), and the signal-idler pair has a spectral bandwidth of 2 nm. Specify the required metasurface element placement and phase profile across a 1 mm × 1 mm aperture to maintain a fidelity >0.95 in the generated entangled state after propagation through the tissue.
