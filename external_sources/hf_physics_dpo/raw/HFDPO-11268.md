# HFDPO-11268

A semiconductor engineer is designing a new type of solar cell using doped silicon. The material must absorb light efficiently in the visible spectrum (400–700 nm), but the current bandgap of pure silicon (1.12 eV) leads to poor response in the blue end of the spectrum. To shift the bandgap down to 1.5 eV (optimizing for high efficiency), she considers doping with phosphorus (which adds electrons) or boron (which creates holes). She needs to determine which dopant, if any, will shift the band structure to achieve the target bandgap—while maintaining good charge carrier mobility.
