# HFDPO-10715

A marine biologist studying coral reef health notices that underwater sound patterns near a reef have shifted dramatically after a nearby offshore wind farm was commissioned. Using hydrophones, she detects a persistent 120 Hz tone superimposed on natural reef noise, with a measured intensity of 3.2 × 10⁻⁶ W/m² at 50 m from the turbine. The water temperature is 22°C, and the salinity is 35 ppt. She suspects this tonal noise may disrupt reef communication. Determine the amplitude of the pressure variation caused by this sound wave and evaluate whether the wave could cause measurable cavitation in the water, given that cavitation occurs when local pressure drops below vapor pressure (≈ 2.3 kPa at 22°C).
