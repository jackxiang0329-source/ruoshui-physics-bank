# HFDPO-02790

In a next-generation neural interface chip, graphene-based microelectrodes are embedded in a biocompatible polymer substrate to record brain activity. Each electrode forms a capacitor with the surrounding extracellular fluid, but due to the non-uniform ion distribution near the electrode surface, the effective dielectric constant varies spatially. A 10 μm diameter electrode exhibits a capacitance of 450 fF at 1 kHz, yet during real-time recording, the signal-to-noise ratio degrades sharply when the temperature rises from 37°C to 42°C. Given the thermal expansion coefficient of the polymer is 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ /°C and the relative permittivity of the fluid drops by 2.5% per °C, what is the net change in capacitance, and how might spatial non-uniformity amplify this effect?
