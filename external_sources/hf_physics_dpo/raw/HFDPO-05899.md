# HFDPO-05899

In a solar-powered microgrid on a remote island, a hybrid system uses three photovoltaic arrays (A, B, C) with differing spectral responses and internal resistances, connected through a shared DC bus with a 200 mH inductor and a 50 mF capacitor for filtering. Array A outputs 120 V with 0.8 Ω internal resistance; B produces 115 V at 1.1 Ω; C delivers 125 V at 0.6 Ω. At peak sun, the system supplies 45 A to a battery bank (30 V nominal), but measurements show the bus voltage is 33.2 V, not the expected 30 V. Use Kirchhoff’s laws to determine the actual current through each array and the power lost in the bus wiring due to resistance—given that the inductor and capacitor effectively stabilize voltage but do not dissipate power.
