# HFDPO-13862

A privately funded microsatellite constellation is designed to maintain continuous optical coverage over Earth’s polar regions using a Sun-synchronous orbit. After launch, mission control detects a subtle but persistent drift in the satellite’s orbital plane relative to the prime meridian—gaining 0.3° per day. The team suspects a combination of gravitational perturbations from Earth’s oblateness (J₂) and third-body effects from the Moon, but can’t isolate the dominant cause. Estimate the rate of orbital plane precession due to J₂ alone, verify whether it matches the observed drift, and determine if the Moon’s influence is significant enough to require real-time correction.
