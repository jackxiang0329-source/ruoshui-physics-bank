# HFDPO-06949

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses a central receiver tower with a heliostat field to concentrate sunlight onto a molten salt receiver. During a clear summer day, the receiver absorbs 120 MW of solar power at a temperature of 560°C. The salt flows through the receiver at 1.2 kg/s, entering at 300°C and exiting at 560°C. The system operates under steady state with negligible heat loss. Using the measured specific heat capacity of the salt (1.5 kJ/kg·K), determine the theoretical minimum flow rate required to maintain the exit temperature, and explain why the actual flow rate must be higher—considering conduction losses through the receiver walls and temperature non-uniformity.
