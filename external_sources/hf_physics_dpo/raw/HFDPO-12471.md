# HFDPO-12471

In a cryogenic quantum computing chip cooled to 15 mK, researchers observe an unexpected 30% increase in residual heat capacity at low temperatures, deviating from the expected T³ dependence of phonons in amorphous silicon. The chip’s dielectric layer contains nanoscale inhomogeneities with a distribution of local tunneling states. Estimate the effective density of these two-level systems (TLS) per unit volume that would account for the excess heat capacity, assuming they follow a uniform distribution of energy splittings between 0.1 μeV and 10 μeV, and calculate the temperature at which the TLS contribution becomes dominant over phonons.
