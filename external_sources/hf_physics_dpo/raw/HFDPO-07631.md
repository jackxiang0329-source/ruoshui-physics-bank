# HFDPO-07631

In a remote Arctic research station, a critical sensor array relies on a 500-meter copper cable to transmit data back to a central hub. The cable is buried under permafrost, where temperature averages –25°C, but during a sudden heatwave, the ground warms to 5°C. The system requires a minimum current of 1.2 A to operate reliably. Given that the wire’s resistivity increases by 0.39% per °C above 20°C due to temperature dependence, and the power supply voltage is fixed at 12 V, will the current drop below the required threshold during the heatwave? Assume the wire’s cross-sectional area is 2.5 mm² and its resistance at 20°C is 0.85 Ω.
