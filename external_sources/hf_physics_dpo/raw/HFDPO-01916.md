# HFDPO-01916

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a series of high-temperature superconducting (HTS) tape coils cooled to 65 K to generate a 12 T magnetic field. During a sudden quench event, one segment of the coil loses superconductivity, causing a localized heating pulse that raises the temperature to 75 K—just above the critical temperature of 70 K. Engineers detect a growing voltage spike across the coil’s terminals, but the cryogenic system remains operational. How does the sudden loss of superconductivity in one segment alter the magnetic flux dynamics, and why might the measured voltage rise not fully reflect the energy dissipated in the quench?
