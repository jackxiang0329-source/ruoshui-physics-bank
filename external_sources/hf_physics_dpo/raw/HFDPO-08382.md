# HFDPO-08382

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from whole blood using inertial focusing in a serpentine channel with a 50 μm width and 200 μm height. The blood flows at 1.2 mL/min, and the CTCs (6 μm diameter, density 1050 kg/m³) are suspended in plasma (μ = 1.2 cP, ρ = 1030 kg/m³). As the particles travel through the curved sections, they migrate radially due to a balance between lift forces and viscous drag. The device must ensure that CTCs consistently focus near the inner wall of each bend, where they can be captured. What is the minimum channel curvature radius needed to achieve stable focusing at this flow rate, considering the dominant viscous forces and the lift force scaling with the square of velocity and inverse with viscosity?
