# HFDPO-09234

During a high-altitude wind energy experiment, a portable turbine array is deployed on a remote mountain ridge where average wind speeds reach 18 m/s at 3000 m elevation. Engineers notice that two adjacent turbines, each with a 5 m diameter rotor, operate inefficiently when positioned less than 12 m apart—despite identical wind conditions. One turbine spins significantly slower than the other. Use Bernoulli’s principle to explain the observed performance imbalance and calculate the expected pressure difference between the windward and leeward sides of a turbine blade during normal operation, assuming air density is 0.90 kg/m³ and assuming ideal flow over the airfoil.
