# HFDPO-06660

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole with a 1.2 km deep, 15 cm diameter bore to circulate a conductive fluid (saline water, σ = 2 S/m) that naturally rises due to convection. As it ascends, it passes through a 300-turn solenoid coil wrapped around the borehole, generating electricity via induced EMF. The magnetic field is not externally applied; instead, the fluid’s motion through Earth’s magnetic field (B ≈ 50 μT, inclined 65° below horizontal) induces voltage. The fluid flows upward at 0.8 m/s, aligned with the borehole axis. Calculate the peak induced EMF across the coil, and explain why the actual measured EMF is significantly lower.
