# HFDPO-00952

A lunar rover traversing the edge of a 200-meter-diameter crater on the Moon encounters a 5° slope leading toward a 10-meter-deep fissure. The rover’s mass is 450 kg, with its center of mass 0.8 m above the ground and 0.3 m behind the rear axle. As it climbs the slope at 1.2 m/s, the regolith under the rear wheels suddenly loses friction due to subsurface ice. If the rover’s wheels lock and it begins to slide backward down the slope, at what critical deceleration (in m/s²) does the front wheels lose contact with the ground, risking a flip? Assume the Moon’s gravity is 1.62 m/s² and the slope is uniform with no rolling resistance.
