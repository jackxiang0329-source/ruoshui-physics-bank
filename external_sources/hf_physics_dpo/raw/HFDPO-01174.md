# HFDPO-01174

In a cutting-edge neuromorphic computing chip, researchers are designing a synaptic capacitor array using graphene-based dielectrics, where each synaptic unit must store 50 fJ of energy with minimal leakage. The capacitor is formed between two 100 nm thick graphene layers separated by a 2 nm thick h-BN (hexagonal boron nitride) insulator with a relative permittivity of 4.8. However, due to atomic-scale defects, the effective area of the capacitor varies spatially—modeled as a radially symmetric Gaussian distribution centered at the origin with a standard deviation of 50 nm. What is the minimum average capacitance (in fF) required for each synaptic unit to meet the energy storage goal, and how does the non-uniform area affect the total stored energy compared to a uniform area?
