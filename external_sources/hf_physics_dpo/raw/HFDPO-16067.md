# HFDPO-16067

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses supercritical water (T = 374°C, P = 22.1 MPa) to drive turbines, but after months of operation, efficiency drops sharply due to sudden mineral scaling in the heat exchangers. The scaling occurs when pressure drops slightly during flow, pushing the water into a metastable region near the critical point. Given that the solubility of calcite (CaCO₃) in water peaks near the critical point but collapses rapidly upon crossing the liquid–vapor coexistence curve, determine the minimum pressure drop required to trigger calcite precipitation from a saturated solution at 380°C, assuming the process is isenthalpic and the fluid path stays within the two-phase region.
