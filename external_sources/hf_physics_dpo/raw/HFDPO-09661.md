# HFDPO-09661

During a geomagnetic storm, a high-latitude power grid experiences rapidly fluctuating magnetic fields (dB/dt ≈ 0.5 mT/s), inducing currents in 300-km-long transmission lines running east-west. The Earth’s magnetic field at that location is 50 μT inclined at 60° to the horizontal. Engineers observe unexplained voltage spikes in the grid’s monitoring system. Determine whether these spikes are likely due to induction from the changing geomagnetic field alone, and evaluate if the observed voltages could exceed safe operational limits (10 kV) under realistic assumptions.
