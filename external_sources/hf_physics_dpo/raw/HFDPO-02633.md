# HFDPO-02633

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a submerged, irregularly shaped hydrothermal vent chimney composed of porous mineral deposits. The chimney has a height of 3.2 m and a base area that tapers from 1.8 m² at the bottom to 0.6 m² at the top, with a uniform porosity of 0.35. Seawater (density 1025 kg/m³) rises through the porous structure due to buoyancy-driven flow. Engineers need to estimate the net upward hydrostatic force on the chimney caused by the buoyancy of displaced seawater if the chimney is filled to 80% of its volume with ambient seawater. The chimney sits on a seabed at 2100 m depth, where pressure is 2.15 × 10⁷ Pa.
