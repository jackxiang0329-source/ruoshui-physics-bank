# HFDPO-14315

In a quantum computing prototype, a 1.2-meter-long niobium-tin (Nb₃Sn) superconducting loop maintains a persistent current of 3.5 A at 2.1 K. During a cooling ramp, a localized quench occurs, raising the temperature in a 5 mm segment to 4.5 K—just above the critical temperature of 4.2 K. If the loop’s inductance is 8.7 μH and the quench creates a transient voltage pulse lasting 7.2 ns, calculate the maximum potential energy stored in the magnetic field before the quench, and evaluate whether the energy released during the quench could trigger a secondary quench in adjacent loops spaced 3 mm apart, assuming coupling via mutual inductance of 1.4 nH.
