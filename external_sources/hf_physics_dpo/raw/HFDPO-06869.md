# HFDPO-06869

A quantum dot solar cell prototype exhibits unexpected efficiency drops under broad-spectrum sunlight. The device uses a 200-nm-thick silicon nitride anti-reflective coating with refractive index 2.0, designed for peak transmission at 550 nm. However, measurements show a 15% loss in power output at 600 nm. Given that the incident light is unpolarized and the solar cell operates at 300 K, determine the phase difference between the reflected waves at the air-coating and coating-silicon interfaces, and explain how constructive interference in transmission is compromised despite correct coating thickness.
