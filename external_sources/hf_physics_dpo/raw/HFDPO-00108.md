# HFDPO-00108

In a cryo-electron microscopy lab, researchers image a virus coat composed of 180 identical protein subunits arranged in a quasi-spherical lattice. Each subunit can adopt one of three conformations—A, B, or C—due to thermal fluctuations, but only conformation A is biologically active. At 200 K, the energy differences between conformations are ΔE_AB = 0.4 eV and ΔE_AC = 0.8 eV. What is the probability that a randomly selected subunit is in conformation A, and how does collective coupling (modeled via a mean-field Ising-like interaction with J = 0.02 eV) alter this probability compared to the independent case?
