# HFDPO-10657

A space probe's thermal control system relies on a thin, self-heating resistor array made of doped silicon to maintain instrument warmth during a deep-space mission. The array must dissipate exactly 15 W of power at 120 K to prevent cryogenic failure. However, the resistance of doped silicon decreases nonlinearly with temperature due to carrier mobility changes. At 120 K, the resistivity is 0.05 Ω·m, but it drops to 0.03 Ω·m at 200 K. If the array is designed for 15 W dissipation at 120 K but ends up operating at 180 K due to a malfunctioning thermostat, what is the actual power dissipated? Does this create a thermal runaway risk?
