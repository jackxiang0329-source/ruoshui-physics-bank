# HFDPO-14074

In a remote Arctic research station, engineers face a critical energy shortfall during polar night. The station relies on a hybrid heat engine system: a Stirling engine powered by a geothermal heat source (85°C) with ambient air at -10°C as the cold reservoir. However, ice buildup on the external heat exchanger reduces heat transfer efficiency by 32% over time. To maintain 15 kW of continuous power output, the system's actual efficiency drops to 45% of its ideal Carnot efficiency. What minimum heat input rate (in kW) must the geothermal source provide, and how does this compare to the theoretical minimum required under ideal conditions?
