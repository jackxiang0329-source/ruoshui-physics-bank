# HFDPO-14962

A lunar research outpost uses a buried geothermal heat exchanger to maintain habitable temperatures in the regolith, but during a 14-day polar night, the system's thermal efficiency drops unexpectedly. The exchanger consists of a 200 m long copper pipe (k = 400 W/m·K) buried 3 m deep in regolith with average thermal conductivity 1.5 W/m·K. Initial regolith temperature is -50°C; heat is supplied at 500 W/m² via solar collectors before dusk. After 7 days, the ground temperature at the pipe depth reaches -35°C. What is the effective thermal resistance between the pipe and the surrounding regolith at that point, and how does this compare to the steady-state conductive resistance?
