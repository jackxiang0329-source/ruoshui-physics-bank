# HFDPO-00122

A high-performance electric vehicle battery pack, operating at 85°C during sustained high-power discharge, is cooled by a liquid coolant flowing through microchannels in a copper heat spreader. The pack generates 12 kW of heat uniformly across a 0.5 m² surface. The coolant enters at 30°C with a flow rate of 0.3 kg/s and a specific heat capacity of 4180 J/kg·K. The heat transfer coefficient between the copper and coolant is 800 W/m²·K. The copper spreader is 2 mm thick and has a thermal conductivity of 400 W/m·K. After 180 seconds of operation, the system reaches steady-state. Estimate the maximum temperature rise across the spreader (from coolant side to battery side), and evaluate whether the current design could prevent localized hot spots (>95°C) on the battery cells.
