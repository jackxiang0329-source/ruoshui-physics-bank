# HFDPO-06434

A remote weather station in the Arctic uses a solar-powered AC system with a 240 V, 60 Hz inverter, but during long winter nights, it switches to a battery bank through an inverter that introduces a 35° phase lag between voltage and current. The station's only AC load—a heater—has a rated power of 1.2 kW at 240 V. During a storm, the inverter’s output voltage drops to 220 V RMS, and the power factor drops to 0.75 lagging. What is the actual power delivered to the heater, and why does the heater produce less heat than expected despite the voltage being close to nominal?
