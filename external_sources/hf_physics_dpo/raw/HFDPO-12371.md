# HFDPO-12371

A research team at a fusion reactor facility is testing a new tokamak design that uses high-temperature superconducting (HTS) coils to generate a 12 T magnetic field. The coils are made of REBCO tape wound into a solenoid with a 1.5 m radius and 2 m length, carrying 450 A. During a cooldown to 45 K, one segment fails to quench properly, causing a local current density spike. The team discovers that the critical current density of the REBCO tape drops sharply below 75 K, but the coil's operating temperature is only 45 K. Is the coil capable of sustaining the required current without thermal runaway, given that the tape’s Jc = 1.4 × 10⁶ A/cm² at 45 K and the cross-sectional area of the tape is 0.12 cm²?
