# HFDPO-07001

A research team at a renewable energy startup is developing a high-efficiency energy harvester that captures ambient vibrational energy from industrial machinery using a MEMS-scale capacitive transducer. The device uses two parallel plate electrodes with a variable gap (initially 10 μm) driven by mechanical vibrations of frequency 120 Hz. During testing, they observe that the average harvested power drops to 65% of the theoretical maximum when the gap modulation amplitude increases beyond 2 μm due to nonlinear fringing field effects and dielectric breakdown risks in the thin SiO₂ layer (κ = 3.9, breakdown field 10⁷ V/m). What is the maximum voltage that can be safely applied without inducing breakdown, and how does this constrain the design of the capacitor’s geometric and operational parameters?
