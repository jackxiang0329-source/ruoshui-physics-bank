# HFDPO-13345

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a submerged hydrothermal vent system on the ocean floor. The vent emits superheated brine at 350°C with a density of 970 kg/m³, discharging into cold seawater at 2°C (density 1025 kg/m³) at a rate of 0.8 m³/s through a 0.4 m diameter orifice. As the plume rises, intense mixing occurs due to buoyancy-driven instability. Engineers must predict the height at which the plume reaches neutral buoyancy to avoid damaging sensitive microbial communities. Using the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy with variable density and turbulent dispersion, determine the vertical rise before neutral buoyancy is achieved, assuming a turbulent entrainment coefficient of 0.08 and constant ambient pressure.
