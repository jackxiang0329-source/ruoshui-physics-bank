# HFDPO-00372

During a polar expedition, a team uses a novel phase-change material (PCM) housed in a cylindrical thermal battery to maintain equipment at 25°C in −40°C ambient conditions. The PCM has a latent heat of fusion of 210 kJ/kg and a melting point of 25°C. The battery’s 1.2 kg PCM is initially solid at −20°C. After 8 hours, the ambient temperature rises rapidly to −25°C. If the thermal conductivity of the PCM is 0.4 W/m·K and the outer surface is exposed to a convective heat transfer coefficient of 12 W/m²·K, determine the fraction of PCM that remains solid after 12 hours. Assume uniform temperature within the PCM during phase change and negligible heat loss through the ends.
