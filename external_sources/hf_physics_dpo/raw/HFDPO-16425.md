# HFDPO-16425

A marine biologist studying humpback whale communication in the Gulf of Alaska detects a complex sequence of low-frequency pulses (15–35 Hz) recorded by a hydrophone array. The pulses arrive at different times across three sensors spaced 2 km apart, but the sound pressure level drops by 12 dB over just 5 km of travel through cold, stratified water (1.5°C, 35 ppt salinity). The biologist suspects the sound beam is refracting due to underwater sound speed gradients, but the recorded arrival times don’t match simple ray tracing models. What is the actual sound speed gradient (in m/s per km) that explains the observed time delays and attenuation, assuming the sound source is at a depth of 50 m and the hydrophones are at 100 m depth?
