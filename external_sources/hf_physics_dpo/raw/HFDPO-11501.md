# HFDPO-11501

A quantum imaging lab uses a heralded single-photon source based on spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) to detect faint biological samples under low-light conditions. The pump laser (1064 nm) generates entangled photon pairs with a spectral bandwidth of 1.2 nm (FWHM), and the signal photon is directed toward a 2 mm thick biological tissue sample with a scattering coefficient of 15 cm⁻¹ and a reduced scattering coefficient of 13 cm⁻¹. The system must maintain quantum interference in a fiber-based Mach-Zehnder interferometer to achieve sub-shot-noise sensitivity. However, phase noise from thermal drift in the interferometer arms (ΔL ≈ 500 nm over 1 hour) causes decoherence. What is the minimum coherence time required to preserve quantum interference, and how does the spectral bandwidth of the SPDC source affect the achievable visibility?
