# HFDPO-06273

During a deep-sea mining operation, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) retrieves a cylindrical core sample from a hydrothermal vent field at 2,500 meters depth. The core, sealed in a rigid titanium capsule with internal air pressure initially at 1 atm, is slowly brought to the surface. At 2,500 m, the external seawater pressure is 252 atm. As the ROV ascends, the capsule’s pressure relief valve fails to open, and the internal air volume remains constant. What happens to the capsule’s structural integrity as pressure drops, and at what depth does the net inward force on the capsule’s end cap reach 2.0 × 10⁴ N—beyond engineering safety limits? Assume the end cap has a 0.15 m radius and no deformation occurs until failure.
