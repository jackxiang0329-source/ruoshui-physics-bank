# HFDPO-00924

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system near the Mid-Atlantic Ridge emits superheated fluids at 350°C into cold ocean water at 2°C. The vent’s chimney structure forms a porous, thermally insulating mineral deposit with a thermal conductivity of 0.8 W/m·K and a thickness of 12 cm. Over time, heat transfer through the chimney reduces the internal temperature gradient, affecting fluid chemistry and microbial habitat stability. Calculate the steady-state heat flux through the chimney wall and evaluate whether the observed cooling rate of vent fluids (measured at 2.1°C/min) is consistent with the predicted heat loss via conduction, assuming a 50 m² surface area and negligible convection. How would violating the Second Law influence the long-term structural integrity of the chimney?
