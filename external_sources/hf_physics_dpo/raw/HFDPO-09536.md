# HFDPO-09536

A fusion reactor prototype uses a complex toroidal magnetic field to confine plasma at 150 million Kelvin. Engineers observe that the magnetic pressure near the inner wall is causing micro-instabilities, threatening containment. The toroid has a major radius of 6.2 m, minor radius of 1.8 m, and the poloidal field is generated by 24 identical superconducting coils, each carrying 45 kA. The plasma current is 12 MA, producing a self-generated field that opposes the external field near the inner wall. Calculate the net magnetic field magnitude and direction at the innermost point of the toroidal chamber, and evaluate whether the resulting magnetic pressure exceeds the plasma’s thermal pressure (180 atm).
