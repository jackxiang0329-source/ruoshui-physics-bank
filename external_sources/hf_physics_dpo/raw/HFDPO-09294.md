# HFDPO-09294

A quantum dot solar cell prototype exhibits unexpectedly low efficiency under broad-spectrum sunlight, despite ideal material absorptivity. Measurements show interference fringes in the reflected light when illuminated at 45°, with fringe spacing of 1.2 μm on a detector 1.5 m away. The top layer (n = 1.6) is a thin dielectric coating (150 nm thick) designed for anti-reflection, but it’s suspected to be causing destructive interference in the visible range. Determine the dominant visible wavelength (400–700 nm) most suppressed by constructive interference in the coating’s reflection, and explain why the efficiency drops in the 550–600 nm range.
