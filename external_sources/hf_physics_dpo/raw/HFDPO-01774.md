# HFDPO-01774

In a microfluidic lab-on-a-chip device designed for early cancer detection, a suspension of cancerous microvesicles (diameter ~1.2 μm, density 1040 kg/m³) flows through a serpentine channel (width 50 μm, height 20 μm) at a volumetric flow rate of 15 nL/min. The fluid is a plasma surrogate with viscosity 1.4 cP and density 1025 kg/m³. Due to the channel’s narrow geometry and high aspect ratio, the flow is laminar but non-uniform. The microvesicles begin to aggregate near sharp turns, compromising detection sensitivity. Determine the condition under which inertial effects could destabilize the flow and initiate vortex formation, and explain how this might promote particle aggregation in the bends. Assume the channel curvature radius is 80 μm at each turn.
