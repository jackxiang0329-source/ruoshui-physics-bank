# HFDPO-04535

A biomedical engineer is designing a fiber-optic probe for early detection of cancerous tissue using Raman spectroscopy. The probe uses a 785 nm laser, but the signal from malignant cells is weak due to fluorescence background from healthy tissue. To enhance specificity, the team considers placing a narrowband interference filter (reflectivity ≈ 99%) at the output to block fluorescence (400–700 nm) while transmitting the Raman-shifted signal at 830 nm. However, the filter’s angular tolerance is limited—its peak transmission shifts by 1.2 nm per degree. If the fiber is aligned at 30° to the filter normal, will the desired 830 nm signal still be transmitted efficiently?
