# HFDPO-10525

In a deep-space quantum communications experiment, a spherical superconducting cavity (radius 1.2 m) is cooled to 1.8 K to suppress electromagnetic noise. During a calibration pulse, a transient magnetic field of 3.5 mT is detected at the cavity’s center 15 ns after a current pulse is applied to its surface winding. The cavity is designed to shield external fields, but measurements indicate a non-zero ∂B/∂t inside. Determine whether this observation violates Maxwell’s equations, and if not, explain how it can be consistent with Faraday’s law when the cavity’s surface current density is temporally non-uniform.
