# HFDPO-11094

During a Mars rover mission, engineers discover that the robotic arm’s joint bearings are overheating and seizing after repeated deployments in the thin, dusty atmosphere. The arm uses a high-precision rotary actuator with a 10 mm diameter shaft lubricated with a temperature-resistant grease. Initial tests show the torque required to rotate the shaft is 1.8 N·m at 0.5 rad/s, but this increases to 2.3 N·m after 12 hours of operation in simulated Martian conditions (0.6% Earth’s atmospheric pressure, 100 μm diameter silica dust particles suspended in air). Determine the effective drag torque due to atmospheric shear and dust adhesion, assuming laminar flow and Coulomb friction-like behavior from particulate adhesion, and explain why the rise in torque is non-linear over time.
