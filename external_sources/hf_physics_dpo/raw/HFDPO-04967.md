# HFDPO-04967

A fusion reactor’s tokamak design uses a toroidal magnetic field to confine plasma at 150 million Kelvin. During a sudden disruption, the plasma current collapses in 10 ms, inducing a massive eddy current in the surrounding copper shell (resistivity 1.7×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, thickness 5 cm). The shell is modeled as a conducting toroid with inner radius 2 m and outer radius 2.1 m. Calculate the peak electric field magnitude induced in the shell and explain how this electric field could compromise the structural integrity via joule heating and Lorentz forces.
