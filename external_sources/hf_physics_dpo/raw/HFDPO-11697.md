# HFDPO-11697

During a high-altitude expedition, a climber discovers that their emergency thermal blanket—designed to reflect 90% of body heat—has become damp from condensation due to a sudden temperature drop. The blanket now traps a thin layer of water (0.1 mm thick, 40 cm × 60 cm) between its layers. If the climber’s core temperature drops below 35°C, hypothermia risks increase rapidly. Given that the ambient temperature is –10°C and the water layer is initially at 0°C, how long will it take for the water to freeze completely, and will it be enough to protect the climber’s body heat during a 30-minute descent? Assume the blanket’s insulation reduces heat transfer by 85%, and the latent heat of fusion for water is 334 kJ/kg.
