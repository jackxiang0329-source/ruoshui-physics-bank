# HFDPO-11115

A marine biologist studying whale communication in the North Atlantic detects a series of low-frequency pulses (15–30 Hz) during a deep-sea sonar survey, but the signals are distorted and inconsistently received. The hydrophones are 2 km apart, deployed at different depths (800 m and 1,200 m) in a region with a known sound speed gradient of 1450 m/s at the surface and 1520 m/s at 1,500 m depth. The researcher suspects internal wave interference from tidal mixing above the thermocline. Evaluate whether the observed signal phase shifts and amplitude variations can be explained by multipath propagation due to sound channeling and waveguide effects, given the measured temperature and salinity profiles. Use ray acoustics and normal mode theory to compare expected arrival times and coherence.
