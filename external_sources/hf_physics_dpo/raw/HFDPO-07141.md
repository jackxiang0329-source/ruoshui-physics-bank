# HFDPO-07141

A remote environmental monitoring station in the Arctic uses a solar panel array and a battery bank to power sensors that transmit data every 30 minutes. The solar panels generate 18 V under peak sunlight, but the station's critical electronics require a stable 12 V supply. The system uses a series-connected buck converter with a switching frequency of 50 kHz, but due to temperature-induced resistance changes in the wiring (–40°C to +25°C), the effective resistance between the solar array and the converter varies from 0.8 Ω to 2.5 Ω. The battery bank (12 V, 100 Ah) is connected in parallel via a diode to prevent reverse discharge. During a prolonged overcast period, the solar input drops to 11 V, and the system must continue operating on battery power alone. What is the minimum average current that the battery must supply to keep the load voltage above 11.5 V, and how does the varying wiring resistance affect this under different thermal conditions?
