# HFDPO-15647

A Mars rover's front wheel, coated with a novel electrostatic dust-repelling layer, begins to slip during ascent on a 25° incline covered in fine, cohesive regolith. The wheel’s torque is fixed at 18.5 N·m, radius 0.35 m, and mass 8.2 kg. The coefficient of static friction between wheel and regolith is 0.62, but the regolith’s effective cohesion creates a drag force proportional to velocity: F_d = 0.45v² (N) with v in m/s. The rover’s motor delivers constant power. Determine the maximum speed the front wheel can achieve without slipping, and explain why increasing motor power beyond a threshold may *reduce* the effective traction despite greater torque.
