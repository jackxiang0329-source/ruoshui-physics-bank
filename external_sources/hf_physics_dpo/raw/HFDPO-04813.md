# HFDPO-04813

A spacecraft's power system uses a network of solar arrays and rechargeable lithium-ion batteries to sustain operations during long-duration missions. During a critical phase, the main power bus must maintain a stable 30 V output despite fluctuating solar input and variable load demands. Engineers discover that a fault in one of the parallel solar string branches causes a 15% drop in current output, and the current-limiting circuitry in that branch fails. The remaining six identical solar strings each deliver 8 A under optimal conditions, but one now delivers only 6.8 A due to the fault. The battery bank (24 V nominal) is connected in series with a non-ideal diode (0.7 V forward drop) and is meant to regulate the bus voltage. The total load draws 8 A at 30 V when stable. Determine whether the power bus can remain at 30 V, and explain how the failure changes the voltage regulation mechanism.
