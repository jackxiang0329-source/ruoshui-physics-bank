# HFDPO-07235

In a next-generation neural interface, a micrometer-scale electrode array is implanted in brain tissue, where it must maintain stable capacitive coupling with neurons despite fluctuating extracellular ion concentrations (4–12 mM K⁺). The electrode’s effective capacitance drops by 35% when K⁺ falls from 5 mM to 3 mM due to local ion depletion during sustained neural firing. Given that the membrane capacitance is 1 μF/cm², the electrode diameter is 50 μm, and the dielectric constant of the biocompatible coating is 6, determine the thickness of the dielectric layer needed at 5 mM K⁺ to ensure 90% of the theoretical capacitance persists at 3 mM K⁺.
