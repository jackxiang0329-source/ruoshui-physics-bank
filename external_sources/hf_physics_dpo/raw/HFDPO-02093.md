# HFDPO-02093

In a quantum computing testbed, a team observes that electrons tunneling through a 1.2 nm-thick silicon nitride barrier exhibit interference patterns on a detector array, but only when the barrier is maintained at 4.5 K. At higher temperatures, the fringes vanish. The team measures a peak-to-peak fringe spacing of 1.8 μm at 4.5 K, yet the electrons are prepared in a narrow momentum state with Δp ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻²⁵ kg·m/s. How can this apparent contradiction—wave-like interference from particles with limited momentum uncertainty—be reconciled with the principles of wave-particle duality, and what does the disappearance of interference at higher temperatures reveal about the role of decoherence?
