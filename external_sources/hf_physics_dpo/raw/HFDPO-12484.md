# HFDPO-12484

A space probe’s heat shield during atmospheric entry experiences peak temperatures of 12,000 K at the stagnation point, but the underlying electronics must remain below 350 K. The shield is made of a ceramic composite with variable thermal conductivity (k = k₀(1 + αT), where k₀ = 8 W/m·K, α = 5×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹) and is 15 cm thick. Heat is conducted radially inward, and the base is cooled by a regenerative loop using a liquid metal coolant. If the coolant maintains the base at 350 K and the shield’s outer surface is adiabatic except at the stagnation point, determine the steady-state temperature distribution in the shield and the maximum allowable heat flux through the base to prevent thermal runaway.
