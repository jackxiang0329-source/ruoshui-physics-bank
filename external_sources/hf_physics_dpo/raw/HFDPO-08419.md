# HFDPO-08419

A fusion reactor prototype uses a ring of high-temperature superconducting tapes (YBCO) to generate a 12 T magnetic field to confine plasma. The tapes are cooled to 77 K via liquid nitrogen and carry 150 A with zero resistance. However, during a rapid power surge, the magnetic flux in the ring begins to decay unpredictably over 0.3 seconds. Engineers measure a voltage spike of 2.4 mV across a small section of the coil. Is the superconducting state being disrupted, and if so, what might explain the flux decay and voltage? Evaluate whether the system is operating within the critical current density limits or if vortex penetration is occurring.
