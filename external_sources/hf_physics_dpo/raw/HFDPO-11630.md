# HFDPO-11630

A quantum computing chip uses a 2D array of superconducting qubits coupled via microwave photons, where the coupling strength depends on the phase difference between photons traveling along two paths of slightly different lengths. The array includes a periodic grating structure with 500 nm spacing to route photons through interference. When a photon enters, it creates a diffraction pattern on a detector array 2 cm away. A critical error occurs: 12% of the photons are being lost due to destructive interference at a specific detection point. What must the phase shift between the two paths be to cause this loss, and how could the grating period be adjusted to eliminate the effect while maintaining quantum coherence?
