# HFDPO-11315

A biomedical engineer is designing a lens-free holographic microscope to detect early-stage malaria parasites in blood samples. The system uses coherent light at 632.8 nm (He-Ne laser) to illuminate a thin blood film on a glass slide. Due to the high refractive index contrast between infected red blood cells (n ≈ 1.38) and surrounding plasma (n ≈ 1.33), phase shifts of up to 1.8π develop across the parasite’s region. The challenge is to determine the minimum resolvable spatial frequency of the phase pattern in the interference fringe pattern when recording the hologram on a sensor with 2.4 μm pixel pitch. How does the presence of a 300 nm thick air gap between the slide and sensor affect the reconstructed image fidelity?
