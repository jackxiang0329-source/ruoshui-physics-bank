# HFDPO-09833

A new quantum imaging sensor for medical diagnostics uses a coherent laser beam (λ = 633 nm) passed through a microstructured diffraction grating to generate interference patterns encoding tissue density. During testing, a 2.0 mm-wide sample of biological tissue produces a diffraction pattern where the 3rd bright fringe is displaced by 1.4 cm from the central maximum on a screen 1.2 m away. However, due to scattering within the tissue, the observed intensity distribution deviates from the ideal sinc² pattern. Evaluate whether the observed fringe shift implies structural inhomogeneity (i.e., non-uniform refractive index) or is consistent with a homogeneous medium under the assumption of single-slit diffraction.
