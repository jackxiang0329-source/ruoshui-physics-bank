# HFDPO-01072

In a microfluidic device designed for rapid detection of circulating tumor cells, a suspension of 10-μm diameter cells in plasma (μ = 1.2 mPa·s, ρ = 1040 kg/m³) flows through a 50-μm-wide channel at 1.5 mm/s. Due to viscous forces, cells migrate laterally toward the channel centerline, but sudden narrowing to 25 μm causes flow reversal in the periphery. Determine the critical Reynolds number at which inertial effects begin to destabilize the laminar flow, and explain why cell concentration gradients form asymmetrically near the constriction—despite symmetric geometry.
