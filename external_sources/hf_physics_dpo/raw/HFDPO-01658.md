# HFDPO-01658

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system emits superheated fluid at 400°C into the surrounding ocean at 2°C. The vent’s chimney structure is a porous ceramic tube with a thermal conductivity of 1.2 W/m·K and a wall thickness of 8 cm. Engineers observe that the vent is slowly clogging, reducing heat transfer efficiency. If the internal fluid flow rate drops from 1.5 kg/s to 1.1 kg/s over 24 hours due to mineral precipitation, and the vent’s outer surface remains in near-adiabatic contact with the seabed, analyze how the second law of thermodynamics constrains the maximum sustainable heat transfer rate and whether the system can maintain thermodynamic equilibrium under these conditions.
