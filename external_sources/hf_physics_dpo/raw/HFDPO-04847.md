# HFDPO-04847

A quantum computing lab in Zurich uses a 50 mH niobium-based SQUID (Superconducting Quantum Interference Device) to detect magnetic flux changes from a single electron spin. The SQUID operates at 4.2 K, just above liquid helium’s boiling point, but the power supply to its cooling system fails unexpectedly. The cryostat’s thermal insulation degrades at a rate of 0.15 K/min, and the system must remain below the critical temperature of 9.2 K to avoid losing superconductivity. The SQUID’s critical current is 1.8 mA.
