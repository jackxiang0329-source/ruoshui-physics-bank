# HFDPO-12570

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a collapsed hydrothermal vent chimney composed of porous mineral deposits. To assess structural stability, engineers must determine whether upward buoyancy forces from trapped seawater within the porous matrix can initiate spontaneous re-liquefaction of the sediment–fluid system. The chimney’s porous core has a porosity of 0.35, a mean pore diameter of 15 μm, and a height of 12 m. Seawater density is 1025 kg/m³, and the sediment matrix has a density of 2700 kg/m³. What is the critical depth at which buoyant pore pressure exceeds the effective stress, triggering mechanical failure of the column?
