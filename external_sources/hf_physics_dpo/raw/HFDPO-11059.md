# HFDPO-11059

During a deep-sea exploration, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a submerged hydrothermal vent emitting superheated fluid (350°C) at 0.8 m/s into cold seawater (4°C). The jet forms a turbulent plume that rises rapidly, but the ROV’s sensors detect unexpected lateral oscillations in the plume’s trajectory, with periodic deviations of up to 1.2 m over a 15-second cycle. Given the jet’s diameter is 15 cm, seawater density is 1025 kg/m³, and dynamic viscosity is 1.5 × 10⁻³ Pa·s, explain the origin of the oscillations and determine whether Kelvin-Helmholtz instability or buoyancy-driven vortex shedding is the dominant mechanism.
