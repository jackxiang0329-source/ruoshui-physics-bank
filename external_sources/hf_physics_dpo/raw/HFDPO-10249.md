# HFDPO-10249

In a microfluidic lab-on-a-chip device designed for early cancer detection, a suspension of exosomes (diameter ~100 nm, density 1050 kg/m³) is routed through a 200 μm-wide serpentine channel (20 turns, 5 mm radius) fabricated in PDMS. The fluid (saline solution, μ = 1.0 mPa·s, ρ = 1000 kg/m³) flows at a constant volumetric rate of 0.5 μL/min, but unexpectedly, exosomes accumulate at the outer walls of the bends due to centrifugal effects. Determine whether the observed particle segregation is primarily caused by inertial forces or viscous drag-induced migration over the channel’s curved geometry, and evaluate if the device’s design is robust against such particle focusing errors under varying flow conditions.
