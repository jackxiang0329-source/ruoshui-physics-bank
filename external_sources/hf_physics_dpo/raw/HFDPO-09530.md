# HFDPO-09530

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system emits superheated fluid at 350°C into near-freezing seawater at 2°C. Engineers propose a closed-cycle OTEC (Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion) device using the vent's thermal gradient to generate electricity. The system operates with a maximum theoretical efficiency limited by Carnot, but real-world constraints—heat exchanger fouling, pump work, and finite-time heat transfer—reduce practical efficiency. Design a thermodynamic cycle that maximizes sustainable power output while accounting for irreversibilities in the heat transfer process. What is the optimal working fluid and why?
