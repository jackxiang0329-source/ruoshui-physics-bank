# HFDPO-03382

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystem thrives near a fissure where superheated seawater (350°C, resistivity 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ Ω·m) emerges through fractured basalt. Engineers are designing a fiber-optic cable with a central copper core (resistivity 1.68 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m) to transmit data from sensors to a surface vessel, but must account for self-heating due to current. What is the maximum allowable current if the core temperature must not exceed 80°C in a 20°C ambient sea environment, considering that Joule heating and thermal conduction through the surrounding insulation (thermal conductivity 0.15 W/m·K, thickness 2 mm) must be balanced?
