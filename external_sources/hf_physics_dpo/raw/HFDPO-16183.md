# HFDPO-16183

A fusion reactor's first wall is composed of a tungsten-clad carbon-fiber composite, exposed to a 20 MW/m² pulsed heat flux during plasma disruptions. The wall must withstand 1000 such pulses over its operational lifetime without exceeding a peak temperature of 1500°C. Given a 5 cm thick composite with thermal diffusivity 1.2×10⁻⁵ m²/s and surface emissivity 0.3, determine the required thermal barrier thickness in the composite layer to prevent melt-through, considering both transient conduction and radiative cooling during the 100 ms pulse.
