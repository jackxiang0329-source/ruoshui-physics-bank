# HFDPO-01311

In a microfluidic device designed for early cancer detection, a suspension of exosomes (diameter ~100 nm, density 1050 kg/m³) flows through a 50 μm-wide channel at 0.5 mL/min. The fluid is 90% water with 10% glycerol (viscosity 1.4 Pa·s), and the channel has surface functionalization that induces a slip length of 20 nm. A sudden pressure drop triggers a transient flow where exosomes must be separated by size via differential Stokes drag. Calculate the relative velocity difference between 80 nm and 120 nm exosomes over a 2 mm separation zone, and justify whether slip effects significantly alter the separation efficiency compared to no-slip assumptions.
