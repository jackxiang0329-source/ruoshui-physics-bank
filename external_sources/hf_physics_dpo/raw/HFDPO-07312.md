# HFDPO-07312

A tidal energy generator uses a massive conducting rotor with a radius of 12 meters, rotating at 0.5 Hz in the Earth’s magnetic field (~30 μT, vertical component). The rotor is part of a closed loop connected to a variable load. As tides rise and fall, the rotor’s axial tilt changes from 15° to 75° relative to the magnetic field over a 6-hour period. Estimate the average power delivered to the load if the rotor’s resistance is 0.8 Ω, and explain why the peak power occurs earlier than the peak tidal flow.
