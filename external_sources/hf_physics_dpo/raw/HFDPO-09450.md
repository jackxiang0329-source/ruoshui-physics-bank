# HFDPO-09450

A 1.2 kg meteoroid enters Earth’s upper atmosphere at 32 km/s, ablating rapidly as it decelerates. Observations show it loses 0.08 kg of mass per kilometer traveled, and its speed drops to 18 km/s after 15 km of atmospheric penetration. Assuming no external forces and perfect conservation of momentum and energy (accounting for internal energy conversion), calculate the total kinetic energy lost, the fraction converted to thermal radiation and atmospheric heating, and explain why momentum conservation requires the atmosphere to experience a net impulse even though it’s not a solid body.
