# HFDPO-14400

In a microfluidic device designed for lab-on-a-chip diagnostics, a 5 mm × 5 mm chamber contains a 15 µL droplet of blood plasma (μ = 1.2 mPa·s, ρ = 1020 kg/m³) suspended between two parallel glass plates separated by 20 µm. A controlled pressure gradient of 180 Pa/m is applied across the chamber to drive flow through a 100 µm-wide channel leading to a detection zone. However, the droplet splits asymmetrically during flow, and the resulting velocity profile deviates from Poiseuille predictions. Determine the effective viscosity required to explain this observed asymmetry, considering wall slip and non-uniform interface tension at the liquid–solid boundary.
