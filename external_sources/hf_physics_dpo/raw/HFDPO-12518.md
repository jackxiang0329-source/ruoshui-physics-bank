# HFDPO-12518

After 10 seconds of drilling into icy regolith, the drill stalls—its angular velocity drops to zero as the motor disengages. Using data from the rover’s telemetry, engineers observe that the angular deceleration is not constant—instead, it increases with the square of the instantaneous angular speed. What is the average frictional torque experienced during the deceleration phase, and what physical mechanism might explain the nonlinear behavior?
