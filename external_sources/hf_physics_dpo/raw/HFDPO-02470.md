# HFDPO-02470

A solar-powered drone designed for high-altitude atmospheric research must glide from 20,000 m to 15,000 m above the surface, where it transitions to powered flight to recharge its batteries. The drone’s mass is 12 kg, and it descends with minimal propulsion, relying on aerodynamic lift and drag. At 20,000 m, it has a speed of 60 m/s. The drag force varies with altitude due to changing air density: F_d = 0.002 rho(z) v^2 A , where rho(z) = rho_0 e^{-z/H} , H = 7,000 m, rho_0 = 1.2 kg/m³, and A = 0.8 m². The lift adjusts to maintain a constant glide angle of 5°. Determine the drone’s speed at 15,000 m, using energy methods.
