# HFDPO-01361

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent emits superheated fluid at 350°C into the surrounding ocean at 2°C. The vent's chimney is a cylindrical structure with a 10 cm inner radius and 50 cm height, made of porous basalt with thermal conductivity 1.2 W/(m·K). Flow is laminar and steady, with fluid viscosity 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa·s and density 1020 kg/m³. The fluid’s specific heat is 4200 J/(kg·K), and it flows at 0.3 m/s. Estimate the maximum steady-state temperature at the outer surface of the chimney wall. How does this result inform the risk of mineral precipitation inside the vent?
