# HFDPO-09130

A quantum materials engineer is designing a metasurface coating for next-gen solar cells that uses subwavelength grooves to enhance light trapping via destructive interference of reflected waves. The grooves are 120 nm deep and spaced 450 nm apart, etched into a silicon nitride layer (n = 2.0). When white light (400–700 nm) strikes the surface at normal incidence, the goal is to minimize reflection at 600 nm—critical for maximizing absorption in adjacent perovskite layers. However, the fabrication process introduces a 3% variation in groove depth. Evaluate whether the interference condition for destructive reflection at 600 nm remains robust under this variability, and determine the angular tolerance (in degrees) over which near-zero reflection persists.
