# HFDPO-07160

A particle accelerator in Geneva detects muons created in the upper atmosphere at 80 km altitude, traveling at 0.999c relative to Earth. Observers on Earth measure their decay time as 30 μs, but the muons’ proper lifetime is only 2.2 μs. However, an onboard detector on a spacecraft moving at 0.8c toward Earth measures the muons’ decay time differently. What is the time interval between the creation and decay of a muon as measured by the spacecraft, and how does this compare to Earth’s measurement? Explain why both measurements are consistent with special relativity.
