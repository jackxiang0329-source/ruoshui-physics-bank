# HFDPO-10680

A satellite-based lidar system measures atmospheric aerosols by analyzing the polarization of backscattered laser light at 532 nm. During a calibration flight, the instrument detects a 28% depolarization ratio in cloud-free air, but the theoretical model predicts only 12%. The satellite’s polarimeter has a measured extinction coefficient of 0.05 km⁻¹ and a scattering phase function consistent with monodisperse spherical particles. The team suspects the discrepancy arises from particle shape effects, but the actual aerosol composition is unknown. Assess whether the observed depolarization is consistent with a mixture of spherical and non-spherical particles, and determine the minimum fraction of non-spherical particles required to account for the data, assuming the non-spherical particles are prolate spheroids with aspect ratio 2:1.
