# HFDPO-09957

A quantum computing lab uses superconducting qubits cooled to 15 mK, embedded in a 3D microwave cavity designed to trap photons at 5 GHz. During operation, a sudden magnetic field transient from a nearby power line induces a current pulse in the cavity walls. The cavity’s copper shielding has a finite conductivity and thickness, causing eddy currents that disrupt qubit coherence. Model the time evolution of the magnetic field inside the cavity, accounting for both displacement current and conduction current in the shield, and determine the minimum thickness required to suppress field penetration to below 1% of the external field within 100 ns.
