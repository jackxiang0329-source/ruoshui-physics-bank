# HFDPO-15170

A fusion reactor's magnetic confinement chamber has a toroidal (doughnut-shaped) plasma vessel with a non-uniform charge density that varies radially: ρ(r) = ρ₀ exp(−(r − R₀)² / σ²), where R₀ = 2.0 m is the major radius, σ = 0.1 m, and ρ₀ = 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ C/m³. Engineers need to calculate the electric field at a point 1.8 m from the center, but the symmetry is broken due to a nearby diagnostic port that slightly distorts the charge distribution. How does the net flux through a Gaussian surface enclosing the entire plasma compare to the total enclosed charge? What can be inferred about the field direction at that point despite the asymmetry?
