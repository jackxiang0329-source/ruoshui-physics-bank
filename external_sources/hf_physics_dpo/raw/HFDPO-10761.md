# HFDPO-10761

During a critical mission on Mars, a rover's primary heater fails due to a corroded connection in its power cable. The system uses a 12 V lithium-ion battery, and the heater is rated at 300 W at 12 V. Engineers measure a 0.25 Ω resistance in the corroded joint, while the rest of the circuit (including the heater) has negligible resistance. They decide to bypass the joint with a temporary copper wire of length 0.8 m and diameter 1.0 mm. Can the bypass carry enough current to power the heater without overheating? What is the minimum safe diameter of a new wire to prevent melting?
