# HFDPO-02997

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole to extract heat from a fractured basalt aquifer at 350°C. The borehole, 2.5 km deep, has a 15 cm diameter and is lined with a 2 cm thick steel casing (k = 45 W/m·K). Water is circulated at 120°C inlet temperature and 60 L/min flow rate. Designers observe a 15°C drop in water temperature over the first 1.2 km, but the expected heat transfer rate from the rock is higher. Evaluate whether the heat loss to the surrounding rock (k = 2.1 W/m·K, assumed constant over the depth) is sufficient to explain the observed cooling, and determine if the thermal resistance of the casing dominates or if convection inside the borehole is the limiting factor.
