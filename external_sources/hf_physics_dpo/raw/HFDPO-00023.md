# HFDPO-00023

A quantum imaging lab is developing a lensless ghost imaging system using entangled photon pairs generated via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) in a periodically poled lithium niobate crystal. The setup uses a 300 μm thick crystal with a poling period of 15 μm, pumped by a 405 nm laser at 10 mW average power. The signal and idler photons are separated by a beamsplitter, with only the idler detected by a single-pixel detector. The signal beam passes through a dynamic spatial light modulator (SLM) displaying a random binary pattern. After 10,000 measurements, the reconstructed image shows a 15% contrast reduction due to temporal jitter in the detection system. What is the minimum coherence time of the photon pairs required to maintain high-fidelity ghost imaging under these conditions, and how does this constrain the pump laser linewidth?
