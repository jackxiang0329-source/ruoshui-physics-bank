# HFDPO-06568

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from blood using inertial focusing in a narrow channel. The device operates at 37°C, with blood modeled as a Newtonian fluid (viscosity μ = 3.5 × 10⁻³ Pa·s), flowing at a rate of 0.5 mL/min through a 200 μm wide, 100 μm high rectangular channel. The CTCs (diameter ~12 μm) are intended to migrate to the channel’s centerline due to balance between shear-induced lift and wall-induced drag. Estimate the Reynolds number and determine whether viscous forces dominate over inertial forces—then explain how this affects the reliability of the separation mechanism.
