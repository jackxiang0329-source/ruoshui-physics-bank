# HFDPO-12190

A laser communication array on a Mars rover uses a 632.8 nm helium-neon laser to send data to an orbiting satellite 400 km above the surface. The receiving photodetector has a finite aperture of 1.2 cm diameter, but atmospheric turbulence and dust in the thin Martian atmosphere cause phase distortions in the beam. To maximize signal strength, engineers must determine the optimal beam divergence angle that balances diffraction spreading with phase coherence over the propagation path. Given that the beam’s complex amplitude at the source is Gaussian and the atmosphere introduces a phase variance of σϕ = 0.6 radians across the beam, what is the optimal divergence angle (in milliradians) that maximizes the fraction of power captured by the detector?
