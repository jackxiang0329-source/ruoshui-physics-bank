# HFDPO-07808

During a high-altitude balloon experiment, a scientist discovers that a 1.2-meter-long, thin-film copper conductor embedded in a flexible polymer matrix begins to overheat unexpectedly at 45,000 m altitude, despite carrying only 0.8 A. The conductor’s resistance measured at 20°C is 3.6 Ω, but thermal runaway occurs when the temperature exceeds 60°C. Given that the thermal resistivity of the polymer is 2.1 K·m/W and it dissipates heat only via conduction through the surrounding air (with effective thermal conductivity of 0.025 W/(m·K)), determine the minimum ambient temperature below which the system remains stable. Assume the conductor’s resistivity increases linearly with temperature (α = 0.0039/°C), and neglect radiation.
