# HFDPO-08771

In a remote desert research station, a solar-powered data logger relies on a 12 V battery bank and a custom DC circuit to manage power during extended sandstorms. The system uses a 50 Ω shunt resistor to monitor current draw from a 240 W load, but readings drift over time due to temperature-induced resistance changes. The logger’s internal microcontroller measures voltage across the shunt but must compensate for the shunt’s temperature coefficient (α = 0.0035/°C) to calculate actual current. If the shunt heats from 25°C to 60°C during operation and the measured voltage drops from 3.0 V to 2.8 V, what is the true current through the load, and how would this error affect long-term data accuracy?
