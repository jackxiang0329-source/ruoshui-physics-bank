# HFDPO-03060

A deep-space telecommunications satellite in a highly elliptical orbit around Titan must perform a precise orbital maneuver to avoid debris from a recent cryovolcanic eruption. The satellite’s current orbit has a periapsis of 1,200 km above Titan’s surface and an apoapsis of 42,000 km. The debris field lies along a 300 km-wide arc near apoapsis, drifting slowly due to Titan’s atmosphere. To avoid impact, the satellite must lower its apoapsis by 4,500 km using a single maneuver at periapsis. Given Titan’s mass (1.345 × 10²³ kg), radius (2,575 km), and negligible atmospheric drag at high altitudes, what is the required Δv? How does the orientation of the burn (prograde vs. retrograde) affect the orbital plane and long-term stability?
