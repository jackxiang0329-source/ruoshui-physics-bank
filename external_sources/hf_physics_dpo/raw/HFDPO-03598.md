# HFDPO-03598

A deep-sea thermal vent ecosystem thrives near a hydrothermal plume where superheated water (350°C, 300 atm) mixes with cold abyssal seawater (2°C). Engineers designing a closed-loop energy harvester aim to extract work from this gradient using a Rankine cycle with a working fluid that remains supercritical. However, the system must operate without external heat sources, relying solely on irreversibilities. The vent emits 50 MW of thermal power, but losses to the surrounding ocean reduce usable heat transfer by 28%. Determine whether the cycle can achieve a second-law efficiency exceeding 60%, and justify your conclusion by analyzing entropy generation rates and exergy destruction in the control volume.
