# HFDPO-02691

During a severe storm, a coastal research vessel experiences structural stress on its weather deck amidships, where the deck slightly bulges upward when wind speeds exceed 35 m/s. Engineers observe that the deck's deflection correlates with wind speed but cannot explain why the pressure distribution causes uplift rather than compression. The vessel’s deck has a length of 18 m, width of 6 m, and the boundary layer wind flow over the deck is assumed to follow a smooth velocity profile, with average flow speed above the deck increasing from 35 m/s at the edge to 52 m/s at the centerline. Using Bernoulli’s principle and assumptions of incompressible, steady flow along streamlines, determine the net upward pressure difference across the deck surface due to the wind-induced pressure gradient, and explain why this could lead to structural fatigue over time.
