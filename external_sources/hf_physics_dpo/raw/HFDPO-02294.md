# HFDPO-02294

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole to extract heat from underground rock formations. To monitor the integrity of the borehole casing, engineers embed a superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) loop near the surface, aligned vertically. When a conductive metallic rock layer (resistivity 0.8 Ω·m, thickness 15 m) moves upward through the borehole at 0.03 m/s due to thermal convection, it induces a transient current in the SQUID loop (radius 2.5 m, 100 turns). The loop measures a peak induced voltage of 1.2 μV. Given the magnetic field decay follows a Gaussian profile with spatial width σ = 0.7 m, determine the average magnetic flux change that occurred through the loop during the passage.
