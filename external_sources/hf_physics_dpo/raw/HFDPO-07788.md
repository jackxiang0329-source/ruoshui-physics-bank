# HFDPO-07788

A research team is designing a new type of electromagnetic shielding for a satellite's sensitive quantum computing module. The module is enclosed in a hollow, spherical copper shell with inner radius 15 cm and outer radius 16 cm. During testing, a point charge of +50 nC is placed at the center of the cavity. The team measures the electric field just outside the outer surface and finds it to be 1.2 × 10⁴ N/C radially outward. Is the shielding effective? What must be the total charge on the outer surface of the shell, and how does Gauss’s Law explain the field distribution?
