# HFDPO-12733

A Mars rover's front wheel encounters a fine, electrostatically charged dust layer resembling loose basalt regolith (density 2,600 kg/m³, particle diameter 15 μm). As the wheel rolls at 0.5 m/s, the dust begins to clump into a coherent, viscous-like layer on the tire surface, increasing rolling resistance. The effective dynamic friction coefficient between the dust layer and tire increases nonlinearly with dust accumulation due to inter-particle van der Waals forces and shear-thickening behavior. Given that the contact patch area grows from 100 cm² to 180 cm² over 10 minutes, and the normal force remains 250 N, determine whether the power required to maintain speed exceeds the rover’s 150 W motor limit, considering both viscous drag and rolling friction.
