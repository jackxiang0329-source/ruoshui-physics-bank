# HFDPO-11047

In a microfluidic lab-on-a-chip device designed for early cancer detection, a suspension of 5 μm diameter antibody-coated beads (density 1.1 g/cm³) flows through a 100 μm wide, 50 μm high rectangular channel at 0.2 μL/min. The fluid is a buffered solution with viscosity 1.3 cP and density 1.02 g/cm³. As the beads approach a Y-shaped junction, they exhibit unexpected clustering upstream — not due to sticking, but due to a rare hydrodynamic focusing instability. Using the Stokes–Einstein and Hagen–Poiseuille frameworks, determine the minimum distance upstream from the junction where flow-induced lift forces begin to dominate over Brownian motion, and explain why this clustering occurs despite uniform flow.
