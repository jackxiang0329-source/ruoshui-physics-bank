# HFDPO-04693

A fusion reactor's cryostat uses high-temperature superconducting (HTS) tapes to confine a 50 T magnetic field in a toroidal geometry, but during ramp-up, localized quenches propagate unpredictably. The tapes are wound in a spiral with 120 turns/m and carry 100 A/mm² current density, yet at 4.2 K, the critical current density drops to 30% of its peak due to vortex pinning inefficiency. If the applied magnetic field exceeds the operational limit of 3 T at any point, the entire winding risks quenching. What is the minimum thickness of the insulating buffer layer needed between adjacent turns to prevent local field exceedance, given the field from a single turn at the center of the coil is 2πμ₀J·r, and field superposition must be considered?
