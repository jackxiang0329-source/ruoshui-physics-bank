# HFDPO-09205

A research team is developing a flexible, wearable biosensor that monitors sweat electrolyte levels in real time during intense athletic training. The sensor uses a microfluidic channel with two interdigitated electrodes (10 mm long, 50 μm wide, 10 μm gap) embedded in a stretchable polymer substrate. During operation, sweat (ε_r ≈ 78) wets the channel, altering the effective dielectric. However, the sensor operates at 100 kHz, and parasitic capacitance from the lead wires and substrate (ε_r ≈ 3.5) adds noise. The team needs to determine the total capacitance across the electrodes as a function of applied strain (0–20%), which changes the gap width nonlinearly due to Poisson’s ratio effects. What is the effective capacitance at 15% strain, and how would you isolate the sensing capacitance from parasitic contributions?
