# HFDPO-11016

A geophysicist studying volcanic activity in Iceland deploys a magnetometer array to detect subsurface magma movements. The array measures a changing magnetic field due to eddy currents induced in conductive rock layers as molten lava shifts beneath the surface. At t = 0, a sudden upward surge in a basaltic magma column (resistivity ρ = 1.5 × 10⁻² Ω·m, conductivity σ = 67 S/m) creates a time-varying magnetic field at a depth of 2 km, given by B(t) = B₀ e^(-t/τ), where B₀ = 10⁻⁴ T and τ = 5 s. The array consists of a square loop of wire (side length L = 100 m) lying flat on the surface, with resistance R = 0.8 Ω. What is the total charge that flows through the loop during the first 20 seconds of the event, and how does this depend on the loop’s orientation relative to the magma’s flow direction?
