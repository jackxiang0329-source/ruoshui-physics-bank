# HFDPO-11707

A solar-powered Mars rover uses a tandem perovskite-silicon photovoltaic cell that must operate efficiently under low-intensity, diffuse sunlight (120 W/m²) and extreme temperature swings (-80°C to +40°C). The cell’s perovskite layer degrades rapidly above 60°C due to ion migration, yet the silicon layer’s bandgap shifts with temperature, reducing voltage output. Design a thermal-electrical feedback system using thermally responsive doping profiles to maintain optimal carrier concentration and prevent hot-spot formation during peak solar intensity.
