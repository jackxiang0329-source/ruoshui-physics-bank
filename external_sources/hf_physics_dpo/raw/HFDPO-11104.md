# HFDPO-11104

During a severe winter storm, an Arctic research station relies on a buried geothermal heat exchanger (100 m long, 0.1 m diameter) to maintain a stable internal temperature. The soil surrounding the pipe has a thermal conductivity of 1.8 W/m·K and a volumetric heat capacity of 2.5 × 10⁶ J/m³·K. The pipe wall is maintained at a constant 15°C, but after 12 hours of operation, the temperature at the center of a 2 m³ insulated concrete block (thermal diffusivity 0.8 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s) located 1 m from the pipe begins to rise. What is the minimum heat flux (W/m²) the pipe must supply to cause a measurable rise (>0.1°C) in the concrete’s temperature, and how does transient conduction in layered soil affect this rate?
