# HFDPO-13996

A quantum imaging team at a research lab is developing a hyperspectral microscope to detect early-stage cancer biomarkers in tissue samples by analyzing scattered light from fluorescent nanoparticles. The setup uses a supercontinuum laser source (spectrum from 450–800 nm) and a spatial light modulator (SLM) to shape the input beam into a complex, structured wavefront. However, they observe unexpected interference fringes in the image plane — not from coherent noise, but from correlated photon pairs generated via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) in a nonlinear crystal placed before the sample. The fringes vary with SLM phase patterns and disappear when the crystal is removed. Estimate the degree of second-order coherence (g⁽²⁾) at the image plane and determine whether the observed fringes imply genuine quantum entanglement or classical interference artifacts.
