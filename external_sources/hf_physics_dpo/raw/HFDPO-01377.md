# HFDPO-01377

In a semiconductor fabrication cleanroom, electrostatic discharge (ESD) damage has caused yield loss in 12 nm node chip production. A technician observes that charged silicon wafers (diameter 300 mm, thickness 775 µm) attract dust particles (mean diameter 0.5 µm, density 2.5 g/cm³) from the surrounding air, even after ionization. The wafers maintain a surface potential of –150 V relative to ground, and the ambient electric field in the cleanroom is measured at 80 V/m due to stray charges on nearby equipment. Determine whether the electrostatic force on a dust particle near the wafer edge exceeds the gravitational force, and evaluate which mitigation strategy—grounding, controlled humidity, or dynamic charge neutralization—is most effective at suppressing particle adhesion.
