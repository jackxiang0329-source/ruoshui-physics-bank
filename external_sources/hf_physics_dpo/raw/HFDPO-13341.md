# HFDPO-13341

During a deep-sea hydrothermal vent survey, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) detects a sudden, oscillatory flow pattern in the vent plume—characterized by periodic density inversions and velocity fluctuations—despite stable thermal input. The plume rises 120 m above the seafloor, with an initial exit velocity of 1.8 m/s from a 0.35 m-diameter vent. At 25 m above the vent, the flow transitions from steady buoyant rise to intermittent, self-sustained vortices. Use the Boussinesq approximation and linear stability analysis to determine the critical Richardson number for the onset of Kelvin–Helmholtz instability, and explain how the interaction between buoyancy and shear modifies the plume’s vertical structure.
