# HFDPO-02165

A quantum imaging team at a research lab is developing a new technique to detect weak biological signals using entangled photon pairs. The setup uses a double-slit interferometer with slits spaced 500 nm apart, illuminated by a coherent 600 nm laser. However, the slits are fabricated with a slight asymmetry—each slit has a width of 400 nm, but one is tapered inward by 5 nm over its length, while the other is uniform. The team observes a distorted interference pattern on the detector, with the central maximum shifted and the side fringes asymmetric. They suspect the tapering causes diffraction effects that interfere with quantum coherence. How does the tapering alter the diffraction envelope, and what is the resulting phase shift at the center of the pattern that could degrade the entanglement visibility?
