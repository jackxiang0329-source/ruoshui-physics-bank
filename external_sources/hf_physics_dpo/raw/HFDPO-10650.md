# HFDPO-10650

A fusion reactor prototype uses a ring-shaped superconducting magnet cooled to 4.2 K to confine plasma at 150 million K. During operation, a sudden power surge causes a localized quench in one segment of the magnet, raising its temperature to 7.8 K—just above the critical temperature for niobium-tin (Nb₃Sn). The magnet’s inductance is 2.5 H, and the stored energy at full current (12 kA) is 180 MJ. Calculate the minimum energy that must be dissipated as heat in the quenched region to initiate a full magnet quench, assuming the heat spreads uniformly over the 0.12 m³ volume of the superconductor.
