# HFDPO-16467

A high-performance fusion reactor’s divertor plate, made of tungsten with a thermal conductivity of 173 W/m·K, is exposed to a localized plasma heat flux of 25 MW/m² for 10 milliseconds during a transient event. The plate is cooled by a subcooled water jet with a flow rate of 0.15 kg/s, an inlet temperature of 60°C, and a heat transfer coefficient of 120,000 W/m²·K. After the pulse, the plate’s surface temperature reaches 2200 K—just below melting—but the bulk material temperature exceeds the boiling point of water at the cooling interface. Assess whether the cooling system is thermally adequate to prevent vapor layer formation and subsequent heat transfer degradation. Justify your conclusion by comparing the actual heat flux to the critical heat flux (CHF) predicted by the Zuber model, accounting for the transient thermal inertia and superheat limits.
