# HFDPO-01837

A high-altitude weather balloon carrying sensitive cryogenic sensors ascends through the stratosphere, where temperatures plummet to −70°C. The sensors are housed in a sealed titanium enclosure filled with liquid nitrogen at 77 K, but after 6.2 hours, data logs show unexpected ice crystals forming on the inner walls—despite no visible leaks. The enclosure’s thermal insulation has a known R-value of 12 m²·K/W, and the outer surface is exposed to −70°C air. Use thermodynamic principles to explain how ice could form *inside* the enclosure, and determine the minimum rate of heat leakage (in W) required to trigger condensation and freezing of nitrogen vapor, assuming the nitrogen remains in equilibrium with its vapor above the liquid at 1 atm.
