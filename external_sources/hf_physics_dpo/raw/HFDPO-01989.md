# HFDPO-01989

During a deep-sea mineral survey, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) detects a submerged hydrothermal chimney structure with a complex internal cavity system. The chimney is partially filled with superheated brine (ρ = 1200 kg/m³) at 350°C, while the surrounding seawater (ρ = 1025 kg/m³) remains at 2°C. At a depth of 2,400 m, bubbles of vapor begin forming at the base of the chimney. Engineers must determine whether the buoyant forces can destabilize the structure over time, given that the chimney’s base is sealed by a porous, permeable sediment cap. Is the chimney structurally stable under the combined hydrostatic and thermal pressure gradients, or will vapor migration cause upward collapse?
