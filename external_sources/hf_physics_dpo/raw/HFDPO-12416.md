# HFDPO-12416

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent emits superheated fluid at 350°C, creating a conductive plume in seawater with resistivity 0.2 Ω·m. A research team deploys a multi-coil sensor array to measure natural electric fields generated by seawater motion through Earth’s magnetic field (50 μT, dip angle 60°). When the plume flows at 0.8 m/s perpendicular to the magnetic field, the induced EMF across a 100 m span of the plume is measured at 1.2 mV. What’s the effective conductivity of the plume fluid, and how does it compare to pure seawater (4 S/m)? Is the observed EMF consistent with ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) induction, or does anomalous resistivity suggest turbulent mixing?
