# HFDPO-12447

In a quantum encryption experiment, photons are sent through a double-slit apparatus at a rate of 10⁶ per second, each with a wavelength of 800 nm. The screen is 1.5 m behind the slits, and the slit separation is 20 μm. After running the experiment for 5 minutes, researchers observe a fringe pattern with a spacing of 0.25 mm. However, when a detector is placed near one slit to determine which path each photon took, the interference pattern vanishes. Explain why the act of measuring "which-path" information destroys the interference, even though the photons are never directly observed during transit. How would you quantify the trade-off between path knowledge and interference visibility using the Englert–Greenberger duality relation?
