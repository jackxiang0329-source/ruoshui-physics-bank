# HFDPO-06506

During a dust storm on Mars, a 2.5-meter-tall robotic explorer detects a persistent electric field of 120 V/m near its solar panel array, which is coated with a dielectric material (κ = 3.2) and has an effective surface charge density of 4.8 × 10⁻⁹ C/m². The panels are arranged in a square lattice with 1.2 m spacing between adjacent panels. As the storm intensifies, dust particles (mean radius 8 μm, density 1.8 g/cm³) begin to levitate near the edges. Using charge distribution modeling and dielectric polarization, determine the minimum charge-to-mass ratio required for a dust particle to experience upward electrostatic force equal to gravity (given g_Mars = 3.7 m/s²), assuming the field is uniform and the particle is symmetrically charged.
