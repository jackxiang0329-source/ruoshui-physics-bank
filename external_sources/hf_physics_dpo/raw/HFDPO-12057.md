# HFDPO-12057

A quantum computing chip operates at millikelvin temperatures, where a superconducting qubit is coupled to a readout resonator via a capacitor with a tunable gap. The capacitor is formed by two parallel superconducting plates separated by a 50 nm oxide layer (εᵣ = 10). Due to quantum tunneling, a small but non-negligible current leaks through the dielectric, causing a decay in the qubit’s coherence time. The system is designed to maintain a capacitance of 1.5 pF with ±1% tolerance. If the dielectric breakdown field is 8 V/nm and the operating voltage is 1.2 V, what is the maximum allowable thickness variation in the oxide layer to avoid dielectric breakdown and preserve coherence — *and how does this constraint interact with the quantum tunneling rate*?
