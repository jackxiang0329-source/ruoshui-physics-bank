# HFDPO-12303

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a rotating cylindrical magnet (radius 0.8 m, mass 240 kg, moment of inertia 45 kg·m²) inside a stationary copper coil array to generate electricity. The magnet spins at 1200 rpm, but due to fluctuating underground heat, its angular velocity varies sinusoidally with time: ω(t) = 1200 + 200 sin(2πf t) rpm, where f = 0.5 Hz. The coil system has 1200 turns, each with area 0.08 m², and resistance 1.2 Ω. The average induced power over one cycle must exceed 15 kW to maintain grid stability. Does it? If not, what design change—increasing turns, area, or magnetic field strength—would achieve the goal, and why?
