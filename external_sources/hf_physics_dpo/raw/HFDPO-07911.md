# HFDPO-07911

A solar-powered drone designed for high-altitude atmospheric research must maintain stable hover over a remote Arctic research station. The drone’s 3.2-meter-long carbon-fiber wing is asymmetrically loaded due to a solar panel array on one side and a thermal sensor array on the other. The wing's center of mass is offset 0.18 m from the fuselage attachment point. To counteract torque from uneven weight distribution, a counterweight of 1.4 kg is mounted on the opposite side. However, wind gusts of up to 22 m/s can induce a 350 N·m moment about the wing's pivot. The drone’s control system adjusts the angle of attack dynamically, but the static stability of the wing-tail assembly must be verified under worst-case wind. Determine the minimum required distance from the pivot to the mass center of the tail fin (8.3 kg) to ensure static equilibrium when the maximum wind moment is applied.
