# HFDPO-03020

A Mars rover’s suspension system uses a dual-axle, spring-damper configuration to navigate regolith-covered slopes with variable tilt angles. During descent on a 25° incline, the front axle encounters a 20 cm high, 40 cm long rock obstruction. The rover’s mass is 220 kg, and the front suspension has a spring constant of 1200 N/m and damping coefficient of 350 N·s/m. The rear axle’s springs are compressed by 0.15 m due to load transfer. Determine the peak accelerations experienced by the rover’s chassis during impact and the condition under which the rover might tip forward—considering friction between wheels and regolith (μ = 0.4), wheelbase = 1.8 m, and center of mass located 0.6 m above ground and 0.9 m behind the front axle.
