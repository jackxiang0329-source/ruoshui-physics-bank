# HFDPO-11646

A coastal city is designing a new seawall to mitigate storm surge damage during Category 3 hurricanes. Engineers estimate peak surge waves will reach 4.5 m in height with a wavelength of 120 m and a period of 12 seconds. The wall is to be built 200 m offshore from the current shoreline, but local sediment transport data shows that wave energy dissipates significantly over the shallow continental shelf. Using the shallow water wave model and energy flux conservation, determine the expected wave height at the wall, and explain how the design might need to account for nonlinear wave steepening as the wave approaches the wall.
