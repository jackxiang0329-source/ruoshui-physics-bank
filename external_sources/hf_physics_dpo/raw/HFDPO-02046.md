# HFDPO-02046

A solar farm in the Mojave Desert uses bifacial photovoltaic panels mounted at a 30° tilt, but energy output drops unexpectedly during midday when sunlight reflects off nearby sand dunes. Engineers suspect glare-induced polarization mismatch in the panels’ anti-reflective coatings, which are designed for unpolarized light. Given that sand reflection produces partially polarized light with a degree of polarization of 0.6 at 30° Brewster’s angle, and the panels’ front glass has a transmission axis aligned vertically, calculate the effective irradiance reaching the cells when sunlight (500 W/m² unpolarized) reflects off sand and strikes the panel. Assume the reflection obeys Fresnel’s equations and the panel’s coating transmits 90% of incident light.
