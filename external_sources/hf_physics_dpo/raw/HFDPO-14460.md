# HFDPO-14460

In a remote Arctic research station, a critical sensor array relies on a 120-meter copper cable running from a central power unit to a weather station buried under snow. During a sudden temperature drop from -5°C to -40°C, the station’s data stream flickers—indicating unstable resistance. The cable’s resistivity at 20°C is 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, with a temperature coefficient of 3.9×10⁻³ /°C. The central unit maintains a steady 12.0 V DC supply. If the power delivered to the sensor drops by 18% due to cold-induced resistance change, what is the actual current drawn at -40°C?
