# HFDPO-05115

A team developing a quantum sensor for detecting underground water reserves uses a nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center in diamond, where electron spin states are sensitive to magnetic fields from subsurface water flow. The NV center's energy splitting is 2.88 GHz at zero magnetic field, but measurements show a 1.05 GHz shift in a 15 m deep borehole. Given that the magnetic field from a 10 L/s groundwater flow generates ~1.2 μT at the sensor depth, does this shift support the hypothesis that the signal arises from hydrodynamic magnetism, or could it be due to stray environmental fields (±0.4 μT)?
