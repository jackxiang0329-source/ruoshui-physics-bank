# HFDPO-09461

A deep-sea mining drone descends 3,000 meters to extract polymetallic nodules from the ocean floor. The drone’s mass is 850 kg, and it uses a buoyancy-controlled descent system with variable ballast. As it sinks, it must overcome drag forces that increase with depth due to temperature and pressure changes. At 1,500 m depth, the drag force is 1,200 N, and it increases linearly with depth to 4,500 N at 3,000 m. The drone’s descent is slowed by a winch that controls its speed to prevent damage. What is the total work done by the gravitational force and the drag force during the descent? If the drone then ascends at constant speed using a counterweight system, how much energy must the winch supply to return it safely? Assume the buoyant force remains constant at 1,750 N throughout.
