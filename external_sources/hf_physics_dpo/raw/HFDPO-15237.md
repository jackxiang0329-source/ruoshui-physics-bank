# HFDPO-15237

During a deep-sea mineral sampling mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) retrieves a cylindrical core sample from a hydrothermal vent field at 2,800 meters depth. The sample, sealed in a rigid titanium tube with a 5 cm diameter and 45 cm length, was saturated with supercritical hydrothermal fluid. Upon ascent, the ROV's pressure sensor detects a sudden 1.8 MPa spike inside the core tube just before surface retrieval—despite no mechanical breach. The crew suspects a thermodynamic phase shift during ascent. Determine the minimum initial temperature of the hydrothermal fluid at depth that could cause this pressure rise, assuming the fluid behaves as a non-ideal gas with compressibility factor Z = 0.8 at depth, and that the core tube volume remains constant during ascent.
