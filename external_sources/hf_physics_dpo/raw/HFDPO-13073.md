# HFDPO-13073

A quantum imaging lab develops a single-photon camera using spatially entangled photons to achieve super-resolution imaging through scattering media. The system employs a heralded photon source with a 10% heralding efficiency and operates at a wavelength of 800 nm. When imaging a biological sample embedded in a turbid medium with a scattering mean free path of 3 μm, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) drops to 1.2—below the detection threshold. To recover meaningful contrast, engineers consider increasing the pump power, but this risks saturation and multi-photon events. What is the minimum number of heralded photon pairs needed per pixel to achieve an SNR ≥ 5, assuming detector dark counts are negligible and the background is dominated by uncorrelated photons?
