# HFDPO-10577

A next-generation quantum sensor designed to detect dark matter axions relies on a single-crystal silicon carbide (SiC) lattice doped with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers. The sensor operates at 4 K, where phonon-assisted spin-flip transitions in the NV centers are sensitive to ultra-light bosonic fields. However, recent measurements show a persistent signal fluctuation at 1.2 THz, correlated with lattice strain from micro-cracks formed during growth. Using first-principles calculations and experimental data, determine how the interaction between axion-induced spin-orbit coupling and localized strain in SiC modifies the effective g-factor of NV centers, and propose a strain-engineering strategy to suppress decoherence without altering the crystal’s topological defect density.
