# HFDPO-07997

In a quantum computing lab, researchers observe that a single electron tunneling through a 1.2-nm-thick silicon dioxide barrier in a Josephson junction exhibits interference fringes when the junction is biased with a superconducting current. The observed fringe spacing corresponds to a de Broglie wavelength of 8.7 nm. However, during a control experiment, a pulsed laser field (λ = 633 nm, intensity ≈ 10⁶ W/m²) is applied perpendicular to the tunneling path. The fringes vanish entirely. Why does the laser field destroy the interference pattern, and does this imply the electron behaves as a particle during the interaction?
