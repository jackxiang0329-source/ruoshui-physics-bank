# HFDPO-10325

A biomedical engineer is designing a handheld device to detect early-stage skin cancer using laser-induced fluorescence. The device uses a 405 nm laser to excite endogenous fluorophores in tissue, and a spectrometer captures the emitted light. However, measurements show that the signal from melanin-rich regions is unexpectedly weak—despite melanin being a strong fluorophore. The engineer suspects that high absorption and scattering in pigmented skin may be attenuating both excitation and emission. Given that melanin absorbs 90% of 405 nm light per 1 mm of depth and scatters light with an anisotropy factor of 0.9, estimate the effective fluorescence yield detected at 2 mm depth relative to the surface, assuming isotropic emission and negligible re-absorption of emitted photons.
