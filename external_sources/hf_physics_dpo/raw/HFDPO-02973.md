# HFDPO-02973

A fusion reactor design team struggles to stabilize a tokamak’s magnetic confinement field, which fluctuates unpredictably when the superconducting coils—cooled to 4.2 K—experience sudden current surges during plasma ignition. The coils, made of Nb₃Sn with a critical current density Jc = 1.2 × 10⁸ A/m² and a critical field Hc₂ = 25 T, are wound into a toroidal solenoid with a major radius of 2.5 m and a minor radius of 0.5 m. During operation, the magnetic field at the coil center reaches 12 T, and the current rises to 85 kA. Is the superconducting state preserved, or has the system entered the normal state?
