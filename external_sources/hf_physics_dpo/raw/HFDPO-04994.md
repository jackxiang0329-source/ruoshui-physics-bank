# HFDPO-04994

A fusion reactor design team faces a crisis: the tokamak’s toroidal magnetic field, essential for confining superheated plasma at 150 million Kelvin, is showing unexpected instabilities. The primary field (0.8 T) is generated by 12 identical superconducting coils arranged in a circle with radius 2.5 m, each carrying 45 kA. However, small deviations in coil alignment cause a net vertical field component that pushes plasma toward the reactor walls. Calculate the minimum angular misalignment (in milliradians) of a single coil that could produce a vertical field of 0.02 mT at the center, assuming perfect symmetry otherwise.
