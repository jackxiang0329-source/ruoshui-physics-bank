# HFDPO-11907

A fusion reactor prototype uses a toroidal magnetic confinement chamber where plasma particles collide at near-relativistic speeds. During a 30-second pulse, 4.2 × 10¹⁹ deuterium-tritium fusion reactions release energy, producing 17.6 MeV per reaction, but the chamber’s power monitors detect only 85% of the expected energy output. Scientists suspect neutrinos—uncharged, weakly interacting particles—carry away some of the energy. Given that neutrinos are emitted isotropically and travel at nearly *c*, and the reactor’s total mass-energy balance must still satisfy conservation of energy and momentum, estimate the average momentum carried by each neutrino if the total measured recoil momentum of the fusion chamber is 1.2 × 10⁻³ kg·m/s.
