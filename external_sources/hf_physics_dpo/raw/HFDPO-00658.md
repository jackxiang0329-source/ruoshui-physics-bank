# HFDPO-00658

A research team is developing a flexible bioelectronic patch for real-time neural monitoring in patients with epilepsy. The patch uses a serpentine-shaped conductor made of a titanium nitride (TiN) nanolayer, 150 nm thick and 5 μm wide, patterned on a polyimide substrate. During clinical trials, the device shows unpredictable signal drift when implanted at 37°C. Measurements reveal the resistance of the trace increases by 8.2% over 72 hours, despite no physical damage. The original resistance is 12.4 kΩ. Estimate the effective thermal coefficient of resistance (α) for the TiN layer under physiological conditions, assuming only temperature and gradual oxidation contribute, and explain why this change might compromise signal fidelity.
