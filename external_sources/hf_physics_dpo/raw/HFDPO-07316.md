# HFDPO-07316

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole with a superconducting loop of wire (radius 5 m, resistance negligible) suspended 2 km below the surface, where the Earth’s magnetic field is 50 μT and inclined at 65° to the vertical. Over 10 minutes, magma movement causes the loop to rotate at 0.5 rad/s about a horizontal axis. Engineers need to determine the peak induced EMF and assess whether this setup could sustain a 1 MW load during peak energy demand, considering only the changing magnetic flux through the loop.
