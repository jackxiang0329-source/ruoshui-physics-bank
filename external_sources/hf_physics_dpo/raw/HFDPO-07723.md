# HFDPO-07723

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses parabolic troughs to focus sunlight onto a central receiver tube, heating thermal oil to 400°C. The oil flows at 1.2 m/s through a 5 cm diameter tube made of stainless steel (k = 15 W/m·K), with a 1.5 mm wall thickness. During a sudden cloud cover, the solar input drops to zero, and the oil begins cooling. The ambient air temperature is 25°C with a natural convection heat transfer coefficient of 8 W/m²·K. The oil has a density of 850 kg/m³, specific heat of 2100 J/kg·K, and thermal diffusivity of 0.8 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s. After 4 minutes of cooling, the oil temperature at the tube’s center is measured as 345°C. Is the oil experiencing transient conduction with negligible internal resistance, or does the temperature gradient within the oil matter significantly? Evaluate using both lumped capacitance and infinite cylinder models, and determine how the heat transfer coefficient would need to change to validate the lumped assumption.
