# HFDPO-00168

A team of quantum materials engineers is designing a new type of solid-state battery using a lithium-terminated graphene anode. During testing, they notice unexpected voltage fluctuations when the battery charges at 2.5 A, despite ideal electrode geometry. Spectroscopy reveals that lithium ions are not binding uniformly—some occupy substitutional sites in the graphene lattice, while others adsorb on the surface. Given that the binding energy for surface adsorption is 1.8 eV and for substitutional incorporation is 3.2 eV, and that the Fermi level shifts by 0.6 eV upon doping, determine whether the system favors surface adsorption or substitutional intercalation under these conditions, and explain how electron density redistribution affects ion mobility.
