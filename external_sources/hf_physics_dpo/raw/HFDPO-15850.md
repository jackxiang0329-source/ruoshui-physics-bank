# HFDPO-15850

A deep-sea submersible’s primary camera lens, designed for high-contrast imaging at 800 meters depth, experiences unexpected image distortion when panning across a hydrothermal vent plume. The plume emits warm, mineral-rich water (n ≈ 1.335) at 200°C, creating a dynamic refractive index gradient that varies radially from the vent’s core (n = 1.340) to the surrounding cold seawater (n = 1.342). The lens has a 50 mm focal length and a 45° half-angle field of view. Calculate the maximum angular deviation of a ray entering the lens from the plume’s edge, 3 meters from the vent, assuming the gradient follows a hyperbolic decay: n(r) = 1.342 – 0.002 / (1 + r²), where r is in meters from the vent. Does this distortion exceed the lens’s acceptable tolerance of 1.2°?
