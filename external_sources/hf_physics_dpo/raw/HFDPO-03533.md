# HFDPO-03533

A bioengineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from whole blood using deterministic lateral displacement (DLD) arrays. The device operates at a flow rate of 1.2 mL/min through a 50 μm-wide channel with 100 rows of 15 μm-diameter cylindrical obstacles arranged in a staggered lattice. Blood has a viscosity of 3.5 mPa·s and density of 1060 kg/m³. The CTCs (mean diameter 12 μm, density 1040 kg/m³) are to be deflected into a specific outlet based on size differences. What is the critical Reynolds number threshold above which the flow becomes unsteady and disrupts separation efficiency, and how does the presence of red blood cells (RBCs, 7.5 μm diameter, volume fraction 45%) alter this threshold through viscous coupling and hydrodynamic dispersion?
