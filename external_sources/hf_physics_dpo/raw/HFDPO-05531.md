# HFDPO-05531

A research team is developing a hybrid geothermal-thermal energy storage system for a remote Arctic village. The system uses molten salt (specific heat capacity 1400 J/kg·K) stored at 580°C to power a Stirling engine during winter nights. During peak solar hours, a 1200 kg batch of salt is heated from 120°C to 580°C using concentrated solar thermal collectors with 62% efficiency. The village’s energy demand is 800 kW for 14 hours nightly. The Stirling engine operates between 580°C and -15°C (outside air), with an actual efficiency 70% of Carnot. What is the minimum mass of molten salt storage required to meet the full nightly demand, and why might the actual system require 25% more salt than this theoretical minimum?
