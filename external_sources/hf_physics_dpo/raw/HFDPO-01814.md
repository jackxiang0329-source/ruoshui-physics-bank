# HFDPO-01814

A solar thermal storage system in a remote desert community uses molten nitrate salt (specific heat 1400 J/kg·K, density 1800 kg/m³) stored in a cylindrical tank insulated with 15 cm of silica aerogel (k = 0.015 W/m·K). During a 6-hour sunny day, the salt is heated from 250°C to 550°C at a rate of 120 kW. At night, the system must maintain a minimum output of 60 kW for 8 hours via natural convection and radiation cooling. Estimate the minimum tank diameter required to meet this demand, assuming negligible heat loss through the tank base and that heat transfer follows Newton’s law of cooling with an effective ambient temperature of 20°C and an overall heat transfer coefficient of 5 W/m²·K.
