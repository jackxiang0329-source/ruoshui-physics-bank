# HFDPO-05607

During a lunar regolith excavation mission, a rover’s heat shield—made of a multilayered aerogel composite—develops a microcrack after impact. The shield must maintain a temperature gradient across its 1.2 cm thickness to prevent thermal runaway in the onboard electronics (which operate at 25°C). The outer surface faces direct solar radiation (1360 W/m²) and radiates to space at 3 K, while the inner surface is thermally coupled to the electronics. Given the aerogel’s thermal conductivity varies with temperature as k(T) = 0.025 + 0.0008T (W/m·K), and assuming negligible convection, evaluate whether the shield can maintain a safe inner temperature under steady-state conditions, considering both radiative and conductive heat transfer balance.
