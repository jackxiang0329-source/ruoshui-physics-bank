# HFDPO-16015

A quantum dot solar cell uses semiconductor nanocrystals to absorb sunlight, but efficiency drops sharply at wavelengths below 400 nm—despite photons having sufficient energy. Engineers observe that at these short wavelengths, electron-hole pairs are generated too rapidly, causing energy loss through thermalization. What is the minimum de Broglie wavelength of the photoelectrons emitted when a 350 nm photon strikes a quantum dot with a work function of 1.8 eV? How does this wavelength compare to the dot’s diameter (5 nm), and what does this imply about electron confinement effects?
