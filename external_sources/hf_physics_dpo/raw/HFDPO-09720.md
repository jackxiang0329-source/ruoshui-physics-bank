# HFDPO-09720

A wind farm in northern Canada experiences frequent power outages during intense geomagnetic storms. Engineers discover that induced currents in the long underground transmission cables—each 45 km long and running north-south—are causing protective relays to trip. During a moderate storm, the Earth’s magnetic field changes at a rate of 25 μT/min across the horizontal component. The cables form a loop with the ground, effectively creating a 45 km by 100 m rectangular area. Estimate the peak induced EMF in the loop and explain why the surge protection system might fail even if the voltage is below nominal line voltage.
