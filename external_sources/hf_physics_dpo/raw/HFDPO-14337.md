# HFDPO-14337

In a remote Arctic research station, a team uses a solar thermal collector to maintain a cryogenic storage unit at −150 °C, critical for preserving ancient permafrost samples. The collector, mounted on a rotating frame, receives 800 W/m² of solar irradiance during peak hours, but heat loss occurs through conduction along a 2 m titanium rod (k = 22 W/m·K) connecting the collector to the storage unit, and through convection (h = 12 W/m²·K, T∞ = −20 °C). The rod has a 5 cm diameter and is insulated only at the outer surface. Determine the minimum solar collector area required to maintain steady-state operation if the thermally conductive path is the dominant heat leak. Assume steady-state, one-dimensional conduction and negligible radiation.
