# HFDPO-12029

A deep-sea thermal vent ecosystem thrives near hydrothermal chimneys where superheated water (350°C, 250 atm) erupts from the ocean floor, mixing with cold seawater (2°C) at depths of 2,500 meters. Engineers propose a closed-loop OTEC (Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion) system using the vent plume as a heat source and the deep cold as a sink. The system must produce 5 MW of net electrical power using a working fluid with a critical temperature of 300°C and a saturation pressure of 12 MPa. What is the minimum theoretical efficiency required, and what practical limitations (beyond Carnot) could prevent the system from achieving this even if the heat exchangers were perfectly designed?
