# HFDPO-01274

In a cutting-edge quantum computing chip, superconducting qubits are coupled via Josephson junctions embedded in a tunable capacitor array. During calibration, engineers notice that the resonance frequency of a qubit shifts unpredictably when the control voltage varies, despite the capacitor’s nominal capacitance being fixed at 12 fF. The capacitor consists of two parallel aluminum films separated by a 5 nm thick aluminum oxide insulator with a dielectric constant of 8.5, but the oxide layer exhibits a non-uniform thickness due to fabrication imperfections. The variation in thickness is modeled as δ(t) = 0.2 sin(2πx/L), where x is the position along the capacitor and L = 10 μm. How does this thickness variation affect the effective capacitance, and what is the resulting change in resonance frequency if the qubit’s inductance is 0.8 nH?
