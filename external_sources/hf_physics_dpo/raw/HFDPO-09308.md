# HFDPO-09308

In a fusion energy experiment, a toroidal plasma chamber is enclosed in a vacuum vessel with a complex, non-uniform magnetic shield. Engineers measure a net electric flux of 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ N·m²/C through a Gaussian surface wrapping the innermost 30% of the toroidal volume, despite the absence of free charges in the region. The surface is a right circular cylinder of radius 0.8 m and length 1.5 m, coaxial with the torus. What can be inferred about the distribution of bound charges on the shield's inner surface, and how must the electric field vary radially to satisfy Gauss’s Law in this non-symmetric, confined geometry?
