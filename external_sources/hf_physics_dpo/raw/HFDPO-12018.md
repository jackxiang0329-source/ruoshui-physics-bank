# HFDPO-12018

During a high-altitude balloon flight, scientists observe unexpected charge accumulation on the conductive Mylar envelope, causing intermittent arcing to the payload harness. The balloon ascends through a region with a vertical electric field of 120 V/m, and the envelope, initially neutral, gains charge at a rate of 0.8 nC/s due to triboelectric contact with ionized air. The envelope is a 12 m diameter spherical shell with a surface resistivity of 1.5 × 10¹² Ω·m. When the potential difference between the envelope and a grounded metal ring (5 cm away) exceeds 3.2 kV, a spark occurs. Find the time delay between launch and first spark, and determine whether field-induced charge leakage or resistive dissipation dominates the charge balance.
