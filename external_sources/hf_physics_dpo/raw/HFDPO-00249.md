# HFDPO-00249

A deep-space satellite detects a faint, polarized microwave signal from a distant exoplanet's atmosphere, but it's corrupted by interference from a nearby orbital debris field. The signal has a frequency of 14.2 GHz (hydrogen line), with a measured intensity of 3.5 × 10⁻²⁴ W/m² at the satellite’s antenna. The debris emits thermal radiation at 200 K and forms a spherical shell 5 km thick centered 100 km from the satellite. Assuming the debris scatters the signal isotropically with 90% efficiency and the original signal was unpolarized, determine whether the interference from debris outweighs the exoplanet signal in detectability—evaluating the signal-to-interference ratio (SIR).
