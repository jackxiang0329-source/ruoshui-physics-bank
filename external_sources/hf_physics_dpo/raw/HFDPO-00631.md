# HFDPO-00631

A marine biologist studying coral reefs uses a handheld laser scanner to map reef structures underwater. The device emits a green laser (λ = 532 nm) through a 0.15 mm-wide slit in a waterproof casing. As the scanner moves, the spread of the diffraction pattern on the reef surface changes subtly, indicating the presence of microcracks in the calcium carbonate structure. When the device is held 2.0 m from a reef face, the first minimum of the single-slit diffraction pattern appears 4.6 cm from the center. Could this measured spread be due solely to diffraction, or might interference from surface microstructures be influencing the pattern?
