# HFDPO-05251

A quantum computing research team is developing a photonic chip that uses a precisely engineered array of 1000 subwavelength slits (width 200 nm) spaced 800 nm apart to generate entangled photon states via near-field interference. When illuminated by a coherent laser (λ = 633 nm), the far-field intensity pattern shows unexpected suppression at angles where constructive interference was expected. The team suspects a phase shift caused by surface plasmon resonance at the slit edges, altering the effective optical path. They need to determine whether the observed pattern is due to interference from the slit array alone, or if edge effects are modifying the phase distribution.
