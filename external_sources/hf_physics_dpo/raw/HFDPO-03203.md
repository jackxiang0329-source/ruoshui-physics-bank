# HFDPO-03203

During a deep-sea mining expedition, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) retrieves a cylindrical core sample from a hydrothermal vent field at 3,200 meters depth. The core, sealed in a rigid titanium casing (density 4,500 kg/m³), is suspended by a tether under tension. As the ROV ascends, the casing begins to compress due to pressure changes, altering buoyancy. The core’s internal volume is 0.012 m³, and it's initially fully saturated with seawater (ρ = 1,030 kg/m³). If the casing’s outer volume remains constant but internal porosity decreases by 15% due to compression, what is the net upward force on the casing just as it reaches 500 m depth, assuming no fluid exchange and isothermal conditions?
