# HFDPO-04372

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate cancer cells from healthy blood cells using a Y-shaped channel with a narrow constriction. The device operates at a flow rate of 3.2 μL/min, with blood viscosity η = 3.5 × 10⁻³ Pa·s and density ρ = 1060 kg/m³. The main channel has a diameter of 200 μm, while the constriction reduces it to 50 μm. If the pressure at the inlet is 120 mmHg and the exit pressure is atmospheric, will the Bernoulli effect cause sufficient pressure drop in the constriction to preferentially deflect cancer cells (larger, more rigid) due to altered flow dynamics, or will viscous forces dominate and prevent effective separation?
