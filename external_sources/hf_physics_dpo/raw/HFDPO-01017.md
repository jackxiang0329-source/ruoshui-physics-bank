# HFDPO-01017

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole to extract heat from underground rock formations, but performance drops unexpectedly after 6 months of operation. Engineers discover that a conductive fluid layer (permeability μ₀, resistivity ρ = 1.2 Ω·m) near the wellbore has developed turbulent flow due to thermal gradients, inducing eddy currents that dissipate energy as heat. The fluid moves axially at v = 0.3 m/s through a cylindrical region of radius R = 2.0 m, where a background magnetic field of B₀ = 50 μT (Earth’s field) is oriented vertically. Estimate the average power dissipated per unit length due to eddy currents in the fluid layer, assuming the induced electric field forms circular loops and the fluid behaves as a uniform conductor.
