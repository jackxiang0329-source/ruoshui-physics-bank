# HFDPO-11056

A spacecraft traveling at 0.95c relative to Earth must synchronize its onboard quantum clocks with a distant space station orbiting a neutron star, where gravitational time dilation is negligible but velocity effects dominate. The station sends a laser pulse at t=0 in its frame to initiate synchronization. Due to Doppler shift and relativistic time delay, the pulse arrives 14.2 minutes later in the station’s frame. The spacecraft must calculate the *proper time* elapsed on its own clock during the pulse’s journey and determine whether to adjust its clock forward or backward, accounting for the non-simultaneity of events in different frames.
