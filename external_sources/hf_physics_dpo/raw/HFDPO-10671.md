# HFDPO-10671

A fusion reactor in a remote Arctic facility experiences unexplained neutron flux spikes during startup, peaking at 3.2 × 10¹⁴ n/s, despite maintaining nominal plasma density and magnetic confinement. The facility’s shielding design assumes negligible neutron production from parasitic reactions, but now contamination in the first wall material suggests unexpected activation. Given a 6 mm thick graphite tile with trace ⁶Li at 0.15 wt%, and a neutron spectrum peaking at 14 MeV with Σₐ for ⁶Li(n,α) = 1.3 b, determine whether (and how) the ⁶Li(n,α) reaction could account for the spike, and explain why this affects reactor safety margins.
