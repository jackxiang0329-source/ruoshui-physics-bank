# HFDPO-02022

A quantum imaging lab develops a ghost imaging setup using thermal light from a broadband LED (λ = 650 nm, Δλ = 50 nm) to image a fragile biological sample without direct illumination. The reference arm uses a single-pixel detector, while the object arm is shielded via a spatial light modulator (SLM) with 1920×1080 pixels. To minimize exposure damage, they aim to reconstruct the image with only 10% of the total light intensity normally required by conventional imaging. How should the SLM's phase patterns be optimized across multiple measurement cycles, and what is the minimum number of random Hadamard patterns needed to achieve a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 15 dB in the reconstructed image, assuming the detector has a quantum efficiency of 85% and dark current noise of 0.5 e⁻/s?
