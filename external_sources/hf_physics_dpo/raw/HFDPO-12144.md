# HFDPO-12144

A solar farm in the Mojave Desert uses parabolic trough collectors to heat synthetic oil to 400°C for steam generation, but during a prolonged heatwave, thermal efficiency drops by 18% despite constant solar irradiance. Measurements show the oil outlet temperature stabilizes at 382°C instead of 400°C. The system has a 500 m² collector area, operates at a 2.1 MW thermal load, and uses oil with a specific heat of 2.1 kJ/kg·K. The oil flows at 1.8 kg/s, and ambient air temperature averages 45°C. Assuming negligible conduction losses and radiation from the pipe to sky is modeled by a sky temperature of 15°C, determine the effective convective heat transfer coefficient on the outer pipe surface, and discuss how changes in this coefficient might explain the efficiency drop.
