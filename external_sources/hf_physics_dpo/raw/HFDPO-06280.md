# HFDPO-06280

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a network of steel-reinforced concrete support legs to stabilize a 120-meter tall heat exchanger tower anchored into a volcanic rock formation. During a seismic event, the tower experiences a lateral force of 4.8 MN (meganewtons) at its top due to ground acceleration. The base of the tower rests on three symmetrically arranged legs, each embedded 15 meters into basalt bedrock with a friction coefficient of 0.65. However, recent drilling reveals a subsurface fault plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal, intersecting one leg’s foundation. What is the minimum horizontal force the remaining two legs must resist to prevent rotational failure of the tower, assuming the fault reduces the effective normal force on the third leg by 40%?
