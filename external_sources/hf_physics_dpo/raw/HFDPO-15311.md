# HFDPO-15311

A lunar research outpost uses a buried heat exchanger to maintain stable temperatures in its habitat modules. The system relies on the Moon’s subsurface regolith (thermal conductivity 0.03 W/m·K), which at 1 meter depth has a nearly constant temperature of 250 K. During a 14-day lunar night, the habitat generates 1.2 kW of waste heat. The heat exchanger consists of a 120 m long copper pipe (diameter 2 cm, thermal conductivity 400 W/m·K) buried 1.5 m deep. Designers must determine how long before the pipe’s surface temperature exceeds the safe operating limit of 350 K, assuming no heat loss to the vacuum above and using a transient conduction model with convective resistance at the pipe wall.
