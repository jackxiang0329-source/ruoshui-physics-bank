# HFDPO-04003

A quantum dot solar cell made of lead sulfide nanoparticles (diameter 5.2 nm) operates under concentrated sunlight (250 W/m²), but efficiency drops sharply above 60°C due to phonon-assisted carrier escape. The dots are confined in a porous silica matrix with surface defects acting as trap states (density ≈ 8 × 10¹⁸ m⁻³). Estimate the fraction of photoexcited electrons trapped at equilibrium at 300 K, given the trap depth is 0.4 eV below the conduction band and the effective mass of the electron in the dot is 0.15mₑ. Consider the system as a two-level quantum system with finite density of states and non-ideal carrier injection.
