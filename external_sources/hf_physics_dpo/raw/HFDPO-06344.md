# HFDPO-06344

A geophysicist studying seismic wave propagation in subduction zones models a segment of the Earth's lithosphere as a layered viscoelastic medium. During a rupture event, a 15 km long fault segment slips by 2.8 meters over 12 seconds, generating strong shear waves. The surrounding rock has a shear modulus of 38 GPa and a Poisson’s ratio of 0.27. Due to high temperature and pressure, the material exhibits time-dependent behavior—its effective shear modulus drops by 18% over 3 seconds post-rupture. What is the peak shear strain in the adjacent crust during the slip, and how does the time-dependent stiffness affect the energy density of the generated seismic waves?
