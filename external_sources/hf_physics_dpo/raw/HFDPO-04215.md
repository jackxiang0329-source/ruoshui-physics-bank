# HFDPO-04215

In a microfluidic lab-on-a-chip device designed for early cancer detection, a suspension of antibody-coated microbeads (diameter 5 μm, density 1050 kg/m³) flows through a 200 μm wide channel lined with hydrogel pillars spaced 400 μm apart. The fluid (viscosity 1.2 cP, density 1010 kg/m³) enters at 45 μL/min. Due to viscous drag, beads deviate from streamlines and accumulate at specific pillar gaps. Estimate the transverse velocity of a bead near the channel centerline as it approaches a pillar, assuming laminar flow and no inertial effects, and explain whether this focusing mechanism could efficiently separate cancer cells (typical size 15 μm) from blood cells (7 μm).
