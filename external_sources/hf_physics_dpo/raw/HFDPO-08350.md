# HFDPO-08350

A remote weather station in the Arctic relies on a small heat engine to power its instruments, using the temperature difference between geothermal heat from underground (85°C) and the frigid ambient air (-30°C). The engine operates near ideal efficiency but produces only 1.2 kW of usable power. Engineers notice the output has dropped to 0.9 kW despite no mechanical failure. They suspect fouling in the heat exchanger reduces the heat transfer rate. What is the new heat input rate to the engine, and how does the fouling affect the thermodynamic efficiency?
