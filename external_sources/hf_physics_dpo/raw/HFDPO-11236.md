# HFDPO-11236

During a deep-space mission, a malfunction in the Mars rover’s power system causes a critical instrument to overheat. The rover’s thermal regulation relies on a custom-designed resistive heater array made of a carbon composite material. The array is shaped like a hollow cylinder (inner radius 5 cm, outer radius 8 cm, length 30 cm) with a resistivity that increases exponentially with temperature: ρ(T) = ρ₀·e^(αT), where ρ₀ = 1.2×10⁻⁵ Ω·m, α = 0.01 K⁻¹, and T is in Kelvin. The heater is powered by a constant voltage source of 24 V. At startup, the temperature is 200 K, but the system stabilizes when heat loss equals power dissipation. The thermal conductivity of the material is 15 W/m·K, and heat is lost radially to the surrounding vacuum via radiation (emissivity ε = 0.9, Stefan-Boltzmann constant σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²·K⁴). Determine the stable operating temperature of the heater.
