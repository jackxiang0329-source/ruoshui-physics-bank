# HFDPO-07216

A quantum dot solar cell prototype uses lead-sulfide nanocrystals (diameter ~5 nm) embedded in a polymer matrix. Under simulated sunlight (100 mW/cm²), the device achieves 14% efficiency, but researchers observe a sharp drop in performance when operating at temperatures above 60°C. The bandgap of the nanocrystals is measured at 1.4 eV at 300 K, but shifts by ~0.08 eV when heated to 370 K. Propose a physical mechanism responsible for this efficiency loss and calculate the dominant contribution of electron-phonon coupling to the bandgap renormalization, assuming a Debye temperature of 250 K and using a linear response model.
