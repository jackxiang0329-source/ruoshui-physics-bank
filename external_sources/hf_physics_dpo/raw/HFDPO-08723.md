# HFDPO-08723

In a microfluidic device designed for early cancer detection, cells suspended in plasma (μ = 1.2 × 10⁻³ Pa·s) move through a 50 μm-wide channel at a flow speed of 0.5 mm/s. The device uses a hydrodynamic focusing technique, where a sheath flow (viscosity 1.1 × 10⁻³ Pa·s) confines the cells into a narrow stream. Given that the flow is laminar and fully developed, determine the distance required for the cell stream to converge to a width of 10 μm if the sheath flow creates a parabolic velocity profile with a centerline speed of 2 mm/s. Assume the cells are non-deformable and the channel is rectangular.
