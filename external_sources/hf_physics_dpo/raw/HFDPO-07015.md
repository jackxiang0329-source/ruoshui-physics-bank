# HFDPO-07015

A biomedical engineer is designing a synthetic artery graft for patients with peripheral artery disease. The graft must withstand pulsatile blood pressure (120/80 mmHg) over decades without failing. The target compliance (change in volume per unit pressure) must match native tissue, but current materials either over-expand (rupturing at 300 mmHg) or under-comply, leading to thrombosis. Given the graft’s cylindrical geometry (radius 5 mm, wall thickness 0.3 mm), Young’s modulus of 1.2 MPa, and Poisson’s ratio 0.45, determine the required pressure threshold for structural failure via elastic buckling instability. Assess whether a slight reduction in wall thickness to 0.25 mm improves compliance while remaining safe under physiological loads.
