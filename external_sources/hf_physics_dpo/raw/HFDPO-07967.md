# HFDPO-07967

During a Mars sample-return mission, a lander must deploy a robotic drill to extract core samples from beneath the polar ice cap. The drill’s anchor system relies on a counterweight suspended via a cable over a pulley at the lander’s edge. Due to a software glitch, the counterweight is accidentally released from rest at a height of 2.5 m above the surface, but the pulley’s rotational inertia and a 12% energy loss during cable spooling due to friction must be accounted for. Given that the lander’s surface gravity is 3.72 m/s², the counterweight mass is 8.0 kg, and the pulley has a radius of 0.15 m and mass 1.2 kg, determine how fast the counterweight strikes the surface.
