# HFDPO-10151

A lunar research station detects unexpected signal interference in its deep-space communication array, operating at 18.5 GHz. The interference pattern matches Earth-based radar pulses, but the station is 384,000 km from Earth. Analysis reveals that the signal reflects off a cloud of charged dust particles near the Moon’s terminator, creating a standing wave pattern in the uplink channel. The dust cloud has a varying electron density, forming a plasma layer with a plasma frequency that shifts between 15.2 GHz and 17.8 GHz due to solar wind variations. What is the minimum thickness of the dust cloud required to produce a standing wave node at the receiver, assuming the wave reflects with a 180° phase shift on the plasma boundary and the wave travels through the medium at 0.95c?
