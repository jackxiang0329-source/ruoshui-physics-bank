# HFDPO-11351

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system sustains microbial ecosystems using natural electric currents generated by temperature-driven ion gradients across mineral-rich rock barriers. Engineers plan to harness this geoelectric potential by placing conductive electrodes into the vent fluid (resistivity ≈ 1.8 Ω·m) at a depth of 2.5 km, where the temperature difference between vent fluid (350°C) and surrounding seawater (2°C) creates a stable voltage of 32 V across a 50 cm thick mineral layer. Calculate the maximum power extractable if the electrode area is 0.25 m² and contact resistance is negligible, then analyze how the system’s efficiency changes if the conductive pathway develops microfractures, increasing effective resistivity by 30%.
