# HFDPO-01044

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole with a spiral coil of superconducting wire (radius 2.5 m, 1000 turns) suspended in a 300°C, conductive basalt layer. The natural geothermal convection creates a time-varying magnetic field described by B(t) = B₀ sin(ωt), where B₀ = 0.15 T and ω = 2π × 0.01 s⁻¹. The coil’s resistance is negligible, but the surrounding rock’s thermal expansion induces a slow, linear rise in the coil’s effective radius at 0.002 m/s. What is the peak induced current in the coil over the first 10 minutes, and how does the changing radius affect the efficiency of energy extraction?
