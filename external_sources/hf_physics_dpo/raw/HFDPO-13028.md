# HFDPO-13028

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a network of buried copper cables (100 m long, 2 cm diameter) to transmit electricity from a deep underground heat exchanger to the surface grid. The cables operate at 15 kV and carry 120 A, but over time, the insulation degrades due to thermal cycling, increasing the effective resistance. Measurements show a 5% voltage drop across the cables. If the ambient ground temperature is 5°C and the operating temperature of the cable reaches 60°C, what is the actual resistivity of the copper at operating temperature, and how much power is lost as heat in the cable? Assume the resistivity of copper at 20°C is 1.68 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m with a temperature coefficient of 0.0039/°C.
