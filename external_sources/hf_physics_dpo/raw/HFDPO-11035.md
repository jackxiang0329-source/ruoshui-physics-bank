# HFDPO-11035

A Mars rover’s front wheel becomes temporarily lodged in a fine, cohesive regolith simulant during a dust storm, with the wheel's tread partially buried. The static friction coefficient between wheel and soil is μ_s = 0.4, but the soil exhibits drag forces proportional to velocity squared due to particle entrapment and airflow. The wheel has a radius of 0.25 m and a mass of 4.5 kg, and the rover’s motor applies a torque of 18 N·m. Determine whether the wheel can overcome the combined static friction and dynamic drag to begin rotating, assuming the effective drag force at initial motion is 22 N and increases nonlinearly with angular velocity.
