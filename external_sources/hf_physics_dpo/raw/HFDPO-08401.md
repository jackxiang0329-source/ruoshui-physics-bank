# HFDPO-08401

A lunar greenhouse on Mars uses a transparent dome to trap solar heat, but thermal runaway threatens crop viability. The dome, made of a composite material with varying thermal conductivity, absorbs 85% of incident sunlight (1400 W/m²), while emitting infrared radiation through a selective coating that reduces effective emissivity to 0.25. The interior air temperature must be maintained at 25°C, but with a 20% increase in solar flux due to a dust storm clearing, the system's passive cooling via convection (h = 5 W/m²·K) and radiative loss is insufficient. Determine whether the dome’s surface temperature will exceed 50°C—risking structural warping—under transient conditions, assuming the dome’s thermal mass is 3000 J/K·m².
