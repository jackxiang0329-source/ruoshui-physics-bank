# HFDPO-02850

A space-based quantum communication satellite orbiting Earth at 750 km altitude must maintain a stable entangled photon link with a ground station during a geomagnetic storm. The storm induces a time-varying magnetic field of 120 nT over 20 ms, with a spatial gradient of 3.5 nT/km across the satellite’s 2.8 m antenna array. The satellite’s superconducting loop (radius 0.6 m, inductance 15 μH) is designed to detect field changes via Faraday’s law, but phase drift in the quantum signal threatens the link. What is the maximum rate of change of magnetic flux (dΦ/dt) the system can tolerate before quantum decoherence exceeds 10%, and how does this constrain the design of the magnetic shielding and feedback control system?
