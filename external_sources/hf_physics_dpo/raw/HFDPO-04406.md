# HFDPO-04406

During a high-altitude research expedition, a team deploys a network of temperature sensors powered by solar-charged lithium-ion batteries. Each sensor draws 12 mA at 3.3 V, but the wiring runs 1.8 km through frozen permafrost, using copper cables with a diameter of 1.2 mm. The resistivity of the cable increases to 2.5 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m due to extreme cold. A voltage drop of more than 0.2 V at the farthest sensor causes data loss. What is the minimum battery voltage required to ensure reliable operation, and how does the current distribution change if two sensors are placed in parallel at the end of the line?
