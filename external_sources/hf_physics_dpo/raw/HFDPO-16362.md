# HFDPO-16362

A research team is developing a wearable sensor patch for monitoring muscle fatigue in elite athletes during training. The patch uses a network of micro-scale strain gauges connected in a complex DC circuit with variable resistors and a single 5 V power source. During field tests, the signal output fluctuates unpredictably due to temperature drift in the strain gauge materials (which change resistance by 0.5% per °C). The circuit includes three parallel branches: one with a fixed 100 Ω resistor, another with a variable 200 Ω potentiometer, and a third with a strain gauge that varies from 150 Ω to 200 Ω as muscle tension increases. The team needs to recalibrate the voltage readout at room temperature (25°C) so that the total current remains stable within ±1% despite temperature-induced resistance shifts. What value should the potentiometer be set to, and how does this configuration minimize signal drift?
