# HFDPO-03850

A quantum computing research team observes unexpected decoherence in a fluxonium qubit cooled to 10 mK in a dilution refrigerator. The qubit’s Josephson junction exhibits a critical current that fluctuates by up to 12% over 24 hours, despite stable external magnetic fields. The device uses a thin-film niobium (Nb) Josephson junction with a nominal width of 150 nm, and the junction’s I_cR_n product is measured at 5.7 mV. However, noise spectroscopy reveals 1/f-like fluctuations in the Josephson phase, strongly correlated with local magnetic field variations from an unknown source. The team suspects vortex dynamics in the superconducting leads, but cannot rule out trapped magnetic flux in the Nb film or interface-related disorder. What mechanism is most likely responsible for the observed decoherence, and how would you experimentally distinguish between vortex motion in the leads versus flux pinning in the junction itself?
