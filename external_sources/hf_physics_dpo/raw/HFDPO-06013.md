# HFDPO-06013

A lunar mining colony plans to deploy a tethered satellite system to stabilize its orbit around a small, irregularly shaped asteroid (mass ≈ 5.2×10¹⁰ kg, radius ≈ 850 m) for continuous resource monitoring. The tether, 1.2 km long, connects two masses: a 120-kg sensor pod at the far end and a 180-kg counterweight closer to the asteroid. Due to the asteroid’s non-uniform density, gravitational acceleration at the tether’s anchor point varies by up to 18% across its surface. Calculate the tension difference between the tether’s ends when the system is in a stable, slow-rotating configuration where the center of mass orbits at 1.1 km from the asteroid’s center, assuming negligible external forces.
