# HFDPO-08564

A quantum computing team at a research lab is troubleshooting decoherence in a superconducting qubit fabricated from a thin niobium film. The qubit’s coherence time drops significantly when the device is cooled below 1.5 K, despite the material’s known superconducting gap of 1.5 meV. Measurements reveal that quasiparticle excitations—broken Cooper pairs—persist in the system longer than expected. Given that the quasiparticle relaxation rate is governed by phonon-assisted scattering and the dominant phonon energy near 2 K is ~1.2 meV, what is the minimum temperature below which quasiparticle density will sharply decrease due to suppressed thermal excitation, and why does the coherence time plateau instead of improving further?
