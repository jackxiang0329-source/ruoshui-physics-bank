# HFDPO-01833

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses parabolic troughs to heat synthetic oil to 390°C, which then transfers heat to a molten salt storage system. During a sudden cloud cover event, the input solar flux drops to 20% of its peak value over 15 minutes. The storage tank holds 120,000 kg of molten salt (specific heat 1400 J/kg·K, initial temp 350°C). Engineers must predict whether the salt temperature will remain above 320°C to maintain plant efficiency—considering heat loss to ambient (25°C) via radiation and convection. Use a lumped capacitance model with a time-varying heat input and a convective-radiative loss coefficient of 1.8 W/m²·K² (from empirical data).
