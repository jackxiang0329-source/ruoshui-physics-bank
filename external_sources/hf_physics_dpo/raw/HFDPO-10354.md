# HFDPO-10354

A biomedical engineer is designing a neural interface chip to detect weak brain signals from a grid of microelectrodes implanted in cortical tissue. Each electrode has a 5 μm diameter and is coated with a 100 nm thick dielectric layer (ε_r = 4.5) to prevent charge leakage and corrosion. The surrounding cerebrospinal fluid has a conductivity of 1.2 S/m and a relative permittivity of 78. When the brain’s electrical activity generates a transient 10 mV potential across the electrode-dielectric-fluid interface, the system must respond within 100 μs without signal distortion. Determine the effective capacitance per electrode and the time constant of the RC circuit formed, explaining why the dielectric thickness is critical in balancing sensitivity and response speed.
