# HFDPO-00302

A lunar research station uses a phased array antenna to transmit high-bandwidth data to Earth, but signal degradation occurs during lunar dusk due to ionospheric distortion caused by solar wind interactions with the Moon’s weak magnetic field. The 10 GHz signal experiences frequency-dependent phase shifts as it passes through a plasma layer with a peak electron density of 1.2 × 10¹⁰ m⁻³ and a scale height of 15 km. Determine the minimum number of antenna elements needed in a linear array (spaced λ/2 apart) to maintain beam steering accuracy within ±1.5° at the edge of the usable bandwidth (9.8–10.2 GHz), assuming the array must compensate for both geometric and plasma-induced phase errors.
