# HFDPO-09857

During a lunar mining operation, an electrostatic dust collector uses a hollow, spherical conducting shell (radius 1.2 m) to trap fine regolith particles charged by solar wind. The shell is isolated and carries a total charge of +4.5 μC. A small probe measures the electric field just outside the shell’s surface as 2.8 × 10⁴ N/C, but measurements inside the shell’s cavity show no field. Engineers suspect a flaw: a hidden, non-spherical charge distribution inside the cavity might be interfering with dust capture efficiency. Verify whether the measured field is consistent with Gauss’s Law and determine if the internal charge distribution must be spherically symmetric to produce this result.
