# HFDPO-00800

A Mars rover’s suspension system uses a spring-damper mechanism to maintain wheel contact on rugged terrain, but during a descent on a 25° incline, engineers observe oscillations that risk wheel lift-off. The wheel (mass = 12 kg) is modeled as a rigid disc with radius 0.3 m, connected via a spring (k = 3500 N/m) and damper (c = 200 N·s/m) to the chassis. The rover’s front wheel encounters a 0.15 m step. Determine the minimum damping coefficient required to prevent the wheel from losing contact with the ground after impact, assuming the step impact is instantaneous and the system is initially at rest. The coefficient of static friction is 0.6, and gravity on Mars is 3.7 m/s².
