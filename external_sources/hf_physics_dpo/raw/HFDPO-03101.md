# HFDPO-03101

During a solar storm, a geomagnetic disturbance induces charge densities in Earth’s upper ionosphere, creating a localized electric field that disrupts satellite communications. Scientists observe a spherical region of charged plasma at 400 km altitude with a non-uniform charge density ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 − r/R), where r is radial distance from the center, R = 20 km, and ρ₀ = 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ C/m³. Using Gauss’s Law, determine the electric field magnitude at 30 km and 50 km from the center, and explain how the field direction and symmetry assumptions affect the result.
