# HFDPO-01631

A solar-powered drone designed for high-altitude atmospheric research must maintain stable hovering at 20,000 meters, where air density is 0.08 kg/m³ and wind gusts can reach 50 m/s. The drone’s 8-meter-wide, 2.5-meter-tall solar wing generates 1200 N of lift but is subject to unpredictable aerodynamic moments. To prevent tip-over during gusts, the center of mass must be positioned within a 1.2-meter lateral tolerance from the wing’s centerline. Given the wing’s weight (350 N) and the payload (800 N), determine the required mass distribution in the tail assembly to maintain equilibrium under a 50 m/s gust that exerts a 600 N force at the wing’s leading edge, 0.8 m above the centerline. Assume no slip at the hinge joint.
