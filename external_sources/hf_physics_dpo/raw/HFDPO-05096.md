# HFDPO-05096

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole to circulate fluid through fractured rock at 4.5 km depth, where temperatures exceed 300°C. The fluid, a specialized nanofluid with density 1,150 kg/m³ and dynamic viscosity 0.0025 Pa·s, flows upward at 0.8 m/s through a 20 cm diameter steel pipe. Due to high internal pressure, a 3 mm thick layer of lubricating graphite slurry forms on the pipe wall. However, during a test, engineers observe a 12% drop in flow rate—despite unchanged pump power. Estimate the effective coefficient of dynamic friction between the fluid and scaled-up roughness defects (modeled as 15 μm-high asperities) that may have developed on the graphite layer. Assume laminar flow and use the Darcy-Weisbach equation with friction factor from Moody chart approximations.
