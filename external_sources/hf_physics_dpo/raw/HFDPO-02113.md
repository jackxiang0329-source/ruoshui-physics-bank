# HFDPO-02113

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a liquid lithium blanket to absorb neutrons and breed tritium, but during a sudden power fluctuation, the blanket cools rapidly from 600°C to 300°C in 4 seconds. At 400°C, lithium undergoes a solid-phase transition to a denser crystalline form, releasing latent heat. If the blanket’s initial state is fully liquid and the heat transfer rate is limited by conduction through the vessel wall (thermal conductivity 15 W/m·K, thickness 1 cm), will the phase transition be completed before the system reaches mechanical instability due to uncontrolled solidification stress?
