# HFDPO-16298

A coastal engineering team is designing a new breakwater to protect a harbor from destructive storm waves. Historical data shows that during major storms, waves with a wavelength of 45 m and height of 6.2 m approach the shore at a speed of 18 m/s. The breakwater must be tuned to interfere destructively with these waves at the harbor entrance, but due to seabed topography, wave speed varies with depth. Using measured wave dispersion data, the team models phase velocity as v_p = sqrt{frac{glambda}{2pi} tanhleft(frac{2pi d}{lambda}right)} , where d is water depth. At the entrance, depth is 12 m. Determine the required width of the breakwater gap to achieve maximum wave cancellation via destructive interference between reflected and incident waves, assuming the breakwater reflects waves with a 180° phase shift.
