# HFDPO-12822

A quantum imaging lab attempts to enhance resolution in biological fluorescence microscopy beyond the diffraction limit using a nonlinear plasmonic metasurface that generates second-harmonic emission. The device operates at 800 nm excitation, and the target structure—a protein complex—emits at 400 nm with a 30 nm full width at half maximum. However, background noise from spontaneous emission and scattered light exceeds the signal by a factor of 2.5. To improve detection, they consider replacing the standard photomultiplier tube with a superconducting nanowire single-photon detector (SNSPD) with 90% detection efficiency and a 10 ps timing jitter. Estimate the minimum achievable signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) improvement and assess whether the SNSPD upgrade alone can resolve the structure under realistic experimental constraints.
