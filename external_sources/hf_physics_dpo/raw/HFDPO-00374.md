# HFDPO-00374

During a deep-sea exploration, a research team discovers a hydrothermal vent system where supercritical CO₂ flows at 350°C and 75 MPa—conditions near the critical point of carbon dioxide (T_c = 304 K, P_c = 7.38 MPa). As the fluid ascends through a 1.2 km chimney, pressure drops rapidly, and the plume begins to phase-separate. The team observes a sudden formation of dense, high-viscosity CO₂-rich droplets that clog sampling instruments. Using thermodynamic data and the Clausius–Clapeyron equation, determine whether a liquid–vapor phase separation will occur during ascent, and explain why the droplets form despite the fluid initially being supercritical.
