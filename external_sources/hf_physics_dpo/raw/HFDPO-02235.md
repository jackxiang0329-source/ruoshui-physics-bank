# HFDPO-02235

A quantum dot solar cell is designed to absorb photons with wavelengths near 600 nm, but experimental data shows unexpected efficiency drops when the light intensity is reduced below 100 W/m². Engineers suspect the issue stems from the wave-particle duality of light: at low intensities, individual photon arrival becomes probabilistic, and the quantum efficiency depends on whether the dot can absorb a discrete photon or if the wave-like interference enhances absorption. What must the energy spacing between quantum states be to ensure a single photon (600 nm) can excite an electron, and how does the probabilistic nature of photon detection affect the cell’s response time under low-light conditions?
