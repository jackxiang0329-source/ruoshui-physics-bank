# HFDPO-00049

A fusion reactor design team faces a critical dilemma: tritium breeding efficiency in a lithium blanket must exceed 1.2 per neutron to sustain operation, but neutron flux measurements from the center of the blanket show a 15% drop over 18 months due to neutron absorption by structural materials. Given a neutron energy spectrum peaking at 14 MeV (typical of D-T fusion), a blanket thickness of 0.8 m, and a lithium-6 concentration of 60%, what is the minimum shielding thickness (in meters) of a boron-doped steel alloy (with neutron absorption cross-section σ = 3000 barns at 14 MeV) needed to prevent tritium production from being compromised by parasitic neutron loss?
