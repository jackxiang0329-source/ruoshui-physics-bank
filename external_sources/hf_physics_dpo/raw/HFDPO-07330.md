# HFDPO-07330

A team of climate scientists discovers that under the Antarctic ice sheet, subglacial lakes are periodically erupting into massive, transient water plumes—some rising over 1 km—before freezing again within days. Measurements show the water at the base of the ice sheet is at 273.2 K, just above freezing, but under high pressure (50 MPa). The ice above is thermally insulated, with a thermal conductivity of 2.2 W/m·K. If the initial plume forms when a 100 m³ volume of superheated water (273.5 K) rises into a cold ice layer, how long will it take for the plume to refreeze completely, and what does this imply about the stability of subglacial hydrological systems?
