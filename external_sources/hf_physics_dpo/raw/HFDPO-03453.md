# HFDPO-03453

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent emits superheated fluid at 400°C into the surrounding 2°C ocean water. The vent's chimney structure is a porous, thermally conductive mineral matrix with an effective thermal conductivity of 1.2 W/m·K and a cross-sectional area of 0.8 m². Over time, a stable thermal gradient forms, but the vent’s efficiency in transferring energy to the environment is dropping. Engineers estimate that 65% of the heat transfer is via conduction through the matrix, while 35% is lost to turbulent mixing. If the observed heat flux at the vent’s outer surface is 2.1 × 10⁶ W, determine the thickness of the mineral layer required to sustain this flux, and explain why the system might be approaching a thermodynamic equilibrium state despite continuous fluid emission.
