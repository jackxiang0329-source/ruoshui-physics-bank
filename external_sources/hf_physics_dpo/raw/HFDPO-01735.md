# HFDPO-01735

In a dusty desert region, a solar farm uses vertically aligned photovoltaic panels spaced 2.5 m apart. During sandstorms, fine silica particles (mean diameter 15 μm, density 2200 kg/m³) become electrostatically charged via triboelectric contact with the panels, accumulating on their surfaces and reducing efficiency. Measurements show charge densities up to 80 nC/m² on the panel surfaces. Calculate the minimum vertical electric field strength required to lift a typical particle (mass = 5.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ kg) from the panel surface against gravity, assuming the particle carries a charge equivalent to 100 electron charges.
