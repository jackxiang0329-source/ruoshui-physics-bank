# HFDPO-04913

A deep-sea mining vehicle descends into the Pacific Ocean, collecting polymetallic nodules from the seafloor at 4,500 meters depth. The vehicle's buoyancy compensator uses a sealed, flexible bladder filled with seawater. As it ascends, the bladder must expel water to maintain neutral buoyancy—otherwise, the vehicle will rise too quickly. If the bladder initially contains 0.8 m³ of seawater at 4,500 m (where pressure is ~45.5 MPa), and the vehicle ascends to 1,000 m (pressure ~10.1 MPa), how much volume of water must be expelled to maintain constant internal pressure and prevent uncontrolled ascent? Assume seawater is incompressible and the bladder adjusts instantly.
