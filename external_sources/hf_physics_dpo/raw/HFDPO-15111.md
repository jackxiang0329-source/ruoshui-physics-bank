# HFDPO-15111

A deep-space probe near Europa detects periodic bursts of microwave radiation (λ = 1.25 cm) originating from a subsurface ocean beneath the icy crust. The signal strength varies sinusoidally with a period of 3.6 hours, suggesting a rotating dipole source. However, the signal is attenuated by 60% upon exiting the ice shell. Given the ice’s complex permittivity (ε_r = 3.5 − j0.2) and assumed thickness of 12 km, determine the minimum dipole moment required to produce the observed signal at the surface, and explain how the imaginary part of ε_r influences signal coherence.
