# HFDPO-08371

A solar-powered thermal storage system in a remote desert village uses a large concrete wall (thermal mass) to absorb heat during the day and release it at night. The wall is 2 meters high, 10 meters long, and 0.5 meters thick, with initial uniform temperature of 25°C. During peak sunlight, the wall's surface is heated to 80°C and maintained for 6 hours. Assuming negligible heat loss through the sides and back, and a thermal diffusivity of 0.6 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s, determine the temperature at the center of the wall after 6 hours of heating, considering transient conduction with time-varying boundary conditions.
