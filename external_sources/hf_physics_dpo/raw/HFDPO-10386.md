# HFDPO-10386

A deep-sea mining robot descends to 3,000 meters below the ocean surface to extract polymetallic nodules. The robot's descent is controlled by a winch system that must counteract both gravity and hydrostatic pressure-induced drag, which increases nonlinearly with depth. The robot has a mass of 1,200 kg and a drag coefficient of 0.85, with a projected area of 0.6 m². The density of seawater is 1,025 kg/m³. The winch applies a variable tension force to ensure a safe descent velocity of 0.5 m/s. Calculate the total work done *by the winch* during descent, considering both gravitational potential energy change and energy dissipated by drag.
