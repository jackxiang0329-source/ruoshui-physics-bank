# HFDPO-12162

A submersible diving near a hydrothermal vent encounters a pocket of superheated water (350°C) trapped beneath a rigid, permeable rock layer. The denser cool seawater (4°C) above exerts a pressure of 12 MPa at the interface. The superheated water is confined in a 500 m³ chamber, and the rock layer has an average pore size of 200 µm. If the density of the hot water is 700 kg/m³ and the cold seawater is 1025 kg/m³, what is the minimum thickness the rock layer must have to prevent the hot water from rising, assuming capillary forces stabilize the interface against displacement?
