# HFDPO-07596

A biomedical engineer is designing an implantable cardiac pacemaker that uses a tiny inductor-capacitor (LC) resonant circuit to wirelessly recharge via an external coil. The implant's internal LC tank must resonate at exactly 13.56 MHz (a standard ISM band frequency), but due to tissue-induced capacitance and manufacturing tolerances, the actual inductance is 2.15 µH with a ±5% variation, and the desired capacitance is 68 pF—but tissue coupling alters the effective capacitance by up to 15%. What range of capacitance values must the system accommodate to maintain resonance within ±1% of 13.56 MHz across all expected variations, and how could variable capacitance be implemented in a miniaturized implant?
