# HFDPO-13731

A wind farm in a coastal desert faces unexpected performance drops during early morning hours. Turbine blades rotate efficiently at midday but stall as dawn breaks, despite stable wind speeds. Measurements show the air temperature near the surface drops by 12°C overnight, creating a stable 30-meter-thick nocturnal boundary layer with a temperature inversion. The turbines operate at 80 m above ground, where wind speed is 14 m/s, but the flow exhibits a 15° directional shear over vertical distance. Use the Prandtl one-seventh power law and the vorticity equation to predict the change in blade loading due to rotational acceleration from the shear, and determine whether the observed stalling is due to dynamic stall or structural resonance induced by the Coriolis effect in the rotating frame.
