# HFDPO-00561

A Mars rover’s rear wheel, designed with hexagonal treads for traction, begins slipping during a sand dune ascent despite high torque. The terrain has a static friction coefficient of 0.65 and a dynamic coefficient of 0.45 with the regolith. The wheel experiences aerodynamic drag as Martian atmospheric density varies with altitude. At 1.5 km elevation, the drag force is 42 N at 0.9 m/s, and the drag coefficient is 1.1. If the rover’s motor delivers 380 N of tangential force at the wheel, determine whether the wheel will slip or grip, and explain how the drag force affects the effective normal force and friction available.
