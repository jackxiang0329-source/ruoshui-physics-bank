# HFDPO-02032

In a quantum imaging lab, researchers use a single-photon interference setup to detect sub-wavelength features in biological nanostructures. Due to fabrication imperfections, the grating exhibits a random phase disorder with a coherence length of just 1.2 μm. The team observes a blurred interference pattern — the expected fringes are washed out. To recover the spatial coherence for high-resolution imaging, they propose embedding the grating in a liquid crystal layer whose refractive index can be tuned via electric field. What electric field strength (in V/m) is needed to restore full interference visibility, assuming the liquid crystal has a birefringence Δn = 0.2 and a linear electro-optic coefficient r₃₃ = 5 pm/V, and that the grating is 10 μm thick?
