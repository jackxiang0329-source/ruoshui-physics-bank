# HFDPO-03710

In a fusion reactor design study, researchers model the plasma confinement in a tokamak using kinetic theory to estimate the mean free path of deuterium ions at 100 million K. The magnetic field confines the plasma in a toroidal chamber with a density of 1.5 × 10²⁰ m⁻³ and an effective collision cross-section of 5 × 10⁻²⁰ m². The team observes unexpected energy loss and suspects that particle collisions are more frequent than predicted by ideal gas assumptions. Evaluate whether the mean free path is consistent with the reactor’s confinement time of 1 second, and determine if classical kinetic theory underestimates transport losses due to plasma non-equilibrium effects.
