# HFDPO-08732

A biomedical engineer is designing a neural interface for a brain-computer implant that uses microelectrodes coated with a dielectric layer to prevent tissue damage from charge buildup. The electrode array operates in cerebrospinal fluid with a relative permittivity of 80 and must maintain low impedance while minimizing capacitive charging time during neural signal detection. Each electrode has a circular footprint of 5 μm radius, and the dielectric is 100 nm thick with a relative permittivity of 3.5. When a 0.5 V step signal is applied, the engineer must determine the total charge stored and the time constant if the surrounding tissue has a resistivity of 50 Ω·m. The system must charge within 10 μs to capture high-frequency neural spikes. Is the design feasible?
