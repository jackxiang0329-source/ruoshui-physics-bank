# HFDPO-04057

A remote solar-powered research station in Antarctica relies on a Stirling engine to generate electricity during the 24-hour polar night. The engine operates between a hot reservoir maintained at 550 K by concentrated sunlight stored in thermal oil and a cold reservoir at 250 K (ambient air). During a week-long power outage, the station’s backup generator fails, forcing engineers to reduce the input heat rate to 1.2 kW to avoid overheating the thermal oil. The team notices the engine’s output power has dropped below target—only 0.35 kW—despite maintaining the same temperature extremes. What could explain the drop in efficiency, and what is the actual efficiency compared to the theoretical maximum?
