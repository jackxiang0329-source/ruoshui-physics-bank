# HFDPO-05368

A new quantum sensor designed for detecting dark matter candidates relies on a defect-rich diamond crystal with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers operating at cryogenic temperatures (~4 K). The sensor’s sensitivity depends on the coherence time of the NV spin states, which is limited by hyperfine coupling to nearby ^{13}C nuclear spins (0.1% natural abundance). Engineers observe a coherence time of only 2.3 ms, significantly below the theoretical limit of 10 ms in ideal conditions. How can the coherence time be restored to near the theoretical maximum, and what physical principle must be leveraged to suppress the dominant decoherence mechanism?
