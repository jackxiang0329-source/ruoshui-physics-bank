# HFDPO-01508

In a cutting-edge neuromorphic computing chip, a dense array of 10,000 artificial synapses—each modeled as a tunable memristor—is arranged in a 100×100 grid. Each memristor’s resistance changes based on the cumulative charge passed through it, following a nonlinear, history-dependent relationship: R = R₀ exp(−kQ), where Q is the total charge (in coulombs) and k = 0.05 C⁻¹. During a learning cycle, a 100 ms pulse of 1.5 μA current is applied to each synapse. If the initial resistance R₀ = 10 kΩ, what is the average final resistance across all synapses, assuming uniform charge delivery and no leakage? How does this outcome affect the chip’s energy efficiency when scaling to 1 million synapses?
