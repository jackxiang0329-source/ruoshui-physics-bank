# HFDPO-06246

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters unexpected turbulence near a hydrothermal vent field. The vent emits superheated fluid at 350°C into cold ocean water (2°C) at a rate of 120 kg/s, forming a complex, buoyant plume that disrupts the ROV’s sensors. The plume rises due to thermal buoyancy but begins to deflect laterally due to ambient ocean currents of 0.8 m/s. Assuming the plume maintains a steady axisymmetric shape with an initial diameter of 1.5 m at the vent and undergoes a transition to turbulent mixing, estimate the radial distance from the vent axis at which the plume’s centerline velocity drops to 10% of its initial value, given a bulk Richardson number of 0.7 and a kinematic viscosity of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s.
