# HFDPO-10024

In a cutting-edge quantum imaging lab, researchers use entangled photon pairs generated via spontaneous parametric down-conversion to probe the chiral structure of a newly synthesized biomolecular crystal. One beam passes through the crystal, which induces a differential phase shift between left- and right-circular polarization states due to its handedness. The other beam bypasses the sample. When the two beams recombine at a polarization-sensitive interferometer, the interference pattern reveals a fringe shift of 1.8 radians. Given that the crystal thickness is 150 μm and the wavelength in vacuum is 780 nm, determine the effective birefringence induced by the crystal’s chirality and explain how this measurement could be used to quantify molecular asymmetry.
