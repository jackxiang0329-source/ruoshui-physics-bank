# HFDPO-11248

In a remote Arctic research station, a solar panel array with a variable output (60–120 V) powers a critical climate monitoring system through a DC regulator. The system's load fluctuates between 50 W (low demand) and 250 W (high demand), but the station’s battery bank (24 V nominal) must remain stable. A custom DC-DC buck converter (75% efficiency) is used between the solar array and battery. During a prolonged polar night, solar input drops to minimum. The station’s engineers notice that when the load increases abruptly from 50 W to 250 W, the battery voltage sags by 4 V in 200 ms before regulation stabilizes. Using only the battery voltage, load current, and known regulator efficiency, determine the minimum capacitance required in the battery bank’s DC bus to prevent voltage sag below 22 V during a 250 W step load.
