# HFDPO-11168

A fusion reactor's magnetic confinement system uses high-temperature superconducting tapes (YBCO) cooled to 77 K, but during a sudden power surge, current density reaches 1.2 × 10¹⁰ A/m²—near the critical limit. The tapes are wound in a solenoid with 150 turns per meter, and the magnetic field at the center must remain stable at 12 T to confine plasma. If the material’s critical current density drops by 40% due to thermal fluctuations from the surge, will the superconductor quench? Justify using both the critical state model and the Bean model.
