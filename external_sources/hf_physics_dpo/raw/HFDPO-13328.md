# HFDPO-13328

A lunar research station detects faint, periodic microwave signals (14.5 GHz) arriving from a distant exoplanet system, but the signal strength fluctuates unpredictably—sometimes vanishing entirely for hours. The station's parabolic antenna, 12 meters in diameter, has a measured gain of 52 dBi and receives power at a rate of 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁸ W during peak transmission. Given that the signal originates from a 300-km-diameter spherical emitter drifting through interstellar plasma, and accounts for a 6.7 dB atmospheric attenuation due to ionospheric scintillation, what is the minimum isotropic radiated power required from the exoplanet source? Assume the distance is 4.2 light-years and the wave is propagating in a dispersive medium with a refractive index of 1.00012.
