# HFDPO-07472

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a massive reinforced concrete dome (radius 15 m, thickness 1.2 m) to contain high-pressure steam at 350°C. The dome is supported by 8 radial concrete pillars, each 3 m long and 0.8 m in diameter, anchored into basalt bedrock. Engineers observe micro-cracks forming at the base of several pillars during seasonal temperature shifts. Determine the maximum allowable temperature fluctuation (ΔT) the system can endure without exceeding the concrete's tensile strength of 3.5 MPa due to thermal stress, assuming the dome and pillars are constrained at the base and the dome behaves as a spherical shell under internal pressure.
