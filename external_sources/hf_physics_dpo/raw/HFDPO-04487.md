# HFDPO-04487

During a deep-space mission, a solar-powered rover on Mars encounters a critical issue: its primary power circuit, using a 12 V photovoltaic array, shows erratic behavior under Martian dust storms. The circuit’s load is a motor with variable resistance (5 Ω to 20 Ω) due to thermal drift and mechanical wear. Engineers on Earth detect a voltage drop from 12 V to 9.6 V across the load when the dust storm reduces sunlight by 40%. They suspect internal resistance in the power bus has increased due to microfractures in solder joints. Given the measured current (0.8 A) under storm conditions, determine the total internal resistance of the power bus and explain why the voltage drop suggests degradation rather than just reduced solar output.
