# HFDPO-04120

A deep-sea mining drone descends at constant speed through a dense, viscous ocean current, extracting polymetallic nodules from the seabed. As it harvests material, the drone’s mass increases at 1.2 kg/s, but its thrusters adjust to maintain a steady velocity of 0.8 m/s. Engineers must ensure the drone’s propulsion system doesn’t overheat—calculate the minimum average power output required from the thrusters to maintain momentum, accounting for the mass gain and energy dissipated by viscous drag. Assume the drag force is proportional to velocity squared, with a drag coefficient of 1.8 N·s²/m².
