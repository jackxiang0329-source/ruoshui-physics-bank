# HFDPO-00855

A spacecraft performing a gravity-assist maneuver around Venus must adjust its trajectory precisely to achieve optimal energy gain while avoiding atmospheric drag. The probe enters Venus’s gravitational influence at a speed of 28 km/s relative to Venus, at a periapsis distance of 6,500 km (just 100 km above the surface). With Venus’s mass at 4.87 × 10²⁴ kg and radius 6,050 km, determine the maximum possible change in velocity (Δv) the spacecraft can gain purely from gravity assist, assuming a perfectly elastic flyby and no propulsion. Account for the fact that Venus rotates at 6.5 km/s at its equator—could the spacecraft leverage this rotation to increase its Δv, and if so, how?
