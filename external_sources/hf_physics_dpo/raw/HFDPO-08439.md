# HFDPO-08439

In a microfluidic chip designed for early-stage cancer detection, a suspension of bioparticles (diameter 5 μm, density 1050 kg/m³) flows through a 100 μm-wide channel at 100 μL/min. The fluid (viscosity 3.5 mPa·s, density 1020 kg/m³) is driven by a pressure gradient, but particles begin to cluster near the channel walls after 2 mm of flow—disrupting detection. Is this clustering due to particle migration driven by viscous stresses, or could it be caused by shear-induced diffusion? Evaluate the relative importance of these mechanisms using the Péclet number and the Dean number, assuming the channel is straight but the flow develops over the first 2 mm.
