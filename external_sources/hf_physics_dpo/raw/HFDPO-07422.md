# HFDPO-07422

A solar farm in Arizona uses concentrated photovoltaic panels with lens arrays that focus sunlight onto tiny high-efficiency cells. Each lens has a diameter of 15 cm and is designed to focus light at a 30 cm distance. However, engineers notice a 12% drop in efficiency during midday—despite perfect alignment and clear skies. They suspect diffraction at the lens edges is spreading the focused spot beyond the cell’s sensitive area. Given sunlight’s average wavelength of 550 nm, calculate the minimum spot size the lens can achieve due to diffraction, and explain why this might cause the efficiency loss.
