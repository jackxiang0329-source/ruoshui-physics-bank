# HFDPO-05681

A high-efficiency perovskite-silicon tandem solar cell operates under 1 sun illumination (1000 W/m²) with a measured open-circuit voltage of 1.92 V and a fill factor of 83%. The current density is limited by the bottom silicon cell, which generates 28.5 mA/cm². However, during field testing, the output degrades by 15% after 1000 hours under continuous illumination and elevated temperature (85°C). Evaluate whether the primary loss mechanism is non-radiative recombination in the perovskite layer or interfacial defect states at the perovskite/silicon heterojunction. Justify your conclusion using the effective radiative recombination lifetime (τ_rad) derived from the measured VOC and the Shockley-Queisser limit for tandem cells.
