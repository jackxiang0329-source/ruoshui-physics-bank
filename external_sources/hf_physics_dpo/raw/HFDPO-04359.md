# HFDPO-04359

A biomedical engineer is designing a wearable cardiac monitor using impedance cardiography to detect early signs of edema. The device uses a 50 kHz AC current injected through electrodes on the chest, measuring tiny voltage changes across the thorax. Due to tissue heterogeneity and movement artifacts, the effective load behaves as a complex impedance with a variable resistive component (150–200 Ω) and a parasitic inductance (1.2 mH) in series with a capacitance (35 nF). The engineer must determine the resonant frequency of the body's impedance at rest and when edema increases tissue conductivity by 30%, altering the capacitance. What is the shift in resonant frequency, and how does it affect signal detection at 50 kHz?
