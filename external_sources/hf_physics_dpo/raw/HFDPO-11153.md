# HFDPO-11153

A fusion reactor prototype in Iceland uses a deuterium-tritium plasma confined in a tokamak with a magnetic field gradient that varies radially. At peak operation, the central plasma temperature reaches 15 keV, and the neutron emission rate is measured at 3.2 × 10¹⁹ neutrons per second. However, neutron detectors placed at 120° azimuthal angles show asymmetrical flux—45% higher on the starboard side. Assuming the neutron production is isotropic in the plasma frame but the plasma is rotating at 1.2 × 10⁶ m/s due to non-axisymmetric current drive, analyze the relativistic Doppler shift and magnetic mirror effects that could explain the observed asymmetry.
