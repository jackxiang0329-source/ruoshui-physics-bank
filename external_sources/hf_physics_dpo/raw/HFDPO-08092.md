# HFDPO-08092

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a complex array of toroidal magnetic coils to confine a plasma at 150 million Kelvin. Engineers detect unexpected electric field spikes near the inner wall, despite the plasma being nearly neutral overall. The vessel’s inner surface is made of a conductive material with a non-uniform surface charge density modeled as σ(φ) = σ₀cos(3φ), where φ is the toroidal angle. Using Gauss’s Law in integral form, determine the net charge enclosed within a Gaussian surface that is a thin toroidal shell centered on the vessel’s axis, and explain why field anomalies appear even though global neutrality is maintained.
