# HFDPO-03734

A team of physicists at a deep-space observatory detects a burst of high-energy gamma rays from a distant quasar, arriving in sync with a gravitational wave signal from a black hole merger. The gamma rays travel at nearly the speed of light, but due to relativistic time dilation in the source frame, they were emitted over a 50-second interval in the quasar’s rest frame. Observers on Earth measure the burst duration as 12.5 seconds. Calculate the Lorentz factor γ of the quasar relative to Earth and determine the relative velocity v as a fraction of c. Then, explain whether the observed Doppler shift in the gamma-ray peak frequency could distinguish between a source moving toward us at that speed versus one receding at the same speed, using only relativistic kinematics.
