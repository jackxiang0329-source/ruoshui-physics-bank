# HFDPO-00498

During a high-altitude expedition, a climber discovers a frozen lake with a 30 cm thick layer of ice, but notices a growing patch of dark water beneath it. The ice is at -5°C, and the surrounding air is -10°C. The climber wonders: could the ice be melting from below due to geothermal heat escaping through the lakebed? Given the ice’s thermal conductivity (2.2 W/m·K), the thermal conductivity of the lakebed rock (1.8 W/m·K), and the latent heat of fusion of ice (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg), estimate the minimum geothermal heat flux (in W/m²) needed to melt 1 mm of ice per hour from the bottom, and explain why the dark water might be spreading despite the cold air.
