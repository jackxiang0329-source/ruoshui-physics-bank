# HFDPO-05411

A team of engineers is designing a gravity-gradient stabilization system for a CubeSat in low Earth orbit (LEO), 620 km above the surface. The satellite is 10 cm wide, and its center of mass is offset from its center of geometry by 2 cm due to an asymmetric payload. To prevent tumbling, they rely on tidal forces from Earth’s gravity to align the satellite along the radial direction. However, recent simulations show instability when the satellite’s spin rate drops below 0.1 rad/s. Using only Newtonian gravitation, determine the minimum stable spin rate required for the satellite to maintain orientation, and explain why a slower spin leads to rotational instability despite the gradient being present.
