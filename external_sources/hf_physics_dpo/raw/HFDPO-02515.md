# HFDPO-02515

A quantum dot solar cell array uses a periodic array of nanoscale TiO₂ pillars (120 nm diameter, 300 nm height) spaced 600 nm apart to enhance light trapping via guided mode resonances. Measured reflectance dips at 580 nm and 640 nm reveal constructive interference in diffracted orders. However, when the device operates under high solar flux, efficiency drops by 18% due to thermal expansion of the substrate. Estimate the new effective grating period and determine whether the interference condition for the 580 nm peak shifts into or out of resonance, assuming a thermal expansion coefficient of 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹ and a 35°C temperature rise.
