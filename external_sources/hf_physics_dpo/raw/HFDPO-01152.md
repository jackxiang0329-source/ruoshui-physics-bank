# HFDPO-01152

A quantum dot array in a next-generation solar cell operates at 310 K, with 1200 electrons confined in a 2D lattice of 600 identical quantum dots, each capable of holding up to two electrons (spin-degenerate). Due to Coulomb repulsion, the average charge per dot is observed to be 1.6 electrons, though the system is not in thermal equilibrium with the lattice. Modeling the system using a generalized Fermi-Dirac distribution with an effective chemical potential that varies spatially, determine the spatial variation of the electron density that minimizes the total free energy under charge conservation and finite capacitance constraints.
