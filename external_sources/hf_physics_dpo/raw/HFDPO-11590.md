# HFDPO-11590

A fusion reactor's divertor plate, made of tungsten, is exposed to a localized heat flux of 15 MW/m² from plasma-facing ions during a transient edge-localized mode (ELM) event lasting 200 μs. The plate has a thickness of 5 mm and is cooled on the backside by liquid lithium flowing at 2 m/s with a bulk temperature of 400 K. Using thermal contact resistance data from experimental studies, determine the maximum temperature rise at the front surface, considering both transient conduction and the cooling capacity of the lithium. Would the temperature exceed tungsten’s melting point (3695 K), and if so, how would microstructural damage evolve over 100 such events?
