# HFDPO-15227

A fusion research team is designing a compact tokamak reactor where plasma confinement relies on a non-uniform magnetic field generated by current-carrying coils shaped like nested toroidal conductors. To ensure stability, engineers must calculate the electric field inside a hollow, concentric, conducting spherical shell surrounding the core, where the shell is held at a fixed potential. The shell has inner radius 2.0 m and outer radius 2.1 m, and carries a total charge of 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ C distributed non-uniformly such that the surface charge density varies as σ(θ) = σ₀ cosθ. What is the magnitude of the electric field at a point 2.05 m from the center, inside the shell, and how does Gauss’s Law help determine this despite the asymmetry?
