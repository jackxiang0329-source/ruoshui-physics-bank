# HFDPO-01125

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from blood using deterministic lateral displacement (DLD) in a channel with 50 μm-wide pillars arranged in a staggered array. Blood flows at 0.5 mL/min through a 100 μm-wide channel, and the CTCs (diameter ~15 μm) are significantly more viscous than plasma (μ_blood ≈ 3.5 mPa·s, μ_plasma ≈ 1.2 mPa·s). The device must ensure CTCs deviate from the main flow path by at least 10 μm over a 2 cm length to be captured. Determine whether the viscous forces alone can achieve this separation, considering the balance between inertial lift and viscous drag at low Reynolds numbers.
