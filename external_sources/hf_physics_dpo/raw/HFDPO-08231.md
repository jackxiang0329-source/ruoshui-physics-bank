# HFDPO-08231

A biomedical engineer is designing a handheld device to detect early-stage melanoma using laser-induced autofluorescence. The device uses a 405 nm laser to excite skin cells, and the emitted fluorescence (peaking at 520 nm) is collected through a fiber-optic probe. However, background scattering from dermal tissue causes a strong signal at 405 nm (Rayleigh scattering), overwhelming the weaker fluorescence. The engineer must calculate the required notch filter bandwidth to suppress the scattered laser light by 99.9% while preserving at least 80% of the 520 nm fluorescence signal. What must the filter’s stopband width be, and how does the choice of filter design (e.g., Fabry-Pérot vs. multilayer dielectric) influence system sensitivity?
