# HFDPO-07257

A deep-sea mining drone, designed to extract polymetallic nodules from the ocean floor at 5,000 meters depth, uses a rigid articulated arm with a gripper to collect samples. The arm consists of two segments: a 3.2 m lower arm (mass 280 kg, center of mass 1.4 m from pivot) and a 2.0 m upper arm (mass 190 kg, center of mass 0.9 m from joint), connected by a frictionless hinge. The gripper (mass 65 kg) is at the tip and exerts a 1,200 N downward force on a nodule. The entire system is supported by a single hydraulic actuator at the base, angled at 15° above horizontal, and the joint at the base must withstand both forces and moments. Determine the minimum required force the actuator must generate to maintain equilibrium, considering hydrostatic pressure effects (100 kPa at surface, increasing by 10 kPa per 10 m depth) on the arm’s surface area (effective projected area: 0.8 m²). Assume the actuator force acts at a perpendicular distance of 0.7 m from the base pivot.
