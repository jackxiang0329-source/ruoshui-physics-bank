# HFDPO-00022

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole filled with conductive brine to extract heat from subsurface rock, but engineers notice unexpected voltage fluctuations in the surrounding monitoring cables—despite no direct electrical connections. The 3.2 km-deep well is surrounded by a 500-turn helical sensor coil (radius 1.5 m) buried 10 m below the surface. During a sudden temperature spike, the geothermal fluid velocity increases from 0.3 m/s to 1.8 m/s over 12 seconds, inducing a changing magnetic field due to motion of the conductive fluid in Earth’s ambient magnetic field (55 μT, tilted 65° below horizontal). Calculate the average induced EMF in the sensor coil during this transient, and explain why the signal persists even when fluid motion stabilizes.
