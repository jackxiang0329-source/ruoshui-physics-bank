# HFDPO-16004

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses parabolic troughs to concentrate sunlight onto a central receiver tube filled with synthetic oil. On a clear summer day, the oil enters the tube at 300 K and exits at 450 K, flowing at 1.2 kg/s. The tubes are 10 m long, 0.05 m in diameter, and made of stainless steel with a thermal conductivity of 15 W/(m·K). The ambient air temperature is 310 K, and natural convection heat loss from the tube surface is estimated via a convective heat transfer coefficient of 12 W/(m²·K). Calculate the *net* heat gain per unit length of the tube, and determine whether the system is operating efficiently—specifically, what fraction of the incident solar energy is actually utilized for heating the oil (assume the concentration ratio delivers 800 W/m² of solar flux to the tube surface).
