# HFDPO-16186

A quantum computing chip based on topological qubits uses Majorana zero modes hosted at the ends of a spin-orbit-coupled semiconductor nanowire. The system operates at 10 mK, and the energy splitting between the two states (due to residual tunneling) is measured at 2.3 μeV. However, recent experimental data shows an unexpected 15% increase in decoherence rates when the device is exposed to a 50 mT magnetic field oriented at 30° to the wire axis. Explain whether this anomalous decoherence arises from the hybridization of Majorana modes with bulk electron states, or from a field-induced shift in the topological phase boundary. Evaluate the physical plausibility of each explanation given the measured tunneling energy and critical field strength required for topological protection.
