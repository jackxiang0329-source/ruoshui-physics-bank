# HFDPO-11915

In a next-generation quantum computing chip, superconducting qubits are coupled via a tunable capacitor made of two parallel aluminum nanodisks (radius 50 nm) separated by a 10 nm-thick aluminum oxide layer. The dielectric constant of the oxide varies locally due to nanoscale defects, creating a spatially inhomogeneous permittivity: κ(r) = κ₀(1 + 0.1sin(2πr/λ)), where κ₀ = 8, λ = 100 nm, and r is radial distance from the center. A control voltage applied to one disk induces a charge distribution that slightly modulates the local dielectric constant. What is the total capacitance between the disks under these conditions, and how does it differ from the uniform case?
