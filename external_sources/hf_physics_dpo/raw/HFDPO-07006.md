# HFDPO-07006

A quantum biologist observes that a certain species of butterfly exhibits iridescent wing patterns under sunlight, but only when viewed at specific angles—suggesting nanoscale diffraction structures. The wings contain periodic ridges with spacing ~450 nm, and the brightest reflection occurs at 58° from the normal when illuminated by green light (λ = 550 nm). However, when the butterfly rotates in flight, the color shifts toward blue. Using interference-diffraction principles, explain the angular dependence of the observed color shift and calculate the effective groove spacing that would produce the blue shift (λ = 470 nm) at the same viewing angle.
