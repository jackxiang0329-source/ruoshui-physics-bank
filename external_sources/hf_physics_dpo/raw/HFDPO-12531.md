# HFDPO-12531

A geothermal energy company is drilling into a deep crustal rock formation (10 km below surface) where temperatures exceed 400°C and confining pressures reach 300 MPa. The rock contains a network of natural fractures filled with high-pressure supercritical CO₂. During injection, the rock exhibits anisotropic elasticity—its Young’s modulus varies by 35% depending on orientation relative to the fracture planes. The drilling team observes microseismic events when injection pressure exceeds 220 MPa. What is the maximum sustainable injection rate (in m³/min) before inducing shear failure along the fractures, given that the rock’s shear modulus is 15 GPa and the fracture shear strength is 48 MPa? Assume the rock behaves as a linear elastic, transversely isotropic material and the injection creates a spherical pressure front propagating at 0.1 m/s.
