# HFDPO-05280

A biomedical engineer is designing a wearable neural interface that uses microelectrodes implanted in the cortex to record brain activity. Each electrode is coated with a biocompatible dielectric layer (εᵣ = 4.5, thickness = 100 nm) and has a circular footprint of 5 μm diameter. The surrounding cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) has a conductivity of 1.5 S/m and acts as the electrolytic counter-electrode. During operation, the system must resolve sub-microvolt signals at frequencies up to 1 kHz. However, parasitic capacitance between adjacent electrodes causes crosstalk, degrading signal fidelity. Estimate the mutual capacitance between two neighboring electrodes spaced 30 μm apart in a 2D array and determine how the effective RC time constant of the interface changes if the dielectric layer degrades (εᵣ → 3.0) due to long-term immersion in CSF.
