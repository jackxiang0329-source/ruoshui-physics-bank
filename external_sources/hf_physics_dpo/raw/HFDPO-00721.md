# HFDPO-00721

In a microfluidic device designed for single-cell isolation, a spherical cell with diameter 12 μm and density 1050 kg/m³ is suspended in a Newtonian fluid (viscosity 1.2 × 10⁻³ Pa·s) flowing through a channel with a rectangular cross-section (200 μm × 50 μm). The flow velocity profile develops into a fully developed laminar state with a maximum speed of 8 mm/s at the centerline. As the cell drifts toward the channel wall due to hydrodynamic lift forces, it approaches a 10° inclined ledge that could trap it. Determine the minimum distance from the wall at which the cell must be located to avoid being pulled into the ledge by the combined effects of viscous drag and pressure gradients, assuming the cell settles only if its net force toward the wall exceeds 2.5 × 10⁻¹² N.
