# HFDPO-01678

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a sudden, unexpected upward current near a hydrothermal vent field. The ROV's thrusters struggle to maintain position as turbulent eddies—driven by density gradients from superheated fluid (350°C, ρ = 930 kg/m³) mixing with cold seawater (4°C, ρ = 1025 kg/m³)—generate horizontal forces. At a depth of 2,500 m, the local buoyancy frequency is 0.015 rad/s, and the current velocity fluctuations reach up to 0.8 m/s over a 10 m length scale. Calculate the Reynolds number for the turbulent eddies and determine whether the flow remains laminar or transitions to turbulence, considering both viscous and inertial forces.
