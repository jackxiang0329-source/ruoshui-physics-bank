# HFDPO-07031

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses molten salt (specific heat 1500 J/kg·K, density 1800 kg/m³) stored in an insulated cylindrical tank to generate electricity during nighttime. The tank, 12 m tall and 6 m in diameter, is initially at 550°C after a full day of solar charging. At night, the salt is slowly withdrawn at 0.8 kg/s to transfer heat to a steam generator, causing its temperature to drop. The tank is not perfectly insulated—its outer surface loses heat to the environment (20°C) at a rate proportional to the temperature difference, with a heat transfer coefficient of 12 W/m²·K. The tank walls are 25 cm thick and made of stainless steel (k = 15 W/m·K). Determine the time it takes for the salt to cool from 550°C to 450°C, accounting for both the heat loss to the environment and the internal cooling due to fluid withdrawal.
