# HFDPO-10910

A deep-ocean geothermal vent near the Mid-Atlantic Ridge emits superheated brine at 350°C, but the surrounding seawater is near 2°C. Engineers propose a hybrid thermal engine using a closed-loop organic Rankine cycle with R-134a, placed in a submersible capsule that maintains internal stability. The system must extract maximum sustainable power while avoiding thermodynamic degradation from salt deposition and phase instability. The vent’s heat flux is limited to 1.2 MW due to environmental regulations, and the capsule’s heat exchanger has a maximum effective surface area of 0.8 m². What is the maximum theoretical power output if the engine operates with a Carnot efficiency between the vent and ambient ocean, and how does this compare to a practical organic Rankine cycle with 60% of Carnot efficiency and an actual heat transfer coefficient of 2,500 W/m²·K?
