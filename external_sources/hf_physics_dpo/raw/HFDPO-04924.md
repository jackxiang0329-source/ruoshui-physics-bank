# HFDPO-04924

A tidal energy converter in the Pentland Firth operates beneath a 15-meter-deep channel where the mean tidal current reaches 4.2 m/s. During peak flow, a vortex shed from a cylindrical turbine support structure induces oscillatory pressure fluctuations measured at 3.5 Hz. The turbine’s blade pitch is fixed, but the yaw mechanism can adjust in real time to reduce vortex-induced vibrations. Given that the structure has a diameter of 1.8 m and the fluid is seawater (ρ = 1025 kg/m³, ν = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s), determine the critical flow velocity at which vortex shedding becomes destabilizing, and evaluate whether a 10% reduction in yaw alignment tolerance (from ±2° to ±1.8°) increases the risk of resonance despite the control system’s adaptive response.
