# HFDPO-03711

A deep-space probe orbits a distant neutron star emitting intense gamma-ray bursts every 1.2 seconds, each lasting 30 ms and releasing 1.8 × 10³⁸ J of energy. The probe’s shield must withstand cumulative radiation damage from neutron-induced transmutations in its silicon-based electronics. Given a shielding thickness of 5 cm of tungsten, a neutron flux of 2.5 × 10¹⁰ n/cm²/s during bursts, and a silicon-28 cross-section of 2.4 barns for neutron capture, estimate the number of silicon atoms per cm³ that undergo (n,γ) reactions over 10 years of operation. Assume 100 bursts per day and that gamma rays cause negligible damage.
