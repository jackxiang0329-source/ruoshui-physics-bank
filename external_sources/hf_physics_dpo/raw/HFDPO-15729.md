# HFDPO-15729

A team at a quantum materials lab observes that a newly synthesized 2D transition-metal dichalcogenide (TMD) monolayer exhibits a sharp photoluminescence peak at 1.85 eV under low-temperature laser excitation, but the peak broadens significantly when the sample is exposed to ambient humidity. They suspect the broadening arises from electron-phonon coupling enhanced by water molecules adsorbing on the surface. Given that the linewidth is 45 meV and the lattice vibrational frequency is 120 meV, calculate the dimensionless electron-phonon coupling strength λ using the Fröhlich Hamiltonian, and analyze whether the system is in the weak- or strong-coupling regime. How might quantum tunneling of the adsorbed water molecules influence the observed optical response?
