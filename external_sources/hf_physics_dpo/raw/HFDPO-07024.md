# HFDPO-07024

A Mars habitat module, 4.2 meters in diameter and 7.5 m long, is delivered via aerobraking through the thin Martian atmosphere (density ≈ 0.020 kg/m³). During descent, it tumbles due to imperfect deployment of its parachutes, inducing unsteady drag. At peak atmospheric entry, it reaches 3.8 km/s with 18% of its surface experiencing turbulent boundary layers. Engineers must determine the peak deceleration (in g’s) to ensure structural integrity, but must account for both skin friction (coefficient μ_s ≈ 0.25 on ablation-resistant coating) and pressure drag from asymmetric flow. Assume the module's drag coefficient varies dynamically with angle of attack from 1.0 to 1.9 based on tilt. What is the minimum stable deceleration threshold needed to prevent structural failure, given a 1200 kg mass and maximum allowable stress of 8.5×10⁶ Pa?
