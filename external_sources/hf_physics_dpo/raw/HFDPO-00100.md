# HFDPO-00100

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a sudden turbulence event near a hydrothermal vent plume, where superheated fluid (600°C, density 750 kg/m³) jets upward at 3 m/s into colder seawater (10°C, density 1025 kg/m³). The plume develops a complex vortical structure due to Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities. The ROV’s sensors detect a 2.5 m diameter swirling region that destabilizes its navigation system. Estimate the minimum angular velocity of the vortex core necessary to stabilize the plume against viscous dissipation, assuming a kinematic viscosity of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s for the jet fluid and that stability requires a Rossby number < 0.5.
