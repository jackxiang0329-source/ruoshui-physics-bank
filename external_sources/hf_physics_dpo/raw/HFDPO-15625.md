# HFDPO-15625

In a deep-space quantum communication experiment, a probe traveling at 0.95c relative to Earth sends entangled photon pairs to a receiving station. Due to relativistic time dilation, the probe’s clock runs slow by a factor of ~3.2. The mission team on Earth must synchronize their detection window with the probe’s signal emission event—accounting for both the probe’s velocity and the finite speed of light—so they don’t miss the entangled photons. The probe emits a signal at its time t' = 5.0 seconds (in its frame), and the Earth station receives it at t = 18.3 seconds (Earth time). The probe is currently 4.0 light-seconds away from Earth, measured in the Earth frame. Evaluate whether the signal emission and reception events are causally connected, and determine if the synchronization strategy is feasible given the relativistic constraints.
