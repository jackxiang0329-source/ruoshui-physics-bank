# HFDPO-12331

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses naturally occurring potassium-40 in basalt rock to generate minor heat, but engineers notice a slight but persistent rise in background radiation near the drill site. Given that potassium-40 has a half-life of 1.25 billion years and decays via beta emission (90%) and electron capture (10%), with a total energy release of 1.31 MeV per decay, and that 1 kg of basalt contains 0.012% potassium by mass, estimate the radiation dose rate at 1 meter from a cubic meter of this rock. Assume the rock is a uniform source and ignore shielding.
