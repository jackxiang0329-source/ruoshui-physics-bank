# HFDPO-01656

During a lunar mission, a rover navigating a 15° inclined crater wall encounters a sudden shift in regolith cohesion due to seismic activity, causing its rear wheels to lose traction and begin sliding downward while the front wheels remain pinned by a rock formation. The rover (mass 220 kg) has a moment of inertia of 340 kg·m² about its center of mass, and the distance from the front axle to the center of mass is 1.1 m, while the rear axle is 1.3 m behind. Assuming the rear wheels (each with radius 0.3 m and mass 18 kg) roll without slipping initially but begin to slip with a kinetic coefficient of 0.4, determine the angular acceleration of the rover about its center of mass as it starts to tilt and descend.
