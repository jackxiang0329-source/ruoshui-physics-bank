# HFDPO-01715

A renewable energy microgrid in a remote island community uses a 10 kV, 60 Hz AC inverter to supply power to a mix of residential loads and a large seawater desalination plant. The inverter’s output impedance is 0.5 + j1.2 Ω, while the load consists of a 75 kW resistive heater bank and a variable-frequency pump motor (modeled as a 50 kW, 0.85 lagging power factor load). During peak demand, harmonic distortion from the motor drive causes a 15% THD in the output current. Engineers notice that the inverter’s output voltage sags by 8% and the grid-tie relay trips intermittently. Determine the required value of a shunt reactive compensation capacitor bank to restore the voltage at the inverter terminal to within 2% of nominal, while reducing the THD at the inverter output to under 5%. Assume the load impedance remains frequency-dependent and the motor current contains significant 3rd and 5th harmonics.
