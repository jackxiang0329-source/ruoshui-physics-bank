# HFDPO-03080

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole lined with superconducting coils to harvest energy from the Earth’s natural magnetic field variations caused by solar wind interactions with the ionosphere. The coil system, 1.2 km long with 5000 turns, experiences a time-varying magnetic flux density of 120 nT at 0.05 Hz due to geomagnetic pulsations. Engineers must determine the peak voltage generated across the coil array during a geomagnetic storm, when the field changes at a rate of 3.0 nT/s. How does superconducting shielding reduce energy loss, and what constraint does the rate of change impose on coil design?
