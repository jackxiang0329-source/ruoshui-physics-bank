# HFDPO-02311

A team of marine biologists observes that certain deep-sea squid emit bioluminescent pulses in rhythmic patterns, but when viewed through a submersible’s underwater optical sensor array—designed to detect faint signals—these pulses appear to shift in frequency and phase depending on the sensor’s orientation. The sensor uses a fiber-optic interferometer with a 1.55 µm wavelength laser, and the squid’s glow spans 450–500 nm. The array is submerged at 600 m depth, where pressure alters the refractive index of seawater by 0.0015%. Investigate why the observed interference fringes change with sensor tilt, and determine the minimum tilt angle that would create a detectable phase shift exceeding the sensor’s noise floor of 0.05 radians.
