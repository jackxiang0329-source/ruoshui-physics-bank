# HFDPO-14614

In a high-voltage power transmission system, a 120 km underground cable uses concentric cylindrical conductors with a dielectric layer (ε_r = 4.5) between them. The inner conductor has a radius of 2.0 cm, and the outer shielding layer has an inner radius of 5.5 cm. Due to thermal creep, the dielectric material gradually degrades over time, increasing its effective dielectric constant non-uniformly. After 15 years of operation, the capacitance per unit length is measured to be 21% higher than the theoretical value. What is the average effective dielectric constant of the degraded region, and how might this affect long-term system stability?
