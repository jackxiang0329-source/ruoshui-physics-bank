# HFDPO-08827

A quantum computing research team is developing a superconducting qubit shielded by a hollow, cylindrical niobium chamber with a radius of 15 cm and finite height. During cryogenic operation, they observe unexpected electric field fluctuations near the chamber’s inner surface, despite it being a perfect conductor. They suspect non-uniform charge distributions from trapped magnetic flux vortices at the quantum level. Using Gauss’s Law, determine the total enclosed charge within the chamber’s interior volume if the electric flux through the inner cylindrical surface is measured as –0.85 × 10⁴ N·m²/C, and the flux through the flat ends is negligible. Explain why the sign and magnitude of this charge are physically meaningful in the context of quantum coherence.
