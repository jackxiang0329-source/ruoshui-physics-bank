# HFDPO-09099

A quantum dot solar cell prototype using lead sulfide nanocrystals shows a dramatic drop in open-circuit voltage when operated under long-term exposure to oxygen and moisture, despite pristine initial performance. The device exhibits a 1.2 eV bandgap, but after 72 hours in ambient conditions, the photovoltage degrades by 0.3 eV. The degradation correlates with an observed increase in surface trap density from 1.5×10¹² cm⁻² to 8.0×10¹³ cm⁻². Explain the physical mechanism behind this degradation, and design a passivation strategy that balances ion migration suppression with carrier transport efficiency, given that the dielectric layer must maintain a tunneling barrier thickness of ≤5 nm while preventing interface dipole formation.
