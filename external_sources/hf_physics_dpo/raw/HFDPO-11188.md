# HFDPO-11188

A wind farm in the Arctic experiences sudden voltage fluctuations during stormy nights, disrupting power delivery to remote communities. Engineers discover that the rotating blades of a 45-meter-diameter turbine, made of carbon-fiber composite with embedded conductive mesh for lightning protection, are inducing unwanted EMF in nearby control cables—despite being insulated. The blades spin at 18 rpm under high wind, and the magnetic field from the Earth’s dipole (45 μT, inclined 70° from vertical) varies spatially due to local crustal anomalies. Determine whether the induced EMF in a 200-meter control cable loop (aligned east-west, lying flat on the turbine base) exceeds 0.5 V during a blade pass, and explain if additional shielding or cable routing would be necessary.
