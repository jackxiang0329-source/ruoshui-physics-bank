# HFDPO-15028

A biomedical engineer is designing a capacitive sensor to detect early-stage lung collapse (atelectasis) in premature infants. The sensor uses two flexible electrodes placed on the chest wall, forming a parallel-plate capacitor whose capacitance changes as the lung expands or collapses. The electrodes are 10 cm² in area, separated by 0.5 mm of air (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m), but due to skin contact and tissue, the effective dielectric is a mixture with a relative permittivity of 3.0. When the infant breathes, the distance between plates varies by ±0.1 mm. What is the percentage change in capacitance during a full breath?
