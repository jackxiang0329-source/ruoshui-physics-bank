# HFDPO-05201

A fusion reactor's first wall is exposed to 10 MW/m² of neutron flux from a plasma at 15 keV. The wall, made of a tungsten–copper composite, must manage transient heat loads during plasma disruptions lasting 10 ms. Using finite element thermal modeling, determine the maximum subsurface temperature rise and assess whether localized melting (T_melt = 3695 K) occurs at the tungsten surface. Assume the composite has a graded thermal conductivity from 100 W/m·K (W) to 400 W/m·K (Cu), with a 5 mm thick tungsten layer and a 2 mm copper heat spreader.
