# HFDPO-01847

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a collapsed cylindrical seabed structure—partially buried in soft sediment—containing trapped gas pockets. The ROV’s sensors detect a pressure rise of 1.4 MPa at a depth of 3,200 m, but the internal gas pressure reads only 1.1 MPa. The surrounding seawater has a density of 1025 kg/m³, and the gas is ideal. The structure is oriented vertically, with a 5-m long, 1.2-m diameter cylinder sealed at both ends. The sediment exerts a lateral confining pressure proportional to depth but is assumed to be static. What is the actual depth to the top of the cylinder, assuming the gas is trapped in a stable equilibrium?
