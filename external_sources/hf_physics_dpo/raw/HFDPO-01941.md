# HFDPO-01941

During a polar expedition, a team discovers a large submerged ice shelf beneath a thin layer of seawater in a fjord. The ice is 12 meters thick, with a surface temperature of –18°C, and the surrounding seawater is at 2.5°C. A research drone is deployed to measure heat flux through the ice base to assess melt rates. The ice has a variable thermal conductivity that increases with depth due to pressure-induced crystal rearrangement: k(z) = 0.5 + 0.08z (W/m·K), where z is depth in meters from the surface (0 ≤ z ≤ 12). The bottom of the ice is in contact with warm seawater, maintaining a steady 2.5°C at z = 12 m. The thermal flux through the ice at the base is measured at 142 W/m². What is the rate of internal heat generation (W/m³) that must be present within the ice to sustain this heat flux profile, assuming no other energy sources?
