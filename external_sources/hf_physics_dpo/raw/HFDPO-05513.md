# HFDPO-05513

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses a central receiver tower with a 100 m tall heliostat field, each mirror reflecting sunlight onto a receiver at the top. On a clear noon, 1200 W/m² of solar irradiance reaches the mirrors, but only 75% is effectively captured due to angular misalignment and reflection losses. The receiver, made of a copper-nickel alloy (specific heat 450 J/kg·K, density 8500 kg/m³), is a spherical shell 1.5 m in diameter with 5 cm thickness. If the receiver is initially at 295 K and operates for 3 hours under steady conditions, how much does its temperature rise? Assume negligible heat loss to the environment during the initial heating phase, but consider that the effective area receiving solar flux isn’t the projected disk area—what fraction of the receiver’s surface can be illuminated at any instant?
