# HFDPO-15021

A remote Arctic research station relies on a hybrid heat engine system—part Stirling, part thermoelectric—powered by a small nuclear reactor. The station requires 15 kW of continuous power, yet the system’s efficiency drops by 12% when the cold sink dips below 20°C. Design a feedback-controlled thermal shunt to maintain optimal performance, and determine the minimum heat input rate required under worst-case conditions.
