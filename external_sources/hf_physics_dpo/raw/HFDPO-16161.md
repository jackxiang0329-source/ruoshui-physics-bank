# HFDPO-16161

A lunar rover navigating the rugged terrain of Enceladus detects a sudden shift in its gyroscopic stabilization system after skidding on a patch of subsurface ice. The rover’s primary gyroscope, a 12 kg flywheel with a 0.3 m radius, spins at 1,800 rpm but begins to precess at a rate of 0.05 rad/s due to unbalanced torques from uneven terrain. Calculate the net external torque acting on the gyroscope during precession and determine whether the rover’s onboard control system can compensate in real time, given a maximum torque output of 2.5 N·m. Assume the wheel is a uniform solid disk and ignore air resistance.
