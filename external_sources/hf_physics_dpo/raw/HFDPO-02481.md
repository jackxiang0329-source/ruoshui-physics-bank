# HFDPO-02481

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a complex array of charged plates to confine plasma at 10⁸ K. One critical region features a radial electric field that must be precisely calculated to prevent plasma contact with walls. The charge distribution is non-uniform: a cylindrical core of radius 0.5 m has a volume charge density ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 – r²/R²), where R = 0.5 m and ρ₀ = 3×10⁻⁶ C/m³. Using Gauss’s Law, determine the magnitude of the electric field at r = 0.3 m and r = 0.6 m, and explain why the field at r = 0.6 m is weaker than expected despite increasing distance from the center.
