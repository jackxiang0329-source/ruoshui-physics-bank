# HFDPO-10597

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole drilled 3.2 km into basaltic rock to harness heat, but the turbine-generator system intermittently loses efficiency during deep drilling. Engineers detect fluctuating induced currents in a buried superconducting coil array (radius 1.5 m, 120 turns) lining the borehole wall, synchronized with vibrations from a rotary drill bit (25 Hz, 1.8 m peak-to-peak displacement). The coil is magnetically shielded, yet voltage spikes of up to 8.4 V appear. Estimate the minimum magnitude of the time-varying magnetic field component responsible, and evaluate whether seismic motion-induced eddy currents in the surrounding rock could explain the observed effects.
