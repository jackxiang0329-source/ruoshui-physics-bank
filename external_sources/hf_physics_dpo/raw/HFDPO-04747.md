# HFDPO-04747

In a solar-powered microgrid on a remote island, three photovoltaic arrays (A, B, C) feed into a central inverter with variable output demand. The arrays are connected through a network of diodes and resistive junctions (each with 0.5 Ω resistance) to a common bus. During peak sunlight, the inverter draws 22 A at 29 V. Using Kirchhoff’s laws, determine the current through each diode branch and explain why the system’s efficiency drops by 12% when the sun dims and Array C’s output drops to 4 A, assuming diode forward voltage remains constant at 0.7 V.
