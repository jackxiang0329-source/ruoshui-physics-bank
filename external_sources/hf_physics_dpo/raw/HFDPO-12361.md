# HFDPO-12361

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystem thrives near a volcanic ridge where superheated water (350°C, 30 MPa) exits into the frigid ocean (2°C). Engineers propose harvesting energy from this natural thermal gradient using a closed-loop Rankine cycle with supercritical CO₂ as the working fluid. The system must deliver 5 MW of net power while operating within strict environmental constraints: no thermal discharge above 15°C and minimal CO₂ leakage. What is the minimum theoretical efficiency possible under these conditions, and how does this compare to the maximum achievable efficiency given real-world component inefficiencies (pump work: 18% of turbine output, turbine and condenser losses: 12% each)?
