# HFDPO-07193

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a tokamak design with a toroidal plasma current of 15 million amperes, confined by a magnetic field that varies radially. During a brief disruption, 3.2 MJ of magnetic energy is rapidly converted into thermal energy in the plasma, heating it to 150 million K—yet the vessel’s outer shell remains at 300 K. How much mass must be added to the shell’s heat sinks to safely absorb this energy without exceeding a 50 K temperature rise, assuming the shell material has a specific heat capacity of 500 J/(kg·K)? Consider energy conservation across both electromagnetic and thermal systems.
