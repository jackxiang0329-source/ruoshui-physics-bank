# HFDPO-02824

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a complex spherical shell of superconducting material to confine plasma at 150 million K. During a diagnostic test, a small charged dust particle (q = +2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ C) is detected at a radial distance of 1.2 m from the center, where the electric field is measured to be 72 N/C radially outward. The vessel has a non-uniform charge distribution: a thin inner shell (r = 0.8 m) with surface charge density σ₁ = +3.0 × 10⁻⁹ C/m², and an outer shell (r = 1.5 m) with σ₂ = −4.8 × 10⁻⁹ C/m². What is the net charge enclosed within a Gaussian surface at r = 1.2 m, and how must the internal charge distribution be adjusted to maintain stable plasma confinement?
