# HFDPO-01680

A satellite in a geostationary orbit (35,786 km above Earth) carries a highly precise atomic clock synchronized with Earth-based clocks. Due to its high orbital speed (~3.07 km/s) and weaker gravitational field, relativity causes the satellite clock to drift relative to Earth clocks. If not corrected, this drift would accumulate to ~38 microseconds per day—enough to distort GPS positioning. Calculate how much time the satellite clock gains or loses compared to Earth clocks over one day, considering both special relativistic time dilation from motion and general relativistic gravitational time dilation.
