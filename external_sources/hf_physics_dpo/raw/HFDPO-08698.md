# HFDPO-08698

A solar-powered water purifier in a remote village uses a silicon photodiode array to monitor water turbidity. The diodes operate in reverse bias, generating a current proportional to light transmission through the water. During testing, engineers notice the current drops by 18% over one month despite no change in illumination. Given the diode’s saturation current is 2.5 nA at room temperature (25°C), the bandgap energy is 1.12 eV, and the temperature rises to 38°C in the field, is this drop due to temperature effects alone, or could recombination centers be forming in the material?
