# HFDPO-02896

A solar-powered drone designed to monitor volcanic activity in remote Iceland must glide silently from a high-altitude launch point (7,200 m) to a target zone 22 km away, relying solely on gravitational potential energy to maintain flight after initial acceleration. Atmospheric conditions vary: at 6,000 m, wind shear creates a constant opposing drag force of 32 N, while air density changes with altitude, affecting lift and drag coefficients. The drone’s mass is 18.5 kg, and its aerodynamic design allows a glide ratio of 28:1 (horizontal distance per meter of altitude lost). Calculate the minimum speed required at launch to reach the target zone if the drone begins descending from 7,200 m with no propulsion, accounting for energy dissipated by drag over the entire glide path.
