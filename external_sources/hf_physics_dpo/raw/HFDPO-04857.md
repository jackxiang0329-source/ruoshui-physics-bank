# HFDPO-04857

A quantum radar prototype uses entangled microwave photons at 10 GHz to detect stealth aircraft in a cluttered urban environment. The system emits one photon of an entangled pair, sending the other to a distant receiver. When the transmitted photon reflects off a target, the correlation between the two photons allows detection even if the signal is below the noise floor. However, atmospheric water vapor at 22.2 GHz causes significant absorption, and the radar’s signal bandwidth (200 MHz) straddles this absorption peak. Given the vapor’s absorption coefficient is 2.8 dB/km at 22.2 GHz and increases linearly to 5.0 dB/km at 22.5 GHz, estimate the net signal degradation over a 10 km path due to atmospheric absorption and explain why entanglement-based detection might still succeed despite this loss.
