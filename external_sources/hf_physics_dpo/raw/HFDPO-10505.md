# HFDPO-10505

A solar farm in the Atacama Desert uses bifacial photovoltaic panels that harvest sunlight from both sides, but glare from sand and panel surfaces causes up to 18% efficiency loss due to polarized reflections. Engineers must design a semi-transparent, polarization-selective anti-reflective coating using a multilayer thin-film structure that transmits non-polarized sunlight while blocking horizontally polarized glare. Given that the dominant reflected glare is horizontally polarized at Brewster’s angle (θ_B = 57.5°) for sand (n = 1.55), and the coating must be effective across a 30° angular range, determine the required refractive index and thickness profile of the coating’s top layer to maximize transmission of unpolarized sunlight while minimizing reflection of s-polarized light at the critical angles.
