# HFDPO-05768

During a deep-space mission, a crew must stabilize a supercooled liquid hydrogen reservoir on a Mars rover, where temperature fluctuates unpredictably between 15 K and 25 K due to solar exposure and radiative cooling. The reservoir is insulated with a multilayer system designed to delay phase change, but a sudden micro-meteoroid impact fractures the inner wall, initiating spontaneous solidification. The system’s heat capacity is 3.2 J/(kg·K), the latent heat of fusion is 5.8 kJ/kg, and the liquid hydrogen’s initial enthalpy is 1.2 kJ/kg above the solidification threshold. Calculate the minimum thickness of the cryogenic insulation (thermal conductivity 0.015 W/(m·K)) required to prevent freezing for 72 hours if the average heat influx is 0.8 W per kg, assuming no thermal gradients within the tank.
