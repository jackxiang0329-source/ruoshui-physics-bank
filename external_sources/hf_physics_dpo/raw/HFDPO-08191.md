# HFDPO-08191

In a quantum computing lab, a team observes that a single electron in a superconducting qubit exhibits interference patterns when passing through a double-slit setup embedded in a Josephson junction. The slits are 100 nm wide, separated by 300 nm, and the electron’s de Broglie wavelength is precisely 200 nm. However, when a high-frequency electromagnetic probe (100 THz) is used to detect which slit the electron passed through, the interference fringes vanish. Calculate the minimum energy uncertainty (in eV) introduced by the probing field that causes decoherence, and explain why measuring the electron’s path destroys the wave-like behavior, using the uncertainty principle and wavefunction collapse concepts.
