# HFDPO-09759

A fusion reactor prototype uses a toroidal magnetic confinement chamber where plasma currents must be initiated rapidly to stabilize the containment field. Engineers observe that the initial induction coil, designed to generate a 1.2 T rise in magnetic field over 15 ms, fails to induce sufficient current in the plasma at startup. The coil has a self-inductance of 0.8 H, and the plasma’s effective resistance is 0.5 Ω. However, when the same coil is tested in vacuum (no plasma), it draws 200 A from its power supply during the ramp-up. What is the minimum emf the power supply must deliver to overcome back-emf and still achieve the required rate of flux change in the presence of the plasma, and why might the observed plasma current still fall short despite meeting this theoretical emf?
