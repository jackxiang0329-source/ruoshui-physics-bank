# HFDPO-01009

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a liquid lithium blanket to absorb neutron energy and breed tritium, but during a sudden power surge, localized regions exceed 1,800 K—near lithium’s boiling point (1,615 K at 1 atm). The blanket’s design relies on steady two-phase (liquid-vapor) flow for thermal regulation. If a 50 cm² patch of the blanket undergoes rapid boiling, releasing vapor at 1,620 K and 1.1 atm, how does the resulting pressure spike threaten vessel integrity compared to a purely liquid heat transfer scenario? Use thermodynamic tables and the Clapeyron equation to assess whether vapor formation significantly alters local energy flux and mechanical stress.
