# HFDPO-00925

A fusion reactor containment vessel in a next-generation tokamak design experiences unexpected neutron flux spikes during high-beta plasma operation, with measured neutron production rates rising 37% above theoretical predictions from the D-T reaction rate. The reactor’s neutron shielding, composed of 1.2 m of borated concrete (with 2% boron by weight), shows signs of accelerated degradation in neutron-induced helium blistering. Given that the primary neutron source is the D-T reaction (Q = 17.6 MeV), and the neutron energy spectrum follows a Maxwellian distribution peaking at 14.1 MeV, evaluate whether the observed flux anomaly is more likely due to: (a) enhanced reaction rate from plasma instabilities, or (b) unaccounted neutron moderation and leakage through the shielding geometry affecting detector readings. Use the neutron transport equation with energy-dependent cross sections for hydrogen and boron, and integrate over the 1–20 MeV range.
