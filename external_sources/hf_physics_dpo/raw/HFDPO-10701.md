# HFDPO-10701

A small research lab in a remote Arctic station uses a superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) to measure weak magnetic fields from permafrost shifts. The SQUID operates at 4.2 K, cooled by liquid helium. One morning, the system fails to detect signals, though the cryostat shows stable temperature. The team suspects the superconductor may have lost its zero-resistance state due to a magnetic field spike during a solar storm. If the critical magnetic field of the niobium alloy used is 0.15 T and the ambient field from the storm reached 0.12 T, was the superconductor still superconducting?
