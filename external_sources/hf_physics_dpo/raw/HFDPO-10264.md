# HFDPO-10264

A lunar lander descends vertically near the Moon’s south pole, where gravity is 1.62 m/s² and the terrain is a steep, icy slope with a 25° incline. During its final 30 meters of descent, the lander’s thrusters fire intermittently to maintain a controlled rate of descent, but due to a sensor glitch, the vertical velocity is not continuously monitored—only the horizontal drift relative to the slope is tracked. At 10 m above the surface, the lander’s horizontal displacement from its ideal landing point is 0.8 m, and its vertical velocity is 0.6 m/s. Assuming the thrusters apply a constant force vector tilted 15° away from vertical to counteract slope-induced drift, determine the magnitude of thrust required to achieve zero horizontal drift at touchdown, and explain how the lander’s angular momentum about its center of mass must be managed to prevent tipping during descent.
