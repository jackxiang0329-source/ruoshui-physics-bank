# HFDPO-00243

A fusion reactor containment chamber uses a toroidal magnetic field to confine plasma at 15 million Kelvin. Engineers notice a persistent asymmetry in the plasma pressure profile, causing localized erosion of the inner wall. The magnetic field is generated by 12 superconducting coils arranged in a ring, each carrying 120 kA. Given that the plasma current induces a self-field opposing the external field, and assuming a current density in the plasma follows a Pfirsch–Schlüter profile, determine the net poloidal field at a radial position r = 0.8 m, where the safety factor q = 3.2. The vacuum toroidal field is 6.4 T at the plasma center.
