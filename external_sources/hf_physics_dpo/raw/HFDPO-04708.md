# HFDPO-04708

A quantum sensor team at a national lab is calibrating a high-precision atom interferometer used to detect gravitational waves. The device uses laser-cooled rubidium-87 atoms split into superposition states, with their wavefunctions recombined after traversing two 1.2-meter arms at right angles. Due to an unexpected background vibration, the interference pattern shifts unpredictably across 1000 measurement cycles. The team observes a 6.7° phase drift over 30 seconds, but cannot isolate whether it’s due to path-length asymmetry from thermal expansion or a refractive index fluctuation in the vacuum chamber. The laser wavelength is 780 nm, and atoms have a de Broglie wavelength of 0.85 nm. Determine whether the phase shift is more likely caused by a 4.3 μm path-length difference or a 2.1×10⁻⁸ fluctuation in refractive index.
