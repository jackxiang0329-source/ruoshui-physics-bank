# HFDPO-07504

A microfluidic device for early cancer detection uses a branching network of channels (diameter ~50 μm) to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from blood based on size and deformability. At a critical junction, blood flows at 1.2 mm/s with viscosity 3.5 cP and density 1060 kg/m³. The device must ensure CTCs (diameter 15 μm, 20% stiffer than RBCs) follow a specific path to a capture zone, while red blood cells (RBCs, 7 μm) are directed elsewhere. However, simulations show inconsistent sorting due to unexpected vortex formation at the bifurcation. Evaluate the role of inertial vs. viscous forces in the microchannel flow and determine whether the flow regime is truly creeping (Re < 0.1) or if inertial effects are disrupting separation efficiency.
