# HFDPO-09775

A seismologist analyzing data from a subsea cable rupture near a tectonic boundary detects a complex wave pattern: a 1200 Hz P-wave travels through the oceanic crust at 6.8 km/s, while a 400 Hz S-wave moves at 3.9 km/s. The cable’s break generates both waves simultaneously, but due to wave dispersion and varying medium density, the P-wave’s amplitude decays as A_P = A_0 e^{-0.02x} , and the S-wave’s as A_S = A_0 e^{-0.05x} , where x is distance in km. At a monitoring station 25 km from the source, the recorded S-wave peak amplitude is 2.5 times greater than the P-wave peak. What was the original amplitude ratio A_0^S / A_0^P at the source?
