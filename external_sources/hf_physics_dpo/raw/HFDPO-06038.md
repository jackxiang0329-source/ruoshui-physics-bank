# HFDPO-06038

A lunar habitat module on the Artemis Base uses a buried heat-pipe radiator array to reject waste heat from life support systems, but after 6 months of operation, the surface temperature of the radiator plates exceeds design limits by 15°C. The system relies on passive thermal regulation in a vacuum environment with no wind or convection. The radiator plates are made of aluminum alloy (k = 180 W/m·K), 2 mm thick, and 1.5 m long. They are buried 0.8 m below the regolith surface, which has a thermal conductivity of 0.6 W/m·K and an average temperature of 220 K. The heat pipe operates at a constant temperature of 300 K, transferring 450 W total. Why is the radiator surface overheating despite the design thermal resistance?
