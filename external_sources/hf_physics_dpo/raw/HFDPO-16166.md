# HFDPO-16166

During a geomagnetic storm, a satellite in low Earth orbit (altitude 600 km) with a 12-meter conducting tether deploys into a 500 km circular orbit. The tether, oriented radially due to gravity-gradient stabilization, moves through Earth’s magnetic field (B ≈ 30 μT, dipole-aligned). As solar wind compresses the magnetosphere, the field strength increases linearly at 0.5 μT/min. The satellite’s onboard electronics measure a fluctuating induced EMF of 8 V, but the nominal induced voltage from motion alone is only 3.2 V. What explains this discrepancy, and what is the rate of change of magnetic flux due to the time-varying field component?
