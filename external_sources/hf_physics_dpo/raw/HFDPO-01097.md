# HFDPO-01097

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic chip to separate cancer cells from blood samples using size-dependent drag in a narrow channel. The channel is 50 μm wide, and blood flows at 0.2 mL/min under a pressure drop of 150 kPa. Cancer cells (diameter ~12 μm) experience different viscous drag than red blood cells (~7 μm), but the fluid’s viscosity is temperature-sensitive. If the fluid’s viscosity increases from 1.2 mPa·s at 25°C to 1.8 mPa·s at 37°C, how does this temperature shift affect the velocity profile and the relative drag force on the two cell types?
