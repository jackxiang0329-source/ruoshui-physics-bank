# HFDPO-00447

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a polarimetric imager to detect oil slicks on the ocean surface during a storm. The sensor measures the degree of polarization of reflected sunlight at 532 nm, but the data shows unexpected fluctuations. The ocean surface is modeled as a rough, birefringent interface with varying foam coverage, and the incident sunlight is unpolarized but scattered by atmospheric aerosols. Determine the effective polarization state observed when the sensor looks at a 30° glancing angle over a patch of oil-contaminated water (refractive index 1.42) with 15% foam coverage. Assume the foam introduces anisotropic scattering with a polarization-dependent reflectance of 0.20 (parallel) and 0.08 (perpendicular), and the underlying oil-water boundary exhibits Brewster’s angle effects.
