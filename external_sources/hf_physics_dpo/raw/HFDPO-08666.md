# HFDPO-08666

A space probe deploying a solar sail near Jupiter must adjust its trajectory using only momentum exchange with sunlight and residual plasma from the Jovian magnetosphere. The sail, 80 m² in area and 0.001 kg/m² surface density, reflects photons with 98% efficiency. At 5.2 AU from the Sun, solar irradiance is 50 W/m². The probe initially moves at 15 km/s relative to Jupiter. As it nears a gas giant moon, a 0.1 s burst of plasma — 2×10¹⁰ ions per second, each with average momentum 8×10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s — hits the sail at an angle of 30° from normal. Calculate the net change in the probe’s velocity after the burst, assuming no gravity effects.
