# HFDPO-02760

During a Mars rover mission, engineers discover a buried ice-rich layer beneath a rocky regolith. To extract subsurface water, they plan to use a pulsed radio-frequency heater embedded in the surface. The heater delivers 1.2 kW of power in 10-second bursts every 30 seconds, targeting a 0.5 m³ volume of regolith-ice mixture (thermal conductivity 0.8 W/m·K, specific heat 1.8 kJ/kg·K, density 2100 kg/m³). The ice fraction is initially 60%, and the system operates in a vacuum with negligible convection. Estimate the time required for the temperature to rise enough to initiate significant ice melting (at 273 K), assuming the heat diffuses radially from the source and the surface remains insulated except at the heater.
