# HFDPO-11310

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a polarimetric imager to detect oil slicks on ocean surfaces, but data shows unexpected polarization signatures near coastal regions—despite no visible oil. The sensor measures Stokes parameters S₀ = 1000, S₁ = 250, S₂ = 180, S₃ = -50 at 532 nm. The background glint from sun-reflecting water is modeled as partially polarized sky light, but the observed signal exceeds the expected glint polarization. Could this be due to coherent scattering from suspended colloidal particles in surf zone turbulence, and if so, what is the degree of polarization induced by scattering anisotropy?
