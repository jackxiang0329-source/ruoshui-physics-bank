# HFDPO-00936

During a seismic survey in a geothermal drilling site, engineers detect a mysterious low-frequency wave (2.5 Hz) that propagates through basalt rock at 3.8 km/s, but its amplitude decays unusually fast—halving every 150 m. They suspect it’s not a simple P-wave but a trapped surface wave in a thin, high-velocity basalt layer sandwiched between low-velocity sedimentary strata. Using only three sensor readings spaced 200 m apart along a borehole, can the team determine whether this wave is a guided Rayleigh wave or a leaky mode, and what structural thickness of the basalt layer is most consistent with the observed decay and phase velocity?
