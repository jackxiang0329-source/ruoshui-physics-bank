# HFDPO-04411

A satellite-based polarimetric radar system monitoring ice sheets in Greenland detects anomalous polarization signatures in a region where surface meltwater pools are forming beneath a thick layer of snow. The radar emits horizontally polarized pulses at 10 GHz, and measurements show a 27% increase in vertically polarized backscatter relative to the incident signal. Engineers suspect that internal reflections within the snowpack—driven by anisotropic ice crystal alignment—are causing partial depolarization. Given that the snowpack has a dielectric constant of 2.5 and a depth of 1.8 m, estimate the effective orientation angle of the ice crystals relative to the vertical, assuming the dominant scattering mechanism is Bragg scattering from periodically spaced ice layers.
