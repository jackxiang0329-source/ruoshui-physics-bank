# HFDPO-13275

A biomedical engineer is designing a new optical sensor to detect early-stage cancer by analyzing the diffraction patterns of light scattered from tissue samples. The setup uses a laser with wavelength 635 nm, and the sample is placed 15 cm from a screen with a micropatterned grating (300 lines/mm). During testing, a distinct bright fringe appears at 4.2 cm from the central peak. The engineer suspects the fringe is not due to the grating alone, but rather to interference between scattered light and a secondary phase-modulating layer in the tissue. Determine whether this fringe can be explained by the grating alone, and if not, what additional phase shift (in radians) must be present in the secondary layer to account for the observed position.
