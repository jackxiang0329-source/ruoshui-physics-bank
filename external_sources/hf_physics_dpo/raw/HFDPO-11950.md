# HFDPO-11950

A lunar observatory is designing a compact, foldable telescope for deployment on the Moon's surface, where dust accumulation and extreme temperature swings affect optical alignment. The primary mirror is a 1.2-meter diameter parabolic dish with a focal length of 4.8 meters, but due to a manufacturing flaw, the mirror’s vertex is misaligned by 1.5 mm from the intended optical axis. An astronaut measures the focal spot size on a detector placed at the nominal focus and observes it is 2.4 mm in diameter, instead of the theoretical diffraction limit of 0.6 mm. Evaluate whether the observed blur is primarily due to the misalignment, diffraction, or a combination of both, and determine the effective angular misalignment of the mirror relative to the incoming starlight.
