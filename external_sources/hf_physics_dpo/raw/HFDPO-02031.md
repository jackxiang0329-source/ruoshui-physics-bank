# HFDPO-02031

A city’s underground subway system uses a pair of parallel copper rails to supply power to trains, but engineers notice that the rails heat up significantly during peak hours, risking thermal expansion and track misalignment. Each rail is 500 m long, 15 cm wide, and 2 cm thick, with resistivity of 1.68 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. If the system carries 1200 A of current through each rail (returning via the other), and the rails are insulated from the ground, calculate the total power dissipated as heat in both rails. Then, assess whether this heating could cause a 1 cm thermal expansion in steel rails (coefficient of linear expansion = 12 × 10⁻⁶ /°C) if no heat is lost.
