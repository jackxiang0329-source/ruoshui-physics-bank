# HFDPO-13519

A wind turbine blade in a remote mountainous region experiences unexpected wear at the leading edge, reducing efficiency. Engineers suspect that turbulent air flow over the blade increases drag and creates localized friction, but the blade’s composite surface has a complex, non-uniform texture. Given a measured drag force of 1,200 N at 15 m/s wind speed, and knowing the blade’s projected area is 12 m², determine the effective drag coefficient. Then, calculate the reduction in drag if the blade were smoothed to reduce the coefficient by 18%, and estimate how much more power the turbine could generate under the same wind conditions, assuming power is proportional to drag force.
