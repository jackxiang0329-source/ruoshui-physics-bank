# HFDPO-06728

A deep-sea submersible equipped with a high-resolution imaging system detects a bioluminescent organism at a 32° angle below the horizontal, 450 m beneath the surface. The camera’s lens has a fixed focal length of 18 mm and is protected by a sapphire window (n = 1.77) with a thickness of 2.4 mm. Due to the window’s curvature and refractive properties, the image appears shifted laterally by 3.2 cm relative to its true position. Determine the actual depth of the organism in the water, accounting for refraction at both the air–sapphire and sapphire–water interfaces, and explain why the system’s automated depth algorithm fails to correct for this shift.
