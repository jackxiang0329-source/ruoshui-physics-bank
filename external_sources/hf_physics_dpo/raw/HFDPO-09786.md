# HFDPO-09786

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system on the ocean floor releases superheated water at 400°C into cold seawater at 2°C. Engineers propose using a closed-loop Rankine cycle with R-134a as the working fluid to extract power from this thermal gradient. The system operates with a turbine inlet pressure of 10 MPa and a condenser pressure of 200 kPa. Given that the turbine and pump are adiabatic but irreversible (isentropic efficiencies of 85% and 70%, respectively), and the heat exchanger efficiency is 90%, determine the maximum possible net power output per kilogram of working fluid, and explain why the actual cycle must be optimized around the condenser pressure—especially in light of the surrounding cold ocean environment.
