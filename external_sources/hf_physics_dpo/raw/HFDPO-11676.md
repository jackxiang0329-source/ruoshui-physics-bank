# HFDPO-11676

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a network of steel boreholes (diameter 15 cm, length 3.2 km) drilled into hot rock formations to extract heat. During operation, the borehole walls experience a radial stress gradient due to thermal expansion: the inner surface heats to 320°C while the outer surface remains at 180°C. The steel has a Young’s modulus of 200 GPa, Poisson’s ratio of 0.29, and coefficient of thermal expansion of 12 × 10⁻⁶/°C. Engineers observe micro-cracking at the inner bore surface despite material safety margins. Evaluate whether the stress-induced strain state is sufficient to cause failure, considering both thermal expansion anisotropy and elastic stress redistribution in the thick-walled cylinder.
