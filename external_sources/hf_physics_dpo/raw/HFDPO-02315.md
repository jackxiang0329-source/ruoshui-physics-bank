# HFDPO-02315

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate cancer cells from blood based on size-induced differences in hydrodynamic lift forces. The device uses a serpentine channel with a 50 μm-wide constriction; blood flows at 1.2 cm/s with viscosity 3.5 mPa·s and density 1060 kg/m³. At the constriction, the pressure drops by 180 Pa compared to the upstream region. A 12 μm diameter cancer cell (density 1080 kg/m³) experiences a net lift force toward the outer wall. Explain why this happens and determine the magnitude of the radial pressure gradient at the constriction, assuming ideal fluid flow and negligible viscosity effects at this scale.
