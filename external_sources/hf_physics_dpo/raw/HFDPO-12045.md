# HFDPO-12045

A remote Arctic research station relies on a hybrid DC power system: solar panels during daylight (12 V nominal, 15 A max), a wind turbine (18 V, 10 A max), and a lithium-ion battery bank (24 V, 200 Ah). Due to unpredictable weather, the station must maintain a minimum 80% state-of-charge in the battery and power a 1.2 kW load (constant power draw) at 24 V. The system uses a DC-DC buck-boost converter to regulate voltage. During a 36-hour polar night with no solar/wind input, the battery voltage drops to 22.4 V after 12 hours. Evaluate whether the battery can sustain the load for the full 36 hours without external charging, assuming 92% converter efficiency and internal battery resistance of 0.05 Ω.
