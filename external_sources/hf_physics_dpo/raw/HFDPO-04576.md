# HFDPO-04576

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a polarimetric imaging sensor to detect oil spills by analyzing the polarization state of sunlight reflected from the ocean surface. The sensor measures the degree of linear polarization (DOLP) of reflected light at 45° and 135° angles relative to the horizontal. During a test over a suspected spill site, the DOLP is measured as 0.28 at 45°, but drops to 0.06 at 135°. The natural surface reflection from clean seawater has a DOLP of 0.35 at 45° and 0.10 at 135°. Using the Stokes vector formalism, determine whether the observed shift in polarization state suggests an oil slick is present, and calculate the expected change in the polarization ellipse’s orientation due to the oil film’s birefringent properties.
