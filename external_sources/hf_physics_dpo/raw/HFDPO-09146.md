# HFDPO-09146

A cryogenic rocket engine uses liquid hydrogen (LH₂) stored at 20 K, but during launch vibrations, micro-oscillations cause localized superheating in the fuel lines, risking premature vaporization and cavitation. The fuel line has a 5 mm internal diameter, and fuel flows at 1.2 m/s. Thermal conductivity of the line wall is 18 W/m·K, and the heat flux from vibrations is estimated at 2.3 × 10⁴ W/m². Determine the critical superheat temperature above which nucleation becomes thermodynamically probable at the liquid-vapor interface, and assess whether cavitation could initiate under these conditions, assuming a nucleation barrier height of 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁹ J and a contact angle of 0° with the wall.
