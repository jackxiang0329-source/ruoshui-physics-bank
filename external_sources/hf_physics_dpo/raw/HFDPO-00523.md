# HFDPO-00523

A fusion reactor's magnetic confinement chamber uses a highly symmetric, toroidal plasma vessel with a non-uniform charge density that varies radially as ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 − r/R) for r < R, where R = 2.0 m is the major radius and ρ₀ = 3.0 × 10⁻⁶ C/m³. Engineers need to calculate the electric field at r = 1.5 m from the center, but must account for the fact that the plasma's charge distribution is only confined within a thin cylindrical shell of width 0.1 m. How does the symmetry and the shell’s thickness affect the application of Gauss’s Law, and what is the magnitude of the electric field at that point?
