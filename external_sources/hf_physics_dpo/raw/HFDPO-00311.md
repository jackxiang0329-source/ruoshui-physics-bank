# HFDPO-00311

A spacecraft's heat shield during atmospheric re-entry experiences intense heating due to gas molecule collisions at hypersonic speeds. The shield’s surface is coated with a ceramic material that ablates (erodes) as it absorbs thermal energy, but the rate depends on the average kinetic energy of the surrounding atmospheric molecules—specifically, the translational kinetic energy per molecule. At an altitude of 80 km, the air temperature is 250 K, but the gas is not in thermal equilibrium due to high-speed shearing. Using the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, estimate the fraction of nitrogen molecules with translational kinetic energy greater than 2.5 × 10⁻²⁰ J, and explain how this affects ablation efficiency compared to equilibrium conditions.
