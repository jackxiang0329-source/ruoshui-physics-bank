# HFDPO-08104

A quantum dot array in a next-generation solar cell absorbs sunlight across a narrow band (λ = 600–650 nm), but experimental efficiency drops sharply above 110 K despite ideal band alignment. The dots are 3.2 nm in diameter, confined to a 2D lattice with spacing 6.8 nm. Measurements show a sudden loss of carrier delocalization above this temperature. Determine the critical temperature at which thermal energy disrupts the collective coherent state responsible for enhanced absorption—accounting for both electron-electron interactions and phonon-assisted decoherence—using the Bose-Einstein condensation framework with finite-size corrections.
