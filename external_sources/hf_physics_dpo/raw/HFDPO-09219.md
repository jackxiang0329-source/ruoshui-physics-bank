# HFDPO-09219

A quantum dot solar cell uses a periodic array of nanoscale semiconductor pillars (500 nm diameter, 2 μm tall) spaced 1.2 μm apart, designed to enhance light absorption via constructive interference. However, under sunlight (broadband, 400–700 nm), the device shows unexpected dips in efficiency at 550 nm and 650 nm. Given that the grating effectively behaves as a diffraction-limited aperture with significant multiple-slit interference, determine whether these dips are due to destructive interference from the grating structure or diffraction minima, and explain how altering the pillar height would affect the angular spread of the diffracted light.
