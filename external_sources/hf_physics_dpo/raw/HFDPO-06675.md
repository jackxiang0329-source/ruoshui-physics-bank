# HFDPO-06675

A coastal city is evaluating a proposed offshore wave energy converter (WEC) array designed to harness energy from swell waves (peak period 12 s, height 3 m) approaching at a 30° angle to the shoreline. The WECs are arranged in a staggered configuration with 50 m spacing. During storm conditions, a submerged trench (20 m deep, 500 m wide) near the array is found to generate strong lee waves, reducing WEC efficiency by 22%. Engineers suspect the trench alters near-bottom flow structures due to wave-induced currents interacting with the seafloor. Determine the critical Froude number at which lee waves become unstable, and explain how the trench geometry and wave angle influence the energy transfer between the surface wave and the bottom flow.
