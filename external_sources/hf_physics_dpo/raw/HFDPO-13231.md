# HFDPO-13231

A research team at a coastal nuclear facility detects a sudden spike in gamma radiation levels near a decommissioned reactor cooling pond, despite no active operations. The radiation is traced to a small, corroded steel canister buried in sediment, containing mixed fission products. Analysis shows the dominant source is Cs-137 (t₁/₂ = 30.17 years), with an initial activity of 1.2 × 10⁶ Bq when sealed 25 years ago. The canister is now 1.5 m below the pond floor, and the water attenuates gamma rays by a factor of 0.85 per meter. The safety threshold for public exposure is 0.2 µSv/h. Calculate the current dose rate at the surface due to Cs-137 alone, assuming isotropic emission and a 10 cm² detector area, and determine whether the canister exceeds safety limits.
