# HFDPO-00053

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate cancer cells from blood based on size-dependent drag in a narrow channel. The device uses a 100 μm wide channel with blood flowing at 0.5 mm/s. Cancer cells (≈15 μm diameter) experience different drag forces than red blood cells (≈7 μm). Using Stokes’ law for spherical particles in a viscous fluid, estimate the ratio of drag forces on a cancer cell to a red blood cell. Assume blood has a dynamic viscosity of 3.5 × 10⁻³ Pa·s and a density of 1060 kg/m³.
