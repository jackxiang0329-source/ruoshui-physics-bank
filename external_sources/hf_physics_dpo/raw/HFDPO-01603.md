# HFDPO-01603

A high-speed maglev train in Japan experiences unexpected lateral drift during a storm, veering slightly off its magnetic guideway. Engineers discover that turbulent wind forces—exerted at a 30° angle to the track—generate a horizontal drag that destabilizes the train’s alignment. The train, 200 m long with a mass of 450,000 kg, travels at 500 km/h. Air density is 1.2 kg/m³, and the effective frontal area is 120 m². Wind speed is 60 m/s at 30° to the direction of motion. Calculate the net transverse force acting on the train due to aerodynamic drag, and determine whether the magnetic suspension system (capable of correcting up to 7.5 MN of lateral force) can stabilize the train without active intervention.
