# HFDPO-07213

A quantum dot solar cell array uses precisely spaced nanoscale pillars (150 nm diameter, 800 nm tall) arranged in a hexagonal lattice to enhance light trapping via guided-mode resonance. The device operates under broadband sunlight (400–700 nm), but efficiency drops sharply at 550 nm. Given the refractive index of the semiconductor is 2.5 and the surrounding medium is air (n = 1.0), determine whether the observed efficiency dip is due to destructive interference in the first-order diffracted wave, and if so, what the lattice spacing must be to shift the minimum to 600 nm.
