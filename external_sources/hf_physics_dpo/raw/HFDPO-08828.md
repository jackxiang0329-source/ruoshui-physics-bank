# HFDPO-08828

In a high-altitude cryogenic storage facility, a researcher observes unexpected frost formation on the outer surface of a titanium alloy container holding liquid hydrogen (boiling point: 20.3 K), despite insulation rated for 40 K. The container’s exterior is exposed to ambient air at 298 K with a convective heat transfer coefficient of 12 W/m²·K. The titanium wall is 2.5 mm thick with thermal conductivity 22 W/m·K, and the insulation is 5 cm thick with κ = 0.025 W/m·K. The container’s inner surface is at 20.3 K, and the outer surface of insulation is at 80 K. Is the observed frost formation consistent with thermodynamic equilibrium? Evaluate whether a phase transition from vapor to solid (deposition) can occur at the insulation’s outer surface given the actual temperature gradient and surface conditions.
