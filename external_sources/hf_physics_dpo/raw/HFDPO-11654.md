# HFDPO-11654

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses a complex array of toroidal superconducting magnets to confine plasma at 150 million Kelvin. Engineers observe unexpected electric field fluctuations near the vessel’s outer casing, which could destabilize the plasma. The casing is a thin, hollow conductor with inner radius 3.2 m and outer radius 3.4 m, carrying a total charge of +1.2 × 10⁻⁴ C uniformly distributed on its outer surface. Using Gauss’s Law, determine whether the electric field inside the conductor (at r = 3.3 m) should be zero, and explain why the measured field near the casing’s inner surface deviates from theoretical predictions.
