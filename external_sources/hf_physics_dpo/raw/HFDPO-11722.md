# HFDPO-11722

A lunar lander descends toward a crater rim on the Moon, using retro-thrusters to slow its fall. At 500 m altitude, it has a vertical velocity of 12 m/s downward and begins a controlled deceleration phase at 2.5 m/s², but the thruster efficiency drops by 15% due to fuel sloshing. The lander’s mass is 1,800 kg, and its center of mass shifts 0.3 m vertically during the maneuver. To avoid tipping, the landing legs must remain within a 7° angular deviation from vertical. Determine the maximum allowable horizontal acceleration component caused by the shift in center of mass during deceleration.
