# HFDPO-10974

In a cryogenic quantum computing lab, researchers observe that a superconducting qubit array, cooled to 15 mK, exhibits anomalous decoherence rates during gate operations—significantly higher than predicted by standard spin-boson models. The qubits are coupled to a phononic bath in a 3D amorphous silicon substrate, where phonon modes are highly non-uniform due to microvoids (density fluctuations ≈ 1.2% rms). The system is described by a Hamiltonian with non-Markovian coupling to a structured bath. Estimate the effective dephasing rate due to phonon fluctuations, and determine whether the observed decoherence is dominated by low-energy acoustic phonons (ω < 100 GHz) or by localized defect modes near 500 GHz.
