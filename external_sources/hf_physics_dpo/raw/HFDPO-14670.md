# HFDPO-14670

A microfluidic device for early cancer detection relies on separating exosomes (≈100 nm diameter) from blood plasma using a spiral channel with a 5 mm radius and 10 μm height. As plasma flows at 1.2 mL/min through the 20 cm-long spiral, centrifugal forces and viscous drag work in tandem to isolate exosomes near the outer wall. However, the current design yields inconsistent separation due to unaccounted thermal fluctuations and shear-thinning behavior of plasma. Estimate the effective viscosity at the channel wall and determine the critical flow rate at which Brownian motion overwhelms shear-induced migration.
