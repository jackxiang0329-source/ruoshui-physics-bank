# HFDPO-07450

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a polarizing filter array to monitor ocean surface albedo for climate research, but data shows unexpected signal fluctuations during sunrise and sunset. The filters are designed to transmit only vertically polarized light, yet measurements indicate a 22% increase in horizontally polarized component under low-angle sunlight. Given that the ocean surface has a refractive index of 1.34 and the atmosphere causes Rayleigh scattering with 75% of scattered light polarized perpendicular to the scattering plane, determine whether the observed polarization shift is due to s-polarized reflection at the air-sea interface or atmospheric scattering, and quantify which mechanism dominates at a solar zenith angle of 80°.
