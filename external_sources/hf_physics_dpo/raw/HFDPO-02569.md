# HFDPO-02569

During a deep-sea hydrothermal vent expedition, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) retrieves a porous ceramic sample from a 2,500-meter-deep chimney structure. The sample, initially saturated with seawater at 4°C, is brought to the surface in a pressure-isolated chamber. Upon retrieval, researchers observe a sudden, violent expulsion of fluid—despite the chamber being sealed. The chamber’s internal pressure remains at 1 atm, yet the sample’s volume decreases by 1.2% during ascent. What caused this phenomenon, and how much internal pressure was trapped within the sample’s microstructure at depth?
