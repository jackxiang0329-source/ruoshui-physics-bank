# HFDPO-08114

A satellite orbiting Mars uses a polarimetric imager to detect subsurface ice deposits beneath dusty regolith. The instrument measures the degree of polarization of reflected sunlight at 550 nm, where dust scatters light diffusely but ice crystals exhibit strong birefringence. At a 60° incidence angle, the measured polarization degree is 0.32, but the theoretical prediction for pure dust is 0.24. Assuming the surface is a mixture of dust (polarization degree 0.24) and aligned ice crystals (polarization degree 0.68), determine the volume fraction of aligned ice needed to explain the observation, considering both Fresnel reflection and anisotropic scattering.
