# HFDPO-08067

A fusion reactor in a remote Arctic research station relies on a deuterium-tritium (D-T) fuel cycle, but tritium is not self-sustaining—its half-life is only 12.3 years. The station needs to maintain 5 kg of tritium for continuous operation over 10 years. Given that the reactor produces 10^18 fusion events per second (each yielding one tritium atom via neutron capture on lithium-6), and that only 70% of neutrons are available for breeding (the rest are lost to shielding or parasitic reactions), how much lithium-6 must be incorporated into the breeding blanket to sustain the fuel cycle? Assume each neutron produces one tritium atom with 85% efficiency.
