# HFDPO-03445

A Mars rover traversing the Valles Marineris canyon must descend a 25° incline covered in loose, angular basalt regolith with a coefficient of static friction μ_s = 0.45 and dynamic friction μ_k = 0.32. At 10 m/s, the rover’s drag coefficient Cd = 0.8 and frontal area A = 3.2 m². However, as it slows, the regolith compacts beneath its wheels, increasing μ_k to 0.52 while aerodynamic drag drops with v². Evaluate whether the rover can safely stop without skidding—using both energy conservation and force analysis—while accounting for variable friction and drag.
