# HFDPO-07800

A biomedical engineer is designing a wireless power transfer system for an implantable cardiac pacemaker, operating at 13.56 MHz. The system uses a primary coil (100 turns, 15 cm diameter) driven by a 5 V AC source with adjustable frequency and internal resistance of 3 Ω. The secondary coil (15 turns, 2 cm diameter) is embedded in the pacemaker casing, located 1 cm from the primary. Due to tissue inhomogeneity, the mutual inductance varies unpredictably, ranging from 40 nH to 90 nH. The pacemaker’s circuit requires a stable 3.3 V RMS across a 100 kΩ load impedance at resonance. Determine the required capacitance in the secondary resonant circuit to maintain efficient power transfer across the full range of mutual inductance, and explain why detuning could lead to device failure.
