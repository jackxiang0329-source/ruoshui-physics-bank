# HFDPO-03735

A lunar base in the Shackleton Crater uses a closed-loop thermal storage system to manage heat from solar collectors during the 14-day lunar day, storing excess energy in a high-temperature phase-change material (PCM) and releasing it during the 14-day night. The PCM has a latent heat of fusion of 210 kJ/kg and melts at 420 K. The system absorbs 12.6 MJ of solar energy during peak day, and the thermal losses to space (via radiation) are modeled as σAε(T⁴ – Tₑ⁴), where σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴, A = 12 m², ε = 0.85, and Tₑ = 3 K. The system must maintain internal temperature above 380 K for life support and equipment. What minimum mass of PCM is required to sustain the base through the night, assuming no additional input and that the PCM fully solidifies only when the internal temperature drops below 380 K?
