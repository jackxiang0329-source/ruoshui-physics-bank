# HFDPO-05049

A Mars rover’s thermal insulation system uses a porous ceramic aerogel layer to minimize heat loss in the frigid Martian night, where temperatures dip to –100°C. The aerogel’s microstructure contains randomly oriented nanoscale pores, each ~50 nm in diameter, filled with residual CO₂ gas trapped during manufacturing. During a dust storm, the pressure inside the pores briefly rises due to external compression. If the average CO₂ molecule in the pore has an average kinetic energy of 1.38 × 10⁻²⁰ J, what is the effective temperature of the trapped gas, and how does this affect the thermal conductivity of the aerogel layer?
