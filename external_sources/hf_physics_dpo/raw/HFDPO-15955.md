# HFDPO-15955

A lunar research outpost on the Moon’s south pole relies on a regolith-encased thermal battery to store excess solar energy for use during the 14-Earth-day-long lunar night. The battery consists of a 1000 kg bed of simulated lunar regolith (specific heat 800 J/kg·K, thermal conductivity 0.3 W/m·K) buried 2 m below the surface. During the day, it’s heated uniformly to 400 K using concentrated solar reflectors. As the temperature drops during the night, heat leaks out through the regolith and into the cold vacuum above. The surface is exposed to a constant 100 K radiative sink (via deep-space view). Determine whether the core temperature remains above 250 K for more than 100 hours, and evaluate how increasing the regolith depth to 3 m affects thermal retention.
