# HFDPO-15504

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole lined with superconducting coils to harvest energy from natural temperature gradients in the Earth's crust. The system relies on induced currents in a rotating, high-thermal-conductivity copper cylinder suspended in the borehole, which experiences a time-varying magnetic field due to a controlled, alternating current in the superconducting coil array. The cylinder’s rotation is driven by convection currents from geothermal heat, but friction and eddy losses are critical. Given a cylinder radius of 1.2 m, mass of 180 kg, and a magnetic field that varies sinusoidally at 15 Hz with peak amplitude 0.7 T, determine the minimum rotational speed (in RPM) the cylinder must achieve to generate a net power output of 4.5 MW, considering that 65% of the induced EMF is dissipated as eddy currents.
