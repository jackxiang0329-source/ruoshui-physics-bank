# HFDPO-08418

A quantum dot solar cell uses a periodic nanoarray of titanium dioxide pillars (150 nm diameter, 600 nm height) arranged in a hexagonal lattice with 800 nm spacing to enhance light absorption via guided modes and diffraction. When illuminated with broad-spectrum sunlight, a sharp peak in absorption is observed at 630 nm—just outside the visible range—but the cell’s efficiency drops significantly under low-angle morning light. Explain why this peak appears at 630 nm, and determine the effective order of diffraction responsible, assuming the incident light is normal to the surface. Consider both single-slit diffraction from the pillars and the grating-like behavior of the array.
