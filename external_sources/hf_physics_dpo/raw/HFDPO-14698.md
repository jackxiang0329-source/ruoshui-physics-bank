# HFDPO-14698

A lunar terraforming outpost uses a network of buried radiative heat exchangers to stabilize subsurface temperatures for growing extremophile crops in regolith. The system consists of 120 parallel, 10 m-long tubes (diameter 4 cm) filled with a high-thermal-conductivity liquid (k = 18 W/m·K), buried 3 m below the surface. During a 14-day lunar night, solar input ceases, and the tubes must maintain a minimum internal fluid temperature of 270 K to prevent freezing. The regolith has a variable thermal conductivity (k = 0.7 + 0.3*sin(θ) W/m·K, where θ is latitude), and the surface temperature drops from 120 K to 80 K over the night. Estimate the required flow rate (kg/s) to ensure the fluid exits the system at ≥270 K, assuming transient conduction dominates and neglecting internal convection resistance.
