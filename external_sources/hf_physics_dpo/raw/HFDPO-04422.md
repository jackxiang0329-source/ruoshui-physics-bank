# HFDPO-04422

During a deep-sea drilling operation, a rigid, cylindrical riser pipe (diameter 0.5 m, length 3 km) connects a floating platform to a subsea wellhead. The riser is filled with drilling mud (density 1,800 kg/m³) and surrounded by seawater (density 1,025 kg/m³). As ocean currents flow at 2.5 m/s past the riser, hydrodynamic forces induce vortex shedding, leading to periodic lateral oscillations. Engineers observe that at certain current speeds, the riser experiences resonance, causing excessive fatigue. Estimate the critical current speed at which vortex shedding frequency matches the riser’s fundamental natural frequency (1.2 Hz), and determine whether this speed is likely to occur in the operational zone (0.5–3.0 m/s). Assume the Strouhal number for vortex shedding from a cylinder is 0.21.
