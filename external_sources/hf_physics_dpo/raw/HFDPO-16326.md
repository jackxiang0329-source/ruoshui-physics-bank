# HFDPO-16326

In a next-generation quantum computing chip, a lattice of superconducting qubits is cooled to 15 mK to suppress thermal fluctuations. However, unexpected decoherence occurs when electron-hole pairs are excited via phonon-induced scattering, disrupting entanglement. The chip’s designer observes that coherence times drop below 50 μs when a magnetic field of 0.8 T is applied—despite the field being below the critical threshold for superconducting breakdown. Assuming the lattice is a 2D square array of NbN (niobium nitride) with a bandgap of 5.2 meV and effective mass of 0.2mₑ, determine whether the observed decoherence is due to a topological defect (a vortex) or phonon-mediated pair breaking, using only measurable coherence times and Fermi-Dirac statistics.
