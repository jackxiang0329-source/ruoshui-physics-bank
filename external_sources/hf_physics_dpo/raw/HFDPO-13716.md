# HFDPO-13716

A lunar surface rover equipped with a laser interferometer is attempting to map subsurface ice deposits beneath the Moon’s south pole, where permanent shadow zones create temperatures below 100 K. The instrument fires a 1550 nm infrared laser into a regolith layer suspected of containing ice-rich strata. However, interference fringes in the return signal show unexpected contrast decay and phase shifts after just 30 cm of propagation. The team suspects a nonlinear refractive index in the icy regolith due to quantum confinement effects in nanoscale ice grains. Given that the laser pulse has a 50 fs duration and peak power of 2 MW, determine the critical power threshold for self-focusing in the icy medium, assuming a nonlinear refractive index of n₂ = 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ cm²/W and a beam waist of 1 mm. Assess whether the observed optical distortions are consistent with self-focusing.
