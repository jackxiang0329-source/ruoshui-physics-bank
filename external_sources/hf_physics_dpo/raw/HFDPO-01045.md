# HFDPO-01045

A research team is designing a next-gen fusion reactor with a toroidal plasma chamber surrounded by a complex, non-uniform array of superconducting magnetic coils. To ensure plasma stability, they need to verify the net charge distribution within a critical 3D Gaussian surface embedded in the plasma boundary, but the charge density varies with radial distance and azimuthal angle as ρ(r,φ) = ρ₀e^(−r²/R²) sin²φ. The Gaussian surface is a spherical shell of radius R, centered at the torus’s midplane. However, the plasma is not symmetric—due to magnetic asymmetry, the charge distribution breaks spherical symmetry. What is the total enclosed charge? Can Gauss’s Law still simplify the analysis?
