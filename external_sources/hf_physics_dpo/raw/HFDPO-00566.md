# HFDPO-00566

A fusion reactor prototype using high-temperature superconducting (HTS) magnets in a compact tokamak design experiences unexpected flux jumps during ramp-up of the toroidal field, causing mechanical stress and quench alarms. The magnets, made of REBCO tapes wound into a 2 m diameter toroidal coil, are cooled to 40 K with a thermal link to a dilution refrigerator. Measurements show the critical current density *J<sub>c</sub>* varies with magnetic field as *J<sub>c</sub>(B) ∝ (B<sub>c2</sub> – B)<sup>α</sup>*, where *B<sub>c2</sub>* = 80 T and α ≈ 0.6. The peak field at the tape surface reaches 12 T during ramp-up. Design a quench protection strategy that balances current density limits, thermal stability, and energy dissipation—evaluating whether a 10% field ramp rate (1.2 T/s) is sustainable without triggering thermomagnetic instabilities.
