# HFDPO-04279

A quantum computing lab in Zurich is testing a new superconducting qubit design using a niobium-based loop with a critical temperature of 9.2 K. During operation, the system must remain below 4.5 K to avoid thermal quenches. The qubit is coupled to a cryogenic amplifier cooled by a dilution refrigerator, but a power fluctuation causes the temperature to spike briefly to 6.8 K. The loop has a self-inductance of 12 nH and carries a persistent current of 150 nA. Will the qubit remain in a superconducting state during the spike? What happens to the magnetic flux trapped in the loop if the superconductivity is lost and then restored?
