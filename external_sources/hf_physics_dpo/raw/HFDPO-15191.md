# HFDPO-15191

In a cutting-edge quantum imaging experiment, researchers use a single-photon source to probe a nanoscale biological sample with minimal radiation damage. The photons are directed through a double-slit interferometer embedded in a cryogenic photonic chip, but the interference pattern vanishes when a detector is placed near one slit to determine which path the photon took. However, when the detector is switched to a "weak measurement" mode—low interaction strength—partial interference re-emerges. Given that the visibility of the fringes drops from 0.95 (no path info) to 0.30 (with path detection), and the weak measurement has a coupling strength of lambda = 1.2 times 10^{-3} , calculate the minimum number of such weak measurements required to recover near-complete visibility, assuming decoherence scales quadratically with measurement strength.
