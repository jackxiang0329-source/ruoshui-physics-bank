# HFDPO-01408

NASA’s Mars Sample Return mission is in crisis—its Earth-return orbiter, in a 200 km circular orbit around Mars, must execute a precisely timed Hohmann transfer to rendezvous with a sample capsule currently in a 400 km elliptical orbit. The capsule’s pericenter is 300 km, apocenter 400 km, and it’s currently at pericenter moving at 3.58 km/s. The orbiter’s propulsion system is failing, and only one burn is possible. Calculate the Δv required for the orbiter to intercept the capsule at pericenter, and determine whether a prograde or retrograde burn is optimal. Include the effects of Mars’ non-spherical gravity (J₂ = 6.68 × 10⁻⁴) on orbital perturbations over the transfer window.
