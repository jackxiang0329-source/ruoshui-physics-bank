# HFDPO-00482

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole 3.2 km deep to extract heat from basaltic rock. Engineers install a superconducting loop at the bottom, suspended by a fiber-optic tether, to measure Earth's magnetic field variations due to solar-driven geomagnetic storms. The loop has a diameter of 1.8 m and is oriented horizontally. During a sudden geomagnetic disturbance, the magnetic field changes from 52 μT to 47 μT over 7.5 seconds, inducing a current pulse. The system's quantum-flux-probe detects the induced current, but the signal is noisy. What is the peak induced current in the loop, and how does the temporal shape of the field change affect the pulse?
