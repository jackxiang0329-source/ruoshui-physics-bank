# HFDPO-06931

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a submerged, irregularly shaped hydrothermal vent chimney made of porous mineral deposits. The chimney exerts a buoyant force that destabilizes its position as it descends through a stratified seawater column (density varies with depth due to temperature and salinity gradients). The ROV must adjust its ballast system to maintain stable hovering near the chimney's base. Given the chimney’s volume is 3.2 m³, its average density is 2,800 kg/m³, and the surrounding seawater density increases from 1,025 kg/m³ at the surface to 1,048 kg/m³ at 2,500 m depth, determine the depth at which the chimney’s net buoyant force reverses direction—i.e., when it transitions from being buoyant to sinking relative to its local environment.
