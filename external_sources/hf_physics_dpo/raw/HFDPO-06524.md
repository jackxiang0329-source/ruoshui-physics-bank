# HFDPO-06524

A solar-powered desalination unit in a remote coastal village uses a porous membrane with nanoscale pores (diameter ≈ 2 nm) to separate salt ions from seawater under thermal gradients. The efficiency depends critically on the probability that water molecules pass through the pores while ions are blocked. Given that the average thermal speed of water molecules at 30°C is 600 m/s and the mean free path in the membrane is 10 nm, estimate the transmission probability of water molecules through a single pore, assuming Knudsen diffusion dominates.
