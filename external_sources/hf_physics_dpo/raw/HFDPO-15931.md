# HFDPO-15931

A deep-sea research team deploys a spherical pressure sensor array (diameter 1.2 m) to monitor hydrothermal vent activity at 2,800 m depth in the Pacific Ocean. The array is tethered by a flexible cable with linear density 0.85 kg/m and is designed to remain neutrally buoyant when fully submerged. However, during preliminary tests, the system tilts and drifts upward despite being rated for neutral buoyancy. The team suspects the cable's weight distribution and the non-uniform pressure gradient are altering hydrostatic equilibrium. Determine whether the buoyant force on the sensor array is actually greater than, less than, or equal to the total effective weight of the system (including submerged cable) and explain how this imbalance affects stability.
