# HFDPO-02250

A Mars rover traversing a sandy dune encounters a sudden 15° incline with regolith exhibiting a variable coefficient of friction that increases nonlinearly with depth due to compaction under the rover’s weight. The rover’s front axle applies a constant torque of 120 N·m to its drive wheels (radius 0.3 m), but the rear wheels slip when the frictional force exceeds static limits. Given that the rover has a mass of 220 kg, center of mass 1.1 m behind the front axle, and moment of inertia of 45 kg·m² about its center of mass, determine the maximum incline angle before the rover loses traction at the front and tips forward simultaneously. Assume the dune’s surface has a depth-dependent μ_s = 0.3 + 0.02z (z in meters below surface), and the wheels sink 0.05 m under load.
