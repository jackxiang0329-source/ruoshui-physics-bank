# HFDPO-02493

A lunar research station uses a closed-loop gas circulation system to maintain breathable air in its habitat module. The system relies on a high-efficiency molecular sieve that selectively removes CO₂ by adsorbing it at low temperatures, then releasing it upon thermal cycling. During a 48-hour test, engineers notice a gradual 12% rise in internal pressure despite no gas input. The module’s volume is 85 m³, initial pressure is 101.3 kPa, and temperature is held constant at 295 K. The sieve’s regeneration cycle heats the trapped CO₂ to 420 K for 10 minutes before venting. Assuming ideal behavior and that only CO₂ is released during regeneration, estimate the average number of CO₂ molecules desorbed per cycle and explain why the internal pressure increases over time.
