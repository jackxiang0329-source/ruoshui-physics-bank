# HFDPO-11523

A fusion reactor's first wall is made of a functionally graded material (FGM) with thermal conductivity varying exponentially from 20 W/m·K at the plasma-facing surface to 5 W/m·K at the coolant interface over a 10 cm thickness. During a 10-second high-power pulse, the surface absorbs 1.2 MW/m² of heat flux. Simultaneously, the coolant maintains a constant 120°C at the back face. What is the peak temperature rise in the FGM layer, and how does the temperature gradient profile compare to a uniform-material wall of equivalent average conductivity?
