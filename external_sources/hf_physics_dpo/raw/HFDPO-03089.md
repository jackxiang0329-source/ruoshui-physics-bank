# HFDPO-03089

A fusion reactor in a remote Arctic research station experiences anomalous neutron flux spikes during startup, despite the plasma remaining stable and confined within the magnetic field. The reactor uses a deuterium-tritium (D-T) fuel mix, and neutron detectors record a 27% increase in fast neutron emission (E > 1 MeV) over expected values during the initial heating phase. However, the total fusion power output (measured via thermal load) shows only a 5% rise. Using neutron energy spectra, gamma-ray activation data from surrounding structural materials, and estimates of tritium breeding efficiency, determine the likely mechanism behind the excess neutron emission—specifically whether it stems from (1) parasitic neutron production from inelastic scattering in the first-wall shielding, or (2) a hidden non-D-T fusion reaction pathway involving trace helium-3 impurities.
