# HFDPO-01041

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a submerged volcanic vent emitting superheated, low-density hydrothermal fluid at 350°C into cold, high-pressure seawater at 2°C. The vent opens into a 1.2 m diameter chimney with a 4.5 m vertical height. Engineers observe that the rising plume begins to destabilize and break into discrete buoyant bubbles above the chimney’s exit, threatening sensor accuracy. Given that the average seawater density at depth is 1035 kg/m³, the hydrothermal fluid density is 970 kg/m³, and atmospheric pressure at the surface is 101.3 kPa, determine the minimum critical height (from the vent opening) at which the plume transitions from stable, continuous rise to fragmented, bubbly flow due to Rayleigh-Taylor instability. Use concepts of hydrostatic pressure, buoyancy gradients, and density stratification.
