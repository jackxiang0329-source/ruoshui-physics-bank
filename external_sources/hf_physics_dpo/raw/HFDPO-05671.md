# HFDPO-05671

A spacecraft’s thermal management system uses a phase-change material (PCM) in its hull to absorb radiant heat from a distant star (1200 K). The PCM, encapsulated in a 1.5 m³ aluminum matrix, undergoes melting at 480 K with a latent heat of 180 kJ/kg and a density of 890 kg/m³. During a 45-minute solar flare exposure, the system must absorb 12 MJ of energy without exceeding 500 K. Is the PCM reservoir sufficient? If not, what minimum additional mass of a secondary PCM (latent heat 210 kJ/kg, same melting point) must be added?
