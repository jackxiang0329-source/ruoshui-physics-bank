# HFDPO-07416

A neural implant designed to treat epilepsy uses a micro-scale capacitor array to deliver precise, localized electrical stimuli to brain tissue. The array consists of 100 rectangular plates, each 20 μm × 10 μm, arranged in parallel with 1 μm thick insulating layers of phospholipid bilayer (ε_r = 5). Due to biological degradation, the dielectric thickness increases non-uniformly over time—by 0.2 μm in the center but only 0.05 μm at the edges—leading to a spatially varying electric field. Engineers observe a 30% drop in effective capacitance after 6 months. What is the new effective capacitance, and how does the non-uniform dielectric thickness affect the energy distribution across the array?
