# HFDPO-14368

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses a central receiver tower with molten salt (NaNO₃/KNO₃) as both heat transfer fluid and thermal storage. At peak operation, the tower receives 20 MW of solar flux over a 15 m diameter receiver surface, heating the salt from 290°C to 565°C. However, during a sudden cloud cover, the solar input drops to zero. The salt is stored in a cylindrical tank (height 10 m, diameter 6 m) with negligible heat loss to surroundings initially. Using a heat exchanger, the salt must maintain 400°C output for 6 hours to keep turbines running. Estimate the minimum required thermal capacity of the storage tank to sustain this load, considering the salt’s temperature-dependent specific heat: c_p(T) = 1.4 + 0.001T (J/g·K), where T is in °C.
