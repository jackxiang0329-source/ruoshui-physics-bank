# HFDPO-11172

A remote Arctic research station relies on a hybrid heat engine system combining a Stirling cycle generator powered by a small nuclear heat source and a thermoelectric module harvesting waste heat from the engine’s exhaust. The system operates with a hot reservoir at 650°C and ambient air at -20°C, but due to ice buildup on the heat exchanger fins, the effective cold reservoir temperature rises to -5°C during winter storms. Calculate the maximum theoretical efficiency drop and determine whether the station can still meet its 15 kW power demand under these conditions, assuming the generator’s actual efficiency is 60% of Carnot.
