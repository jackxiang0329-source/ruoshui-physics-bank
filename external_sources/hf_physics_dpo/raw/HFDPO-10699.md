# HFDPO-10699

During a volcanic eruption, molten rock (lava) flows down a slope and solidifies into a basaltic crust. Scientists observe that the solidified rock fractures into rectangular columns, like those at Giant’s Causeway. If the thermal contraction stress exceeds the rock’s tensile strength (approximately 10 MPa), cracks form. Given the rock’s Young’s modulus is 30 GPa and the coefficient of thermal expansion is 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ /°C, what temperature drop from molten state to solid state would just trigger cracking?
