# HFDPO-00394

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical, cylindrical coil (radius 1.2 m, 300 turns) buried 1.5 km underground to monitor subtle changes in the Earth’s magnetic field caused by magma movement. During a seismic event, the horizontal component of Earth’s field (normally 50 μT) increases linearly at 1.8 × 10⁻⁹ T/s. The coil is connected to a superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) amplifier with negligible resistance. Calculate the induced current in the coil at the peak rate of change, assuming the coil’s self-inductance is 0.8 H and the internal resistance is 0.05 Ω.
