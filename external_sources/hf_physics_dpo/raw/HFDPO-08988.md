# HFDPO-08988

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole (depth 2.5 km) to circulate water through a heat exchanger at the bottom, where it heats up and rises due to convection. The well is surrounded by a borehole liner made of conductive material, and the plant's engineers notice a small but persistent voltage (12 V) generated between the top and bottom of the liner during operation—despite no explicit power source. They suspect this arises from Earth’s magnetic field (50 μT, inclined 70° from horizontal) interacting with the moving conductive fluid. Estimate the average upward velocity of the fluid if the liner's effective diameter is 0.3 m and the voltage is due to motional EMF across the conductor.
