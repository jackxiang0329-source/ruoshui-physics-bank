# HFDPO-09724

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a network of buried copper pipes (0.5 cm diameter, 150 m long) to extract heat from underground rock, but engineers notice that the electrical resistance of the pipe system increases over time—despite no visible damage. The initial resistance is 0.02 Ω, but after 18 months of operation in a high-mineral-content water environment, it rises to 0.042 Ω. Given that the pipes are in a 150°C environment and the temperature coefficient of resistance for copper is 0.00393 /°C, calculate the actual temperature rise in the pipe due to both the ambient geothermal heat and resistive heating from a 500 A current running through it. Assume the pipe has uniform cross-section and the material’s resistivity increases with temperature, but corrosion has also added an unintended resistive layer—estimate the equivalent resistance contributed by this degradation.
