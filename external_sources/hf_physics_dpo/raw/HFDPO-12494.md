# HFDPO-12494

A fusion reactor prototype uses a compact toroidal superconducting magnet operating at 4.2 K, stabilized by a 100-μm thick Nb₃Sn layer with critical current density Jc = 1.5 × 10⁶ A/m². During a quench event, local heating raises the temperature to 6.5 K over 20 ms in a 2 cm² cross-section of the winding. If the heat capacity of the coil is 2.4 × 10⁴ J/K and the heat generated is entirely thermal, will the material remain superconducting or trigger a persistent resistive zone? Consider competing effects: the decrease in Jc with temperature and the self-induced magnetic field from trapped current.
