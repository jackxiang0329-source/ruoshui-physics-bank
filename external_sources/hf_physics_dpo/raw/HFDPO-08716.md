# HFDPO-08716

A deep-space communication array on Mars receives a faint signal from Earth during a solar storm, but the signal’s polarization is scrambled. The signal, transmitted at 8.4 GHz with linear polarization, has traveled 225 million km through interplanetary plasma with a variable electron density. The array measures the signal’s Stokes parameters but cannot determine the original polarization state due to Faraday rotation. Given the observed polarization angle shift of 45° and assuming uniform plasma density along the path, calculate the total electron column density in the interplanetary medium and evaluate whether the observed Faraday rotation is consistent with solar wind models.
