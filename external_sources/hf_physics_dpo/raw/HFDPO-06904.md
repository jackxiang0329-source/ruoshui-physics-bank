# HFDPO-06904

In a next-generation semiconductor fabrication facility, charged dust particles (radius 2.5 μm, charge +1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) are suspended in a vacuum chamber during a critical deposition step. The chamber's walls are held at -150 V relative to the central electrode, while the dust is initially at rest 15 cm from the wall. Due to electrostatic forces, the dust accelerates toward the wall, but surface charging effects cause its charge to fluctuate unpredictably. The process fails if the dust hits the wall with kinetic energy exceeding 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. Determine whether the process is safe under the given conditions, assuming constant charge, and analyze how a 10% charge variation might change the outcome.
