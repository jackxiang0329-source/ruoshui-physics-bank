# HFDPO-01889

During a heatwave, a solar-powered desalination unit in a remote coastal village starts producing brine that’s too hot to handle safely—reaching 85°C. The system uses 200 W of solar power to heat seawater, but the cooling fin array attached to the condenser is underperforming. Engineers estimate that heat loss through convection and radiation to the ambient air (25°C) is insufficient to maintain safe brine temperatures. What minimum effective surface area (in m²) must the fin array have to keep the brine below 80°C, assuming heat transfer coefficients are 15 W/m²·K for natural convection and 5 W/m²·K for radiation? Assume the fins are at the same temperature as the brine.
