# HFDPO-02100

A solar farm in rural Nevada experiences erratic voltage drops during peak sunlight, disrupting grid stability. Engineers discover that a faulty inverter has reconfigured the local network: three identical 500 kW solar arrays, each with internal resistance of 1.2 Ω, are connected in parallel to a central bus, but one array’s diode bridge failed, creating a reverse current path through a 3.5 Ω shunt resistor instead of the intended load. The bus voltage sags to 460 V instead of the expected 500 V. Use Kirchhoff’s laws to determine the reverse current through the faulty array and identify the total power loss in the system due to the fault.
