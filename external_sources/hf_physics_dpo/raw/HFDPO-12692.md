# HFDPO-12692

A quantum dot solar cell uses a nanoscale grating (spacing 450 nm) on its surface to enhance light absorption. The grating is designed to couple incident sunlight (λ = 600 nm, normal incidence) into guided modes via diffraction, but the efficiency drops sharply at angles beyond ±20° due to destructive interference in the far field. Engineers notice unexpected dips in the absorption spectrum at 600 nm and 640 nm when measured at 15° incidence. Determine whether these dips arise from single-slit diffraction minima, interference fringes from the grating, or a combination—using both the grating equation and the single-slit diffraction envelope.
