# HFDPO-00718

A solar-powered spacecraft in deep space uses a thin, nanostructured membrane to capture interstellar hydrogen atoms (≈1 atom/cm³) traveling at ~50 km/s. The membrane’s surface is designed to trap atoms via inelastic collisions, but overheating from kinetic energy transfer threatens material integrity. If the membrane has an area of 10 m² and the atoms reflect with 80% of their incident kinetic energy per collision (treated as partial inelastic collisions), what is the average thermal power (in watts) dissipated into the membrane per second? Assume the atoms are monatomic hydrogen and are isotropic in velocity distribution.
