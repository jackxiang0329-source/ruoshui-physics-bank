# HFDPO-15696

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical 500-meter-long cylindrical coil (radius 1.2 m, 1500 turns) buried in hot rock to harvest induced currents from natural, slowly varying magnetic fields generated by convective magma movements. The magnetic field component perpendicular to the coil's face varies sinusoidally with time as B(t) = B₀ sin(ωt), where B₀ = 1.8 μT and ω = 2π × 10⁻⁵ rad/s. Due to the coil’s high resistance (35 Ω), energy lost as heat must be minimized while maximizing useful power extraction. Engineers propose rotating the coil at 120 rpm to amplify induced EMF via motion. Evaluate whether rotating the coil significantly improves net power output compared to the stationary case, and determine the optimal rotation axis orientation (axial vs. transverse) for maximum efficiency under realistic constraints.
