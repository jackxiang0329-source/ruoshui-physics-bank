# HFDPO-09413

A malfunctioning solar microgrid on a remote research station in Antarctica relies on a network of 12 identical photovoltaic panels connected in a complex mesh with 8 rechargeable lithium-ion batteries and variable-load inverters. During a 72-hour polar night, three panels fail due to ice accumulation, reducing panel output to 60% of nominal voltage (24 V each). Engineers must redistribute power using Kirchhoff’s Laws to prevent battery over-discharge. The system includes two parallel 12 V and three 48 V load branches with variable resistances due to temperature. Determine the current through the 48 V branch and explain how adjusting one battery’s internal resistance (originally 0.8 Ω) to 1.5 Ω alters the power balance across the entire network.
