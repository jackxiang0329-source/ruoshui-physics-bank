# HFDPO-02814

A research team is designing a small-scale geothermal power system for a remote village in Iceland, using a binary cycle with isobutane as the working fluid. The geothermal source provides 25°C water at 10 kg/s, which heats the isobutane in a heat exchanger. The isobutane enters the turbine at 180°C and 4 MPa, expands to 0.6 MPa, and exits as a saturated vapor. The turbine has an isentropic efficiency of 88%, and the condenser rejects heat to the ambient air at 10°C. The system must produce at least 450 kW of net power. Evaluate whether the cycle can meet this requirement, and if not, suggest one design change that would improve performance without exceeding the available heat source temperature.
