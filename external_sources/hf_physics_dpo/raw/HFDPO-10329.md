# HFDPO-10329

A lunar lander descends toward a crater rim on the Moon at 3.2 m/s, using a retro-thruster providing 1,800 N of thrust. The lander’s mass is 1,450 kg, and it must halt within 50 m of the surface to avoid impact. The Moon’s gravity is 1.62 m/s². However, a small fault line beneath the landing site causes a 2° tilt in the surface, and the thruster’s exhaust is slightly misaligned—resulting in a 4° angle between the thrust vector and the vertical. Can the lander stop safely without tipping? Evaluate the net force components, determine if the horizontal thrust component induces tipping, and assess whether angular momentum conservation or torque analysis is more appropriate for validating stability.
