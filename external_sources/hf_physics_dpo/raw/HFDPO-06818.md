# HFDPO-06818

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole lined with superconducting coils to harvest induced currents from naturally occurring geomagnetic field fluctuations caused by solar wind interactions. The coil system, 1.2 km long with 3000 turns, is wound around a cylindrical borehole of radius 0.8 m. During a geomagnetic storm, the vertical component of Earth’s magnetic field changes at a rate of 35 nT/s. The superconducting loop maintains zero resistance but has a self-inductance of 15 mH. Determine the maximum induced current and the peak rate of energy dissipation that would occur if the system were not superconducting. Explain how the time-varying magnetic flux couples to the coil’s inductance and why the actual current is limited by mutual inductance with Earth’s field rather than simple Faraday’s law.
