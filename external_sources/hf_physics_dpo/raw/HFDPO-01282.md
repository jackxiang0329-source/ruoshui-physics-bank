# HFDPO-01282

A fusion reactor's magnetic confinement system uses a complex array of superconducting coils to stabilize a plasma toroid at 15 million Kelvin. One critical coil, shaped as a helix with 500 turns over 2 meters, carries 20 kA of current. Engineers observe a slight drift in the plasma boundary, suspected to stem from a 0.3% asymmetry in the axial magnetic field due to a misaligned current distribution. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the net magnetic field component along the toroidal axis at the center of the helix, assuming uniform current distribution, and explain why even a small field asymmetry could cause instabilities in plasma confinement.
