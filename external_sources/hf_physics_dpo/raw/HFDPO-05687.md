# HFDPO-05687

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses hot rock formations at 300°C to heat water into steam, which drives turbines. Engineers must calculate the minimum depth at which the rock must be accessed to sustain a 50 MW power output, given that the thermal efficiency is limited by the Carnot cycle and the ambient ground temperature is 15°C. The rock’s heat capacity is 800 J/kg·K, density is 2700 kg/m³, and the drill extracts rock at a rate of 100 kg/s. How deep must the borehole go to maintain this power indefinitely, assuming all extracted heat is converted with maximum theoretical efficiency and no heat loss during transport?
