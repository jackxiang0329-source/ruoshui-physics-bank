# HFDPO-15107

A bioengineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from whole blood using deterministic lateral displacement (DLD) in a periodic array of posts. The device operates at 37°C with a flow rate of 50 μL/min through a 50 μm-wide channel. Blood has a viscosity of 3.5 mPa·s (vs. 1.0 mPa·s for water), and CTCs are modeled as 15 μm rigid spheres with density 1060 kg/m³. The posts (diameter 10 μm, spacing 20 μm) induce complex viscous flows that create lateral migration. Determine the minimum post spacing required to ensure CTCs are separated from 7 μm erythrocytes (density 1080 kg/m³) via inertial effects amplified by viscosity, assuming the flow is laminar and the Reynolds number is below 1.
