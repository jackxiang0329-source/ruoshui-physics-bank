# HFDPO-02276

A deep-space communications array on Mars receives a faint signal from Earth — 210 million km away — at 1.2 GHz, but the signal's phase is scrambled due to ionospheric turbulence during transmission. The array uses phased antennas with 1.5 m spacing. Determine the minimum time delay between adjacent antennas needed to compensate for atmospheric phase distortions caused by variable electron density in Earth’s ionosphere, assuming a peak electron density gradient of 3×10¹⁴ m⁻⁴ and a 20 km vertical thickness of the disturbed layer.
