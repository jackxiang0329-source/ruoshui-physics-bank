# HFDPO-03817

A portable wind turbine used in remote Arctic monitoring stations generates power via a rotating permanent magnet inside a stationary coil array, but its output drops unexpectedly during sudden temperature fluctuations. The turbine’s rotor spins at 150 rpm, and the magnetic flux through each of its 2400 turns varies sinusoidally with time as Φ(t) = Φ₀ cos(ωt), where Φ₀ = 2.8 × 10⁻⁴ Wb and ω = 2π × 2.5 rad/s. However, during a cold snap, the coil wires contract by 0.35%, changing the effective area and altering the induced EMF. What is the magnitude of the average induced EMF over one cycle *before and after* the contraction, and why might the power output drop by up to 12% despite constant rotational speed?
