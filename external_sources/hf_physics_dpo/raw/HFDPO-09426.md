# HFDPO-09426

A coastal city faces recurring flooding during storm surges, but traditional seawalls fail to prevent water infiltration through a network of underground storm drains. Engineers observe that during peak surge, water backs up into the drain system and exits at a significantly lower rate than expected, despite identical inlet and outlet geometries. The system consists of a 500 m long, 1.2 m diameter concrete pipe with a 0.02 m² cross-sectional blockage from sediment buildup. The water level outside the city rises 3.5 m above the drain inlet, while the outlet is 1.8 m below sea level. Using unsteady flow modeling, determine the time it takes for the internal water level to reach equilibrium, considering viscous dissipation, turbulent eddies, and compressibility effects in the trapped air pocket above the water column.
