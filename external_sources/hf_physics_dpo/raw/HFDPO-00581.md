# HFDPO-00581

A biomedical engineer is developing a capacitive sensor to detect early-stage lung edema by measuring changes in thoracic capacitance during breathing. The sensor uses two flexible, conductive electrodes attached to the chest wall, forming a parallel-plate capacitor with a variable dielectric (lung tissue and air). During inhalation, the lung volume increases by 1.2 L, reducing the average distance between electrodes from 5.0 mm to 4.2 mm while the effective plate area remains 80 cm². The dielectric constant of lung tissue varies from 12 (fluid-rich) to 7 (healthy air-filled). If the baseline capacitance (healthy lungs) is measured at 18.5 pF, what is the minimum detectable change in capacitance due to a 15% increase in lung fluid (dielectric constant rising to 13.8), and how does this compare to the change from breathing alone? Can the sensor distinguish between fluid buildup and normal respiration?
