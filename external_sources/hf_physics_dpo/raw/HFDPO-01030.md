# HFDPO-01030

A fusion reactor's magnetic confinement chamber uses a toroidal plasma with a non-uniform charge density that varies radially as ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 − r/R₀), where r is the radial distance from the torus center and R₀ = 1.2 m is the major radius. Engineers need to determine the electric field at a point inside the plasma, r = 0.6 m, using Gauss’s Law. The system is symmetric under rotation around the torus axis, but the charge distribution breaks spherical symmetry—what closed surface should be used, and how does the field depend on the radial coordinate despite the lack of spherical symmetry?
