# HFDPO-03733

A research team studying ice dynamics in polar regions discovers that a 2.5-meter-thick ice shelf is experiencing accelerated melting due to warm ocean currents. The ice shelf is bounded by a subglacial cavity where seawater at 2.8°C flows beneath it. Given the ice’s thermal conductivity is 2.2 W/m·K, the latent heat of fusion is 3.34×10⁵ J/kg, and the density of ice is 917 kg/m³, determine the minimum steady-state heat flux (in W/m²) required to sustain the observed 1.2 m/year melting rate at the ice-ocean interface. Assume the ice temperature at the interface is exactly 0°C and neglect convection effects in the ice.
