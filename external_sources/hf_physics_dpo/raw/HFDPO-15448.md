# HFDPO-15448

A renewable energy startup is designing a vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) for urban rooftops, where space is limited and wind turbulence is high. The turbine’s central support column is anchored to a reinforced concrete foundation, but vibrations during high winds threaten structural integrity. Engineers must determine the minimum required base torque resistance to prevent rotation under a worst-case wind load of 1,200 N applied at a 3.2 m height, acting horizontally. The foundation's concrete has a known coefficient of static friction (μ_s = 0.65) with the soil beneath, and the base diameter is 1.4 m. However, uneven soil settlement could reduce effective friction on one side—how much of the torque can be safely resisted by friction alone, and what minimum additional anchoring torque is required to ensure stability under extreme gusts?
