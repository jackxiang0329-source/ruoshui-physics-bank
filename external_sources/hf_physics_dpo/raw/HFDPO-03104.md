# HFDPO-03104

A deep-space communications relay satellite, traveling at 0.8c relative to Earth, transmits a burst of data encoded in microwave pulses. An Earth-based observatory detects the signal after a 5.0-year delay (Earth time) from when the satellite began transmitting. However, due to relativistic Doppler shift and time dilation, the signal's frequency and duration are distorted. The satellite’s clock records a 3.0-year transmission window. Calculate the actual duration of the signal as measured by Earth, and explain whether the data burst appears longer or shorter to Earth observers compared to the satellite’s own clock—then defend your reasoning with two distinct relativistic arguments.
