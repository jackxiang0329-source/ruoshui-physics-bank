# HFDPO-04000

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses deep boreholes to extract heat from volcanic rock formations. The surrounding rock, with a Young’s modulus of 35 GPa and Poisson’s ratio of 0.25, experiences stress changes due to fluid injection at 4,500 m depth. Engineers detect microfractures in the formation after sustained injection, despite the rock's apparent strength. What is the critical stress state that triggers brittle failure, and how does the anisotropic nature of the rock—evidenced by differential strain in orthogonal borehole directions—reflect underlying elastic inhomogeneity?
