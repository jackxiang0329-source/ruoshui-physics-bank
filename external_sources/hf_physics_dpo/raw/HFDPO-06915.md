# HFDPO-06915

A marine biologist is deploying a passive acoustic monitoring array in a coral reef environment to track whale communication across a 5-km stretch of ocean. The array uses hydrophones spaced 200 m apart, but data shows interference patterns in low-frequency signals (<100 Hz) that distort signal timing. The water temperature varies from 24°C at the surface to 18°C at depth, creating a stable thermal gradient. The biologist suspects sound refraction due to the temperature gradient is bending the path of sound, causing phase mismatches between sensors. Given that sound speed in seawater is approximated by c = 1449 + 4.6T - 0.055T^2 + 0.00031S (where T is temperature in °C and S is salinity in psu, assumed constant at 35), and the thermal gradient is linear with depth, calculate the curved path of a 75 Hz sound wave from a whale at 100 m depth to a hydrophone at 150 m depth. Estimate the time delay difference between expected and actual arrival times due to refraction, and explain how this affects detection accuracy.
