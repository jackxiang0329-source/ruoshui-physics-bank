# HFDPO-04545

In a high-voltage plasma reactor used for synthesizing carbon nanotubes, a pulsed electric field generates filamentary discharges between two parallel graphite electrodes separated by 2.0 mm. During each pulse, a transient current of 150 A flows for 100 ns, creating a localized plasma with a measured electron density of 5×10¹⁷ m⁻³. The system’s capacitance must be dynamically adjusted to match the changing plasma impedance during the pulse. Given that the effective permittivity of the plasma channel is 0.8ε₀ and the electrode area is 5 cm², determine the time-varying capacitance during the discharge and explain why a fixed capacitor would fail to maintain optimal energy transfer.
