# HFDPO-01729

A quantum computing research team is developing a superconducting qubit housed within a cylindrical cryostat shielded by a thin, highly conductive niobium shell. The shell’s inner radius is 15 cm, and it carries a uniform surface charge density of 2.5 × 10⁻⁹ C/m² due to stray electron accumulation during cooldown. To avoid interference, they must ensure the electric field inside the qubit cavity stays below 1.0 V/m. Use Gauss’s Law to determine whether the observed field of 0.8 V/m at a radial distance of 12 cm from the axis is consistent with the charge distribution, and explain the physical origin of the field if it exists.
