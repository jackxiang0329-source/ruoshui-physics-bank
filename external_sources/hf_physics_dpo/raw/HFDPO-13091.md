# HFDPO-13091

During a winter storm, a city’s water pipes in older neighborhoods begin to freeze, causing bursts. Engineers notice that while most pipes fail at around -5°C, some insulated sections remain intact even when ambient temperatures drop to -12°C. The insulated pipes contain a mixture of water and a proprietary antifreeze compound that lowers the freezing point but also alters the heat capacity. Given the heat of fusion of ice (334 kJ/kg), the specific heat of water (4.18 kJ/kg·K), and assuming the insulated pipe initially contains 0.8 kg of water at 0°C, how much antifreeze (in kg) must be added to delay freezing until -12°C, assuming the mixture’s heat capacity drops to 3.7 kJ/kg·K and no supercooling occurs?
