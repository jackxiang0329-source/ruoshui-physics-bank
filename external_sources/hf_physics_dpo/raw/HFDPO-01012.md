# HFDPO-01012

A Mars rover’s suspension system uses a new polymer-coated wheel designed to reduce rolling resistance on regolith, but during a 10-km traverse, the rover's power draw unexpectedly spikes by 42% in the final kilometer. The wheels have a radius of 0.35 m, mass 15 kg each, and the rover’s total mass is 220 kg. The coefficient of rolling friction varies nonlinearly with speed due to dynamic compaction of the Martian soil, modeled as μ_r = 0.03 + 0.0004v² (v in m/s). In addition, aerodynamic drag from Mars’ thin atmosphere (ρ ≈ 0.020 kg/m³, C_dA = 0.8 m²) becomes significant above 0.5 m/s. The rover maintains a constant 0.8 m/s during the final kilometer. Calculate the total energy lost to friction and drag over that segment, and explain why the power draw increased despite constant speed.
