# HFDPO-01349

A fusion reactor's tokamak design uses a pulsed toroidal magnetic field to confine plasma, but a sudden drop in current through the primary field coils induces eddy currents in the conductive vacuum vessel walls. At t = 0, the primary field collapses from 12 T to 0 T in 2.5 ms. The vessel has a wall thickness of 18 cm, resistivity 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m, and is made of 316L stainless steel. If the induced power dissipation exceeds 500 kW/m³, localized melting occurs. Estimate whether the vessel will survive the quench event.
