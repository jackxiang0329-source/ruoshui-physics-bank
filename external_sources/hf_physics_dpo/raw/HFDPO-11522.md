# HFDPO-11522

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a laser communication array to transmit high-bandwidth data to a ground station, but intermittent signal degradation occurs during atmospheric passage. The laser operates at 1550 nm, with a divergence of 0.5 mrad, and the beam’s coherence length is 30 km. During a critical transmission, atmospheric turbulence causes beam wander and scintillation. The receiver on the ground has a 10 cm aperture, and the link distance is 600 km. Given that the turbulence strength (Cₙ²) follows a log-normal distribution with a mean of 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ m⁻²/³ and a standard deviation of 0.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ m⁻²/³, determine the expected signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) degradation due to atmospheric scintillation relative to the ideal free-space case, and propose a coherence-based mitigation strategy.
