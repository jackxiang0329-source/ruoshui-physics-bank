# HFDPO-04896

During a deep-sea exploration mission, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) encounters a dense, slow-moving plume of sediment-laden water exiting a hydrothermal vent at 300 m depth. The plume has a radius of 1.2 m and rises vertically at 0.8 m/s, but exhibits strong stratification due to temperature and salinity gradients. The surrounding seawater is at 4°C with a density of 1027 kg/m³, while the plume’s initial density is 1012 kg/m³. Engineers must predict whether the plume will remain buoyant and coherent over 500 m of vertical rise, or if it will break up due to Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities induced by velocity shear. Design a model using conservation of mass and momentum in a stratified fluid to estimate the critical height at which instability occurs.
