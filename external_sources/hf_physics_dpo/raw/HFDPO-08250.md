# HFDPO-08250

A satellite in low Earth orbit uses a polarimetric imaging sensor to monitor ocean surface roughness for climate modeling. The sensor detects sunlight reflected off the sea surface at a glancing angle of 67°, with the reflected light showing strong polarization due to Brewster’s angle effects. However, the onboard calibration system detects unexpected signal fluctuations in the cross-polarized (S) component, despite a stable incident beam from a known reference source. The instrument’s dual-channel polarizer array (ideal, perfectly oriented) captures the intensity ratio between co-polarized (P) and cross-polarized (S) components as 12.6:1 at this angle. Determine whether the observed ratio deviates significantly from the theoretically predicted value based on Fresnel equations, and evaluate whether the discrepancy suggests a misalignment in the polarizer array or a genuine change in the ocean’s surface dielectric properties.
