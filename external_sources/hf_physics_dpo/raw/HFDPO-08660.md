# HFDPO-08660

A team at a quantum materials lab observes anomalous zero-resistance transport in a 2D van der Waals heterostructure at 30 K—well below conventional superconducting critical temperatures—despite no sign of BCS pairing. The structure consists of twisted bilayer graphene with a 1.3° twist angle, encapsulated in hexagonal boron nitride, and cooled under a 5 T perpendicular magnetic field that should suppress superconductivity. Yet, resistance drops to near-zero in a narrow voltage window. The researchers suspect emergent chiral edge states from a topological phase driven by strong electron correlations and spin-orbit coupling, but cannot confirm the presence of Majorana zero modes or other exotic quasiparticles. How can they distinguish between a topological superconducting phase and a trivial insulator with localized disorder-induced metallic puddles?
