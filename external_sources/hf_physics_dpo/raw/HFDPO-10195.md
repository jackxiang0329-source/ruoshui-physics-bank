# HFDPO-10195

In a Mars rover's thermal management system, a silicon carbide (SiC) junction diode operates under intense solar irradiance (1.4 kW/m²) and extreme temperature swings (-100°C to +50°C). The diode's forward voltage drops from 3.2 V at 25°C to 2.8 V at 125°C, yet the power dissipation remains stable due to active cooling. However, during a dust storm, solar input drops to 0.3 kW/m², and the cooling system fails. Modeling the diode as an ideal p-n junction with a known bandgap of 3.26 eV and assuming negligible recombination, determine whether the junction will undergo thermal runaway when the ambient temperature reaches 40°C, given that the junction's thermal resistance is 2.5°C/W and the diode is dissipating 1.2 W at 25°C.
