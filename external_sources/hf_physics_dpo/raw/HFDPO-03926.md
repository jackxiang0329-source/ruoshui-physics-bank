# HFDPO-03926

In a high-precision neutrino detector buried 1.5 km underground, copper signal cables (diameter 1.2 mm, resistivity 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) run from photomultiplier tubes to surface electronics. Each cable is 1.5 km long and operates in a 20°C environment, but temperature fluctuations in the rock cause local heating. When the cable’s temperature rises to 45°C, the signal voltage drops by 8.5% due to resistance increase. Calculate the required cross-sectional area of a new, thermally stable alloy cable (α = 0.0038/°C, ρ₀ = 2.3×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 20°C) to maintain the same signal strength at 45°C, assuming the same current and length.
