# HFDPO-11425

A fusion reactor’s magnetically confined plasma forms a toroidal (doughnut-shaped) charge distribution with a non-uniform volume charge density ρ(r) = ρ₀ (1 − r/R₀) for r ≤ R₀, where r is the radial distance from the toroidal axis, R₀ = 2 m is the major radius, and ρ₀ = 5×10⁻⁹ C/m³. The plasma is surrounded by a vacuum chamber with a symmetric, conducting shell that is grounded. Using Gauss's Law, determine the electric field magnitude at a radial distance of 1.5 m from the center of the torus (along the equatorial plane), and explain why the result depends critically on the symmetry of the charge distribution despite the toroidal geometry.
