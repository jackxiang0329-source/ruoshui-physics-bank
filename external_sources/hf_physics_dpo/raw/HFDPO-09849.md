# HFDPO-09849

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses parabolic troughs to focus sunlight onto a central receiver tube, heating synthetic oil to 400°C. The tube, 10 m long with a 5 cm diameter, experiences a heat flux of 12 kW/m² from solar concentration. Due to a sudden cloud cover, the sun is temporarily blocked for 15 minutes, during which the oil flow stops and the system must prevent thermal degradation. The oil’s specific heat is 2.1 kJ/kg·K and density is 850 kg/m³. If the tube’s outer surface is insulated but the inner wall is exposed to the stagnant oil, estimate the time it takes for the oil temperature to drop below 300°C. Assume 1D radial conduction in the oil and use a convective heat transfer coefficient of 200 W/m²·K between oil and tube wall.
