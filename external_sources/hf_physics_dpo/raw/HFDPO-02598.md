# HFDPO-02598

A high-efficiency solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses concentrated sunlight to heat molten nitrate salt (60% NaNO₃, 40% KNO₃) to 565°C for thermal storage. During a sudden cloud cover event, the inlet solar flux drops to zero, but the system must maintain 540°C for 3 hours to avoid turbine shutdown. The storage tank holds 12,000 m³ of salt with a density of 2100 kg/m³ and a specific heat capacity of 1400 J/kg·K. The tank is insulated with 25 cm of aerogel (k = 0.015 W/m·K), and its surface area is 2200 m². Assuming the ambient temperature is 25°C and the heat transfer coefficient from the tank surface to air is 8 W/m²·K, estimate the minimum thickness of an additional insulation layer (k = 0.02 W/m·K) required to prevent the salt temperature from dropping below 540°C.
