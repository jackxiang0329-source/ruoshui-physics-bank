# HFDPO-14010

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystem thrives at 400°C near a tectonic rift, where superheated seawater rich in dissolved minerals spouts into cold, near-freezing ocean depths (2°C). Engineers propose harvesting this thermal gradient to power a submersible microgrid using a closed-cycle Rankine system with R-134a as working fluid. The system operates with a turbine inlet pressure of 12 MPa and requires a minimum condenser pressure of 0.4 MPa to prevent boiling in cold ocean water. Given that the system’s turbine efficiency is 85% and the pump is 90% efficient, what is the maximum possible thermal efficiency of this cycle, and what fundamental thermodynamic limitation prevents the system from achieving higher efficiency despite the large temperature difference?
