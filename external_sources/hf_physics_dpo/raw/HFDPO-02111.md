# HFDPO-02111

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a binary cycle system with isobutane as the working fluid, extracting heat from 180°C geothermal water to produce 5 MW of net electrical power. The condenser operates at 35°C, and the turbine inlet pressure is 3.5 MPa. Due to scaling in the heat exchanger, the geothermal fluid exits at 120°C instead of the design 140°C. How much does the plant’s thermal efficiency drop, and what are the implications for long-term energy yield—considering that the turbine must now operate near the boundary of superheat and two-phase flow?
