# HFDPO-10212

A CubeSat mission to study Jupiter’s magnetosphere must execute a series of low-altitude flybys of Io using gravity assists to gradually raise its orbital inclination. The spacecraft, now in a 300 km circular orbit around Io at 60° inclination, needs to reach a 90° inclination orbit to maximize polar coverage. However, each flyby with Io’s gravity alters both orbital energy and inclination unpredictably due to the moon’s non-spherical gravity field and tidal forces. Using numerical integration of the patched conic approximation with J₂ perturbations, determine the minimum number of flybys required to achieve 90° inclination, assuming each flyby changes inclination by Δi = 11°+/-2° and energy by Δv = ±150 m/s (randomly drawn from a Gaussian distribution), and that the spacecraft can only accept ≤500 m/s total Δv budget. Can the mission succeed?
