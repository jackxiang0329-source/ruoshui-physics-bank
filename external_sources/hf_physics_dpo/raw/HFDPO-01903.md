# HFDPO-01903

A high-altitude solar-powered drone, designed to patrol the stratosphere for 90 days, relies on a triangular wing spar made of carbon-fiber composite to maintain structural integrity under extreme wind shear. During a test flight, engineers detect a subtle asymmetry in lift distribution across the wings, suggesting a torque imbalance around the central fuselage joint. The spar has a 12 m span, with one wing section 3.5 m from the joint, and the other 4.2 m from it. Wind gusts exert a non-uniform pressure of 85 Pa on the forward edge of the wing (aligned with the 3.5 m arm) and 62 Pa on the aft edge (aligned with the 4.2 m arm), both normal to the wing surface and acting over 1.8 m of chord length. Determine whether the net moment about the joint exceeds the allowable 8,500 N·m torque margin, and propose how to redistribute the wing’s internal stiffeners to counteract imbalance—using three distinct approaches.
