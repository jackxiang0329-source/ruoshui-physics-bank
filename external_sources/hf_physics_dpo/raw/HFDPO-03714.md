# HFDPO-03714

During a high-altitude expedition, a team discovers a frozen lake beneath a glacier in the Arctic, where ice has formed under extreme pressure and temperatures below −20°C. They notice that when a drill cuts through the ice, small bubbles emerge and rapidly expand—some forming vapor-like plumes in the surrounding air. The team estimates the ice contains 1.2% by mass of trapped air, mostly nitrogen and oxygen, at a pressure of 30 atm within the ice lattice. What phase transition(s) occur during drilling, and how does the sudden pressure release affect the latent heat balance and the rate of bubble formation, given that the enthalpy of fusion for ice is 334 J/g and the vaporization enthalpy of nitrogen at −196°C is 199 J/g?
