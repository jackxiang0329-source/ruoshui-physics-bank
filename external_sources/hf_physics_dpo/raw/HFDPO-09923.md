# HFDPO-09923

A bioengineer is designing a microfluidic chip to separate exosomes from blood plasma for early cancer detection. The chip uses a narrow, 50 μm-wide channel with a 1 mm long serpentine path to enhance particle separation via size-dependent viscous drag. Blood plasma has a dynamic viscosity of 1.2 × 10⁻³ Pa·s and flows at a mean velocity of 1.5 mm/s. Exosomes (diameter ~100 nm) and larger vesicles (300 nm) experience different drag forces due to their size. The engineer must determine the ratio of viscous drag forces on the two particles to optimize channel geometry and flow rate.
