# HFDPO-15428

During a deep-space mission, a malfunctioning radiation shield heats unevenly due to a partial short in its superconducting layer, causing localized overheating. The shield consists of a 3D grid of 100 μm-diameter niobium filaments (resistivity ρ = 4.7×10⁻⁶ Ω·m) arranged in a cubic lattice with 1 mm spacing. A 2.5 mA leakage current flows through a single filament segment spanning 5 mm, but thermal runaway is detected when the filament’s temperature exceeds 4.2 K, its critical superconducting transition. Given a 1.3 K temperature gradient along the filament and a thermal conductivity of 1.2 W/m·K, determine whether the power dissipation from resistive heating exceeds the thermal dissipation capacity, and explain why the system might fail even if current appears low.
