# HFDPO-10425

A research team in Antarctica is developing a passive thermal shield for sensitive cryogenic sensors used in ice-core drilling. The shield must maintain a stable internal temperature of −40°C despite external conditions that fluctuate between −60°C and −25°C over 24 hours. The shield consists of a 5 cm thick multilayered composite: 1 cm of aerogel (k = 0.012 W/m·K), followed by 2 cm of vacuum-insulated aluminum (k = 0.025 W/m·K), and 2 cm of polystyrene foam (k = 0.035 W/m·K). At what point during a 24-hour cycle does the internal temperature exceed −40°C, and how could the design be modified to prevent thermal overshoot without increasing material thickness?
