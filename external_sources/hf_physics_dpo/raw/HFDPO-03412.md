# HFDPO-03412

In a high-altitude research station, scientists observe erratic behavior in a cryogenic nitrogen storage system: despite constant cooling, liquid nitrogen begins boiling intermittently during periods of atmospheric pressure drop. The tank is a 1.5 m tall cylindrical vessel with a 0.6 m diameter, insulated with 10 cm of vacuum-packed aerogel (thermal conductivity 0.015 W/m·K). During one event, the pressure drops from 1 atm to 0.85 atm over 12 minutes while the tank remains isolated. The system’s temperature stays near 77 K, but boiling resumes 45 seconds after pressure drops begin. What physical mechanism explains this delayed onset of boiling, and how does the time delay depend on the rate of pressure change and the thermal inertia of the system?
