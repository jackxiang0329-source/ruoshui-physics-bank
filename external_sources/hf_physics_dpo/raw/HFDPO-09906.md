# HFDPO-09906

A deep-sea research drone descends at a constant speed of 0.8 m/s through seawater, encountering both viscous drag and form drag. Its streamlined hull has a frontal area of 0.3 m² and a drag coefficient of 0.12, while the fluid’s density is 1025 kg/m³. The drone’s mass is 45 kg, and it experiences a buoyant force of 440 N. As it descends, the water temperature increases slightly with depth, increasing the fluid’s dynamic viscosity from 1.4 × 10⁻³ Pa·s at 100 m to 1.1 × 10⁻³ Pa·s at 1500 m. Determine the total resistive force at 1500 m depth, and explain how the changing viscosity affects the relative contributions of skin friction and form drag.
