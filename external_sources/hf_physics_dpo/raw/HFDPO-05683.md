# HFDPO-05683

In a quantum computing lab, a team is testing a superconducting qubit made from a thin niobium film (T_c = 9.3 K) cooled to 15 mK. They observe that the qubit’s coherence time drops dramatically when exposed to cosmic ray muons—each carrying ~100 MeV of kinetic energy—passing through the cryostat. Estimate the minimum number of muons per second per cm² that could cause measurable decoherence, assuming each muon deposits 100 keV of energy locally in the film via ionization, and decoherence occurs when the energy deposition exceeds 10⁻¹² J per qubit per second.
