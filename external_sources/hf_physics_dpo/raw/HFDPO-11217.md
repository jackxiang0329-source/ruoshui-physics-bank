# HFDPO-11217

In a biomedical lab, researchers are developing a microfluidic device to separate exosomes (30–150 nm diameter) from blood plasma using size-dependent sedimentation in a laminar flow channel. The channel is 1.2 mm wide, 50 µm high, and 2 cm long, filled with plasma (viscosity 1.3 mPa·s, density 1030 kg/m³). A sample containing exosomes of varying sizes is introduced at one end. Due to the low Reynolds number (Re ≈ 0.01), flow is fully laminar and governed by Stokes' law. However, exosomes near the channel walls experience shear-induced migration, altering their trajectory. The team observes that larger exosomes (120 nm) cluster near the centerline, while smaller ones (50 nm) concentrate closer to the walls. What physical mechanism explains this non-uniform distribution despite identical settling forces, and how can the device be redesigned to ensure size-based separation based solely on Stokes drag?
