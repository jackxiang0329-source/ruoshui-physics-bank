# HFDPO-12611

A deep-space probe, traveling at 0.95c relative to Earth, transmits a 10-minute telemetry burst every 12 hours (probe time) to a receiving station on Mars. Due to relativistic time dilation and the changing distance between Mars and Earth, the received signal intervals vary over a Martian orbital cycle. Calculate the average time interval between signal receptions on Mars over one Martian year, accounting for both time dilation and the Doppler shift from the probe’s changing velocity relative to Mars. Assume Mars orbits the Sun at 1.5 AU with a period of 687 Earth days.
