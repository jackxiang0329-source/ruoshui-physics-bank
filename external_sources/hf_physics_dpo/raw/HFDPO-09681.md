# HFDPO-09681

A concert hall’s acoustics team detects a persistent, low-frequency "rumble" (15 Hz) during performances, felt more than heard, particularly near the balcony supports. Measurements show sound pressure levels exceed 90 dB at 15 Hz in those zones, but the source is untraceable. The hall’s ventilation system operates at 15 Hz due to a mechanical resonance in the air ducts. The ducts are 4.2 m long with one open end and one closed end. The air temperature is 22°C, and the ducts are lined with porous fiberglass (absorption coefficient α = 0.7 at 15 Hz). Determine whether the duct system is resonating in a standing wave mode that amplifies the 15 Hz signal, and if so, whether the duct’s lining can adequately dampen the resulting standing wave.
