# HFDPO-11341

A small fusion reactor prototype, designed to power a remote Arctic research station, experiences sudden drops in energy output during operation. The reactor uses deuterium-tritium fusion, and analysis shows that neutron flux readings from the plasma core have decreased by 32% over 14 days, despite stable plasma confinement and input power. The station’s neutron detector is calibrated to capture 90% of neutrons in the 14 MeV energy range, but the decay of tritium in the fuel (half-life = 12.3 years) may be affecting performance. Estimate the expected fractional reduction in tritium concentration over this period and determine whether this decay alone can account for the observed neutron flux drop.
