# HFDPO-12235

In a remote Arctic research station, a 5.0 m long cylindrical copper heating cable (diameter 2.0 mm) is buried underground to prevent permafrost thawing around critical infrastructure. The cable carries a steady current of 8.5 A, but after a winter storm, the temperature sensor detects that the cable’s surface is only reaching 12°C—well below the required 30°C. Engineers suspect that ice buildup around the cable has increased thermal resistance, but they aren’t sure whether it’s due to reduced electrical conductivity from cold or increased thermal insulation. Given that the resistivity of copper at -10°C is 1.78 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m and the surrounding ice has a thermal conductivity of 2.2 W/(m·K), determine the effective thermal resistance per unit length between the cable and the surrounding permafrost, assuming all electrical power is converted to heat and no heat is lost to the atmosphere.
