# HFDPO-03744

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole filled with conductive brine (σ = 2 S/m) to extract heat from subsurface rock. During operation, a 100 Hz alternating current is injected through electrodes at the borehole’s base to create a controlled electromagnetic field for monitoring subsurface conductivity anomalies. A nearby sensor array (80 m from the borehole) detects an induced voltage of 12 mV in a loop antenna (area = 0.25 m², resistance = 5 Ω) oriented perpendicular to the borehole axis. Given that the Earth’s magnetic permeability is μ₀ and the borehole’s effective current is 15 A, estimate the depth of a conductive fault zone that could explain the observed induction, assuming a layered earth model with exponential decay of the field.
