# HFDPO-06081

A high-performance wind turbine blade in a remote Arctic wind farm begins vibrating erratically during extreme cold snaps, causing reduced efficiency and structural stress. Engineers discover that airflow over the blade’s surface, which is designed for optimal lift at 15 m/s, becomes unstable below -30°C due to sudden changes in air density and viscosity. The blade’s tip speed reaches 50 m/s, and pressure sensors indicate a significant localized drop in static pressure at a critical point on the suction side. Given that the air density drops to 1.45 kg/m³ at -30°C (vs. 1.25 kg/m³ at 15°C) and dynamic viscosity increases slightly, determine how the Bernoulli-based lift prediction at this point deviates from the design model, and explain why stall-like behavior might occur despite no change in angle of attack.
