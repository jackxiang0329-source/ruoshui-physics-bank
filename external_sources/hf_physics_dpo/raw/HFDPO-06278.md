# HFDPO-06278

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses concentric cylindrical heat exchangers to transfer energy from molten salt (at 565°C) to water, generating steam. The inner pipe (diameter 10 cm) carries water at 250°C, while the outer annular space contains the salt. The pipes are made of stainless steel (k = 15 W/m·K), 2 cm thick, and the system operates in steady state. However, the plant experiences unexpected thermal losses, reducing efficiency. Given that the heat transfer coefficient on the water side is 2000 W/m²·K and on the salt side is 2500 W/m²·K, determine the rate of heat loss per meter of pipe length and assess whether increasing insulation thickness (1 cm, k = 0.04 W/m·K) on the outer surface would be thermodynamically justified.
