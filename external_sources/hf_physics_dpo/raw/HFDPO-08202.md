# HFDPO-08202

A deep-sea research submersible encounters a sudden malfunction in its thermal regulation system during a 4,000-meter descent, causing its internal heat exchanger to fail. The cabin, initially at 22°C, is thermally isolated but contains a 120 kg aluminum alloy frame with a specific heat capacity of 900 J/kg·K. The surrounding ocean water is at 4°C, and the hull’s thermal resistance limits heat transfer to 0.15 W/m²·K with a surface area of 35 m². After 18 minutes, the cabin temperature drops to 15°C. Estimate the effective heat transfer coefficient of the hull material, and discuss whether the observed cooling rate aligns with the Second Law’s entropy constraints under adiabatic insulation.
