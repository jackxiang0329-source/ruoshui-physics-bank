# HFDPO-07020

A quantum computing chip operates at 15 mK using a dilution refrigerator, with superconducting qubits connected via nanoscale aluminum traces (20 nm thick, 100 nm wide) embedded in a silicon substrate. During calibration, engineers detect an unexpected 1.2 μA leakage current between adjacent qubit lines—seemingly violating the expected near-zero conductivity. The traces are 1.5 mm long, and the resistivity of the aluminum at 15 mK is anomalously high at 3.2 × 10⁻⁹ Ω·m due to scattering from interface defects. Assuming the leakage occurs through an insulating barrier with a tunnelling probability proportional to exp(−2κd), where κ = 1.2 × 10¹⁰ m⁻¹, determine the effective thickness of the insulating layer.
