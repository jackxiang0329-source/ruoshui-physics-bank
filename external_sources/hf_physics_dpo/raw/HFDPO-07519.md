# HFDPO-07519

A lunar habitat module on the Moon’s south pole is shielded from direct sunlight by a rugged ridge, but its thermal stability is threatened by radiative heat transfer from a nearby regolith region that remains at 280 K during the 14-Earth-day night. The module’s outer surface, coated with a multi-layered insulation (MLI) blanket of effective emissivity 0.05, has a total surface area of 120 m² and a thermal mass equivalent to 800 kg of aluminum. If the module’s internal heaters fail and it cools from 300 K to 270 K over 6 hours, estimate the mean effective thermal conductivity of the insulation system, assuming the internal heat capacity is dominated by structural materials.
