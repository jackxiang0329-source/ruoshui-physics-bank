# HFDPO-02370

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses supercritical CO₂ instead of water to extract heat from a fractured basalt reservoir at 450°C. The CO₂ enters the reservoir at 220°C and 25 MPa, flowing through a 1.8 km long, 15 cm diameter borehole with a turbulent flow (Re ≈ 12,000). After extracting 1.4 MW of thermal power, the fluid exits at 370°C. Estimate the minimum required mass flow rate of CO₂ to sustain this power output, assuming negligible heat loss to surrounding rock and accounting for non-ideal behavior using the Peng-Robinson equation of state.
