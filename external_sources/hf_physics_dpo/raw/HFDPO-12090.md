# HFDPO-12090

During a volcanic eruption, a pyroclastic surge travels down a steep, narrow valley at 120 m/s, moving as a dense, gravity-driven turbulent flow. The flow is 30 m thick and spreads laterally with a width of 200 m. A remote weather station located 8 km downstream from the source detects a sudden pressure spike of 45 kPa above ambient, followed by a rapid drop. The flow’s density is 1.8 kg/m³, and the valley’s wall roughness is 0.1 m. Using momentum balance and scaling arguments, estimate the average shear stress at the valley floor and discuss whether the surge is best modeled as a turbulent boundary layer, a shallow-water surge, or a fully developed gravity current.
