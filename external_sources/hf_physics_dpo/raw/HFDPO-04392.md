# HFDPO-04392

A fusion reactor prototype uses a tokamak design where plasma is confined in a toroidal chamber by strong magnetic fields. During a pulse, a sudden disruption releases ~1.2 MJ of thermal energy explosively into the vessel wall over 0.03 seconds. Engineers must calculate the momentum transferred to the containment structure to assess structural integrity. The plasma, initially at rest and with a total mass of 5.4 × 10⁻³ kg, expands radially outward with a velocity distribution peaking at 1.2 × 10⁶ m/s. Use conservation of momentum and energy principles to estimate the average force exerted on the wall, and evaluate whether the design can withstand the impulse without yielding.
