# HFDPO-06332

A spacecraft's solar array, designed to operate at 120°C in deep space, experiences unexpected thermal runaway during a 6-hour eclipse phase, with temperatures spiking to 185°C. The array’s heat capacity is 4.2 kJ/K, and its emissivity is 0.85, but thermal conductivity through the supporting truss is negligible. Radiative heat loss to space (T ≈ 3 K) is governed by the Stefan-Boltzmann law. However, internal heat generation from power electronics (200 W) and residual solar input (18 W) continue. Assuming the array reaches a steady-state temperature under these conditions, determine the *effective* thermal resistance between the array and the spacecraft’s radiator system, given that the radiator dissipates heat at 75 W but operates at 80°C. How would a 15% increase in internal heat generation affect the equilibrium temperature?
