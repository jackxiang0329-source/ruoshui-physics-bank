# HFDPO-01739

A geothermal power plant in Iceland uses a vertical borehole (depth 2.8 km, diameter 15 cm) to circulate fluid through fractured rock. Engineers notice erratic voltage fluctuations in the surface power grid despite stable fluid flow. The borehole passes through a region with a time-varying natural magnetic field (dB/dt ≈ 1.2 nT/s, upward component) due to geomagnetic pulsations. A conductive loop formed by the fluid column and the borehole walls acts as a secondary coil. Estimate the induced EMF in the loop if the fluid has a resistivity of 0.2 Ω·m and the borehole length is entirely conductive, assuming the loop area is defined by the borehole cross-section. Why does the measured EMF not match the theoretical prediction from Faraday’s law alone?
