# HFDPO-03611

A cryogenic fusion reactor’s superconducting magnets are quenched when a small flaw in the niobium-tin alloy causes localized heating, triggering a rapid phase transition from superconducting to normal state across a 1.2 cm² cross-section. The energy released during this transition must be safely dissipated within 25 ms to prevent irreversible damage. Given the critical current density of 1.8 × 10⁸ A/m², the heat of transition is 3.2 × 10⁶ J/m³, and the thermal diffusivity of the composite is 3.5 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s, determine the minimum thickness of the surrounding thermal insulating layer required to delay heat propagation beyond the magnet coil by the quench time.
