# HFDPO-14201

A quantum dot solar cell designed for space missions uses lead-sulfide nanocrystals embedded in a perovskite matrix, but after 18 months in low Earth orbit, its efficiency drops by 37%. Spectroscopic analysis reveals persistent charge traps linked to interstitial sulfur vacancies. Estimate the effective Fermi level shift in the conduction band due to these defects, assuming a defect density of 1.2 × 10¹⁸ cm⁻³ and a dielectric constant of 12.5. How does this shift impact carrier mobility and recombination rates in the context of particle-hole pair dynamics governed by the Dirac equation for low-energy carriers?
