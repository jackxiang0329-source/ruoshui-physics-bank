# HFDPO-09834

A quantum computing lab in Zurich is testing a new fluxonium qubit made from a high-temperature superconducting loop (Tc = 92 K) with a Josephson junction. During operation at 15 mK, researchers observe sudden decoherence when magnetic flux leaks from a neighboring cryogenic magnet. The qubit’s energy splitting depends on the enclosed flux Φ through the loop, and its coherence time τ ∝ exp(−ΔE/kBT), where ΔE is the energy gap between computational states. To prevent flux noise, they plan to surround the qubit with a thin NbTiN film (Tc ≈ 15 K) that should expel magnetic fields via the Meissner effect. But when the system is cooled below 15 K, the NbTiN layer shows unexpected flux penetration at 0.002 T, disrupting the qubit. Estimate the critical magnetic field H_c for flux penetration in the NbTiN layer and determine whether the qubit’s coherence is compromised due to flux trapping.
