# HFDPO-05286

In a microfluidic lab-on-a-chip device designed for early cancer detection, a suspension of exosomes (average diameter 120 nm, density 1.1 g/cm³) is transported through a 50 μm wide, 100 μm long channel lined with hydrophilic polymer brushes. The flow is driven by a pressure drop of 1.8 kPa at low Reynolds number (Re ≈ 0.003), but the exosomes exhibit non-uniform dispersion—clustering near the channel centerline despite expected Brownian diffusion. Using the Stokes–Einstein relation and a modified Poiseuille flow model incorporating slip at the polymer interface, determine the optimal flow velocity profile to achieve uniform particle distribution across the channel cross-section, and explain why the observed clustering contradicts classical viscous flow predictions.
