# HFDPO-03007

A quantum computing chip based on fluxonium qubits operates at 15 mK within a dilution refrigerator, using a niobium-based superconducting loop with a critical temperature of 9.2 K and a critical field of 180 mT. During calibration, a persistent current of 3.7 nA is observed to survive for over 100 seconds without decay, despite a tiny external magnetic flux noise of 1.2 μΦ₀. However, when the device is cooled below 8 mK, the current decays within nanoseconds. Analyze whether this behavior stems from thermal excitation across the superconducting gap, vortex tunneling through the Josephson junctions, or the onset of macroscopic quantum tunneling, and determine which mechanism dominates at sub-10 mK temperatures.
