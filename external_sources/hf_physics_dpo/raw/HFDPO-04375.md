# HFDPO-04375

A high-precision interferometric sensor aboard a satellite measures minute changes in atmospheric density by detecting phase shifts in laser light traversing Earth’s upper atmosphere over a 100 km path. During a geomagnetic storm, unexpected phase fluctuations of up to 0.8π radians are recorded, despite stable laser frequency and path length. The sensor uses a Michelson interferometer with mirrors aligned to maintain coherence over 10 km of optical path. What physical mechanism in wave optics explains these anomalous phase changes, and how does it relate to the spatial coherence of the laser and the refractive index gradients caused by ionospheric turbulence?
