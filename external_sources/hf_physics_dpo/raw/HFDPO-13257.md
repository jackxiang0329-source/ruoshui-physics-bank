# HFDPO-13257

A fusion reactor design team at a private space propulsion startup faces a crisis: neutron flux measurements from the plasma core are 30% lower than predicted by their Monte Carlo simulations, despite maintaining identical magnetic confinement and heating profiles. The team suspects the neutron emission spectrum from deuterium-tritium reactions is being distorted by unaccounted-for neutron moderation in the first-wall beryllium blanket. Given a measured 14.1 MeV neutron flux of 2.1×10¹⁴ n/cm²/s at 10 cm from the plasma edge, and knowing that beryllium (⁹Be) has a significant (n,2n) reaction cross-section peaking at ~13 MeV, determine the effective reduction factor in the primary 14.1 MeV neutron yield due to neutron scattering and absorption in the Be layer, and explain why the discrepancy arises from both nuclear cross-sections and neutron transport geometry.
