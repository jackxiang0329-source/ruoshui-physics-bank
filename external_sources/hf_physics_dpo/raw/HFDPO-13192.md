# HFDPO-13192

A biotech startup is designing a microfluidic device for single-cell analysis, where cells are trapped in a harmonic potential generated by optically manipulated dielectric beads. The system's stability depends on the effective spring constant of the optical trap, determined by laser intensity. During calibration, a 10 μm polystyrene bead (mass 5.2 × 10⁻¹² kg) exhibits damped oscillations with a period of 12 ms and a decay time of 80 ms. The team observes that the oscillations are significantly underdamped but suspects thermal noise is masking the true damping. What is the effective damping coefficient, and how does it compare to the expected value based on Stokes’ law for fluid drag in water at 25°C? Can the system be tuned to achieve critical damping without losing spatial resolution?
