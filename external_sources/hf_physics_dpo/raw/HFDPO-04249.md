# HFDPO-04249

A marine biologist is developing a wireless underwater sensor network using resonant inductive coupling to power deep-sea instruments. The system uses a primary coil on a surface buoy (L₁ = 15 mH, R₁ = 0.8 Ω) and a secondary coil on a submersible (L₂ = 12 mH, R₂ = 1.2 Ω), separated by 8 meters of seawater. At 60 kHz, the mutual inductance is measured at M = 48 μH, and the seawater's conductivity causes a distributed parasitic capacitance of 2.5 nF per meter between coils. The system operates in a highly noisy electromagnetic environment with harmonic interference at 120 kHz. Determine the optimal load resistance to maximize power transfer efficiency while minimizing harmonic distortion, and explain how the parasitic capacitance alters the resonant frequency.
