# HFDPO-02993

During a lunar mining operation, engineers observe that a molten regolith sample (primarily basaltic glass with 15 wt% FeO) cools too rapidly in vacuum, forming microcrystalline dendrites instead of the desired amorphous glass needed for structural integrity. The sample is cooled from 1400°C at 3.5°C/min, but phase separation into Fe-rich and Si-rich domains begins at 1100°C. Calculate the critical cooling rate required to suppress spinodal decomposition and preserve metastable glass formation, given that the activation energy for atomic diffusion is 320 kJ/mol and the diffusion coefficient at 1100°C is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ m²/s.
