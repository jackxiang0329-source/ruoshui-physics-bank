# HFDPO-02269

A lab is developing a new type of quantum dot solar cell using lead sulfide nanocrystals suspended in a polymer matrix. The dots are 5 nm in diameter, and their electronic states are quantized due to quantum confinement. At room temperature (300 K), the energy gap between the first excited state and ground state is 1.2 eV. The researchers observe that the system exhibits a significant population of electrons in the excited state—about 18% of the total electrons—despite the high energy gap. They suspect the effective temperature of the electronic subsystem might be higher than expected due to non-radiative relaxation processes. What is the effective temperature of the electronic subsystem assuming equilibrium with a Boltzmann distribution?
