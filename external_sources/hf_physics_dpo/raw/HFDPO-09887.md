# HFDPO-09887

A marine biologist is studying whale communication in the Arctic Ocean during winter, where thick sea ice limits acoustic transmission. She deploys a hydrophone array beneath the ice, but detects inconsistent signal strength from a migrating humpback whale, despite the animal being within 2 km. The ice thickness varies from 1.2 m to 3.8 m, and the water temperature is 2.5°C. Sound speed in seawater is 1447 m/s, and ice attenuates sound at 0.8 dB/m. The whale’s call (150 Hz, 180 dB re 1 μPa at 1 m) experiences refraction due to a 0.5°C/m temperature gradient. What is the effective received sound pressure level (SPL) at the hydrophone if the closest ice thickness is 1.8 m and the path traverses a 1.5 m layer of ice and 1.8 km of water?
