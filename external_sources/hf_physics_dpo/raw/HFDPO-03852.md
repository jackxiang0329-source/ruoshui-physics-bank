# HFDPO-03852

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent explorer, designed to maintain neutral buoyancy at 2,500 meters depth, uses a variable-density buoyancy compensator filled with a compressible fluid. At the surface, the compensator has a volume of 0.8 m³ and a density of 820 kg/m³. As it descends, the fluid compresses due to increasing pressure, and the vessel begins to sink. To stabilize, the system must adjust the fluid’s density to match the surrounding seawater, which increases with depth due to salinity gradients. Given that seawater density increases linearly from 1,025 kg/m³ at the surface to 1,045 kg/m³ at 2,500 m, and the fluid’s compressibility follows a bulk modulus of 2.2 GPa, what initial pressure-induced volume change must the compensator undergo to achieve neutral buoyancy at depth?
