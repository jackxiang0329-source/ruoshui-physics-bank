# HFDPO-04803

A research team is testing a prototype of a deployable solar shade structure for a Mars rover habitat, consisting of three rigid triangular panels connected by frictionless hinges at their vertices, forming a symmetric, geodesic dome. The structure is anchored at three points on the Martian surface, each with a tether applying a 520 N tension force at an angle of 35° above the horizontal, directed toward the dome’s center. The dome’s weight (including structure and solar panels) is 2100 N, acting at its geometric center, 1.4 m above the base plane. If the base triangle has side lengths of 2.8 m, and the system must remain in equilibrium under Martian gravity (3.7 m/s²), calculate the minimum coefficient of static friction required at each anchor point to prevent sliding—assuming the tethers are perfectly taut and the dome is symmetric.
