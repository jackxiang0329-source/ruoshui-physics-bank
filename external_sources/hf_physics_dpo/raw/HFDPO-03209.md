# HFDPO-03209

A quantum dot solar cell prototype exhibits anomalous efficiency drops under sunlight with strong polarization components. Measurements show that at a critical angle of incidence (58.2°) near the air–glass interface, the transmitted intensity drops by 40% despite the glass having negligible absorption. The glass has a refractive index of 1.62, and the quantum dots are embedded 150 nm below the surface. Engineers suspect interference between reflected and transmitted waves at the interface is degrading carrier generation. Determine the minimal thickness of a dielectric anti-reflective coating (n = 1.35) that must be added to minimize these losses, and explain why a single-layer coating is effective even though the device operates with partially polarized light.
