# HFDPO-05200

A coastal city is designing a new seawater desalination plant to address drought risks. The intake system draws seawater from a submerged pipe 5 meters below the surface, but engineers notice that during peak tides, turbulent vortices form at the intake entrance, reducing flow efficiency and causing sediment to accumulate. The pipe has a diameter of 0.6 m, and the average seawater flow speed at the intake must be maintained at 1.2 m/s to prevent clogging. Using the Reynolds number and Bernoulli’s principle, determine the minimum required upstream distance from the pipe entrance where the flow must be stabilized, assuming the water has a kinematic viscosity of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s and the flow must remain laminar near the intake.
