# HFDPO-01307

A quantum computing chip uses superconducting qubits cooled to 15 mK, but persistent current loops in the aluminum wiring develop tiny resistive losses due to flux trapped in microscopic defects. At 15 mK, the wire’s resistance is 2.3 nΩ per meter, and a loop carries 3.7 µA. If one of the loop’s 12 cm segments suddenly becomes resistive due to a nanoscale grain boundary, how much more power is dissipated in that segment than in a perfect superconducting loop? Compare this to the total power budget of the chip (1.2 µW), and evaluate whether this defect could trigger decoherence through heating.
