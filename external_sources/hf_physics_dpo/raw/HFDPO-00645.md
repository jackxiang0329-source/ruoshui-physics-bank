# HFDPO-00645

A quantum optics lab is developing a high-resolution spectrometer for exoplanet atmospheric analysis using a custom diffraction grating fabricated via nanoimprint lithography. The grating has 1200 lines per millimeter, but due to fabrication imperfections, the groove spacing varies sinusoidally with a period of 50 μm, introducing periodic phase errors. When illuminated with a 632.8 nm He-Ne laser, the central maximum splits into a fine structure of 11 distinct peaks. What is the amplitude of the phase modulation (in radians) that causes this splitting, and why does the number of observable peaks depend on both the modulation amplitude and the grating’s effective width?
