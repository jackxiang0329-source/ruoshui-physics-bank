# HFDPO-14072

A quantum dot solar cell prototype exhibits unexpected efficiency drops under low-light urban conditions. The device uses 2-nm diameter CdSe nanoparticles where electron confinement creates discrete energy states. At 300 K, the system shows photon absorption peaks at 540 nm and 620 nm, but the quantum yield decreases by 40% when light intensity drops below 100 W/m²—despite the photons still having sufficient energy. The researchers suspect wave-like interference effects are disrupting electron tunneling at the dot interface. How does wave-particle duality explain the observed non-linear efficiency behavior under low-intensity, coherent light?
