# HFDPO-13127

A particle accelerator in Geneva detects muons created in the upper atmosphere at an altitude of 15 km, traveling toward Earth at 0.998c. Observers on the ground measure the muons' average lifetime as 2.2 μs when at rest, yet over 90% survive the journey to sea level. From the muon’s frame, the distance to Earth is contracted. Calculate the minimum speed required for a muon to reach the surface before decaying, assuming its proper lifetime is 2.2 μs—then explain why the observed survival rate exceeds predictions from classical physics, using both time dilation and length contraction frameworks.
