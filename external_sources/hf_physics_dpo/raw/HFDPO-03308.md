# HFDPO-03308

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a network of underground heat exchangers embedded in basalt rock. One critical support pillar, 12 meters tall and 0.8 m in diameter, is anchored into a fracture zone where the rock has varying frictional resistance. The pillar must resist a lateral thermal expansion force of 950 kN due to cyclic heating, applied at 9 m above the base. The frictional coefficient μ varies linearly from 0.6 at the top of the embedded section to 0.3 at the bottom, over a 6-meter depth. Can the pillar remain stable under this load without slipping, and if not, what minimum additional anchoring force is needed at the base to prevent failure?
