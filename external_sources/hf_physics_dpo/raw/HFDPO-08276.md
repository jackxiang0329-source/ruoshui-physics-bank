# HFDPO-08276

In a quantum imaging experiment, a single photon is directed through a modified Mach-Zehnder interferometer where one arm contains a nanoscale mechanical oscillator capable of absorbing and re-emitting the photon. At room temperature, thermal fluctuations cause the oscillator to move randomly, effectively "measuring" which path the photon took. When the average displacement of the oscillator exceeds 0.1 nm, interference fringes vanish. Given the photon’s wavelength is 600 nm and the oscillator has a mass of 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg, calculate the minimum temperature at which quantum interference becomes undetectable due to wavefunction collapse from environmental decoherence.
