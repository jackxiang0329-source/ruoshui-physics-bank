# HFDPO-10333

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent system emits superheated fluid at 350°C into the surrounding 2°C ocean water. Engineers propose harvesting thermal energy using a closed-loop organic Rankine cycle with pentane as the working fluid. The turbine operates between the vent’s heat source and the cold ocean sink, but efficiency is limited not by thermodynamics alone—thermal degradation of pentane at high temperatures accelerates, reducing usable heat input over time. Given the vent’s constant 1.5 MW heat flux, a 30% theoretical Carnot efficiency, and a 20-year lifespan, determine the optimal operating temperature range that maximizes net energy output, considering degradation rates modeled by a first-order reaction with a half-life of 5 years at 350°C.
