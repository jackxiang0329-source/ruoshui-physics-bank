# HFDPO-03027

A team at CERN is designing a new particle detector for the upcoming High-Luminosity LHC upgrade. To minimize signal distortion from high-energy protons (γ ≈ 7500) traveling near the speed of light, they must account for relativistic length contraction in the detector’s scintillator layers. One layer, 2.4 cm thick in its rest frame, must be optimized to fully absorb particles before they pass through. However, due to time dilation, the detector’s trigger system must account for the perceived thickness in the lab frame. What is the effective thickness of the layer as measured in the lab frame, and how does this affect the timing window for signal detection?
