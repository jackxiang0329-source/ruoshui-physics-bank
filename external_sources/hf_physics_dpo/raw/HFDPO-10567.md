# HFDPO-10567

During a geomagnetic storm, a power grid in northern Canada experiences fluctuating currents in its long transmission lines, causing protective relays to trip unexpectedly. Engineers notice the induced voltage spikes correlate with rapid changes in Earth's magnetic field, measured at 250 nT/s over a 10-second interval. The 200 km transmission line runs east-west and lies in a region where the vertical component of Earth's field is 55 μT. What is the magnitude of the induced electric field along the line, and why does this field not immediately dissipate despite being non-conservative?
