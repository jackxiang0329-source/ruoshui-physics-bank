# HFDPO-00629

A quantum computing chip uses a network of niobium-based Josephson junctions operating at 4.2 K. During a cryogenic test, engineers notice unexpected energy dissipation in the control lines—measured as 1.3 μW per junction—despite no external voltage applied. The junctions are designed to be in the zero-voltage state, but subtle magnetic flux from nearby power cables is penetrating the superconducting loops. Calculate the effective current circulating in each loop due to trapped flux, assuming a critical current density of 1.5 × 10⁶ A/m² and a loop inductance of 2.4 nH.
