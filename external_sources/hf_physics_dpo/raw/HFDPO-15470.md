# HFDPO-15470

A lunar research station uses a closed-cycle Stirling engine powered by a small radioisotope heater unit (RHU) emitting 50 W of thermal power. The engine operates between a hot reservoir at 500 K (near the RHU) and a cold reservoir at 120 K (exposed to space via radiative fins). Over a 30-day mission cycle, engineers observe that the actual work output averages only 8.2 W. Evaluate whether the observed performance violates the Second Law of Thermodynamics, and if not, determine what fraction of the maximum theoretical efficiency is being achieved. Consider the finite heat transfer rates and the fact that the engine must operate in steady state despite the slowly decaying heat source.
