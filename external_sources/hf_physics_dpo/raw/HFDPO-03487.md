# HFDPO-03487

In a high-vacuum electron beam lithography chamber, a nanoscale silicon tip (radius 50 nm) is used to deposit charge onto a graphene sheet. Due to imperfect grounding, the tip develops a net charge of +2.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ C. The tip is positioned 1.2 μm above the graphene, which has a work function of 4.5 eV and acts as a perfect conductor. As electrons tunnel from the graphene to neutralize the tip’s charge, the system’s electric field redistributes. What is the resulting surface charge density on the graphene directly beneath the tip, and how does this affect the local electron tunneling rate at the tip apex?
