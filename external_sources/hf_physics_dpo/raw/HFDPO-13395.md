# HFDPO-13395

A biomedical engineer is designing a flexible, implantable vascular stent to support atherosclerotic coronary arteries undergoing pulsatile blood flow. The stent must withstand cyclic stresses up to 180 MPa at a frequency of 1.2 Hz over 10 years (≈3×10⁸ cycles). Material selection is critical: Nitinol (NiTi) has a fatigue limit of 800 MPa but exhibits significant shape memory hysteresis, while cobalt-chromium alloy (CoCr) offers higher yield strength (1100 MPa) but is brittle and prone to crack propagation under cyclic loading. The artery’s compliance varies with plaque buildup—initial stiffness is 0.12 MPa/mm, increasing nonlinearly to 0.4 MPa/mm as stenosis progresses. Which material, under realistic loading and degradation conditions, best balances long-term mechanical stability, fatigue resistance, and adaptive compliance?
