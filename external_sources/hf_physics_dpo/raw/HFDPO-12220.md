# HFDPO-12220

A lunar lander descends vertically toward an irregularly shaped asteroid with a non-uniform gravity field. At 150 m above the surface, the lander’s thrusters are engaged at 12,500 N thrust, but the asteroid’s gravitational acceleration varies from 0.12 m/s² at the north pole to 0.06 m/s² at the equator due to rotational effects and mass concentration. The lander has a mass of 1,800 kg and is designed to land safely at ≤1.0 m/s. Using real-time terrain mapping data, engineers must adjust thrust magnitude and direction dynamically to maintain a controlled descent while minimizing fuel consumption. What is the optimal thrust profile—both magnitude and angle relative to vertical—needed to achieve a soft landing over a 40-second descent, assuming the lander’s attitude is perfectly controlled?
