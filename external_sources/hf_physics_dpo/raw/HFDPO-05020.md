# HFDPO-05020

A quantum imaging lab is developing a lensless ghost imaging system using entangled photon pairs generated via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) in a beta-barium borate (BBO) crystal. The signal photon passes through a test object (a microscopic biological sample with spatial frequency components up to 150 μm⁻¹), while the idler photon is detected by a high-speed single-pixel camera. The system relies on spatial correlations between the two photons to reconstruct the image. However, the reconstructed image suffers from noticeable noise and blurring. The researchers suspect the issue stems from imperfect spatial mode matching between the signal and idler beams, caused by slight misalignment of the pump laser beam relative to the crystal’s optical axis. Given that the pump laser has a Gaussian beam waist of 100 μm and the crystal is 2 mm long with a walk-off angle of 0.6°, calculate the spatial overlap integral between the signal and idler beams at the crystal exit, and explain how misalignment affects the visibility of the intensity correlation and thus the final image quality.
