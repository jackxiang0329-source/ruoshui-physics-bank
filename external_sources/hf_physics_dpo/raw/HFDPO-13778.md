# HFDPO-13778

During a solar eclipse, a researcher observes unexpected fluctuations in the voltage output of a photovoltaic array used to power a remote weather station. The array consists of 12 identical solar cells connected in series, each with a resistance of 0.5 Ω and an internal voltage source that varies with light intensity. Under full sunlight, the open-circuit voltage per cell is 0.6 V, but during the eclipse, when only 10% of sunlight reaches the array, the effective voltage drops to 0.15 V per cell. The station’s load draws a constant current of 0.2 A. What is the actual voltage delivered to the load during the eclipse, and why does the system behave non-linearly despite Ohm’s law?
