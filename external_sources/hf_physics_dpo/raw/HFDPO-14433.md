# HFDPO-14433

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate cancer cells from blood based on size and deformability. The device uses a serpentine channel (75 μm wide, 500 μm long) with a 90° turn every 200 μm. Blood flows at 1.2 mL/min, with a viscosity of 3.5 mPa·s and density of 1060 kg/m³. The cancer cells (diameter ~15 μm) are significantly more deformable than healthy cells. The engineer needs to ensure that the viscous drag in the bends does not cause undesirable cell aggregation or wall adhesion during transit. What is the minimum Reynolds number threshold below which viscous forces dominate the flow behavior in the bends, and how does this affect cell separation efficiency?
