# HFDPO-09296

During a winter storm, a power line sags between two towers 120 m apart, strung with a tension that prevents ice accumulation. But after a freezing rain, a layer of ice 1.5 cm thick forms uniformly along the wire. The ice has a density of 917 kg/m³, while the wire itself has negligible mass. Assuming the ice adds mass but not stiffness, and that the wire’s maximum tension capacity is 8500 N, will the wire snap under the added weight? Use the wire’s sag and the added mass to estimate the new tension, assuming a parabolic approximation for the sag.
