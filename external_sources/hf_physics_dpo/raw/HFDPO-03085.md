# HFDPO-03085

A laser satellite laser interferometer (LISA) detects gravitational waves by measuring tiny phase shifts in light beams traveling 2.5 million km between test masses. During a calibration run, a sudden atmospheric disturbance at the ground station introduces a refractive index fluctuation of Δn = 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ over a 100 m path at the edge of the optical bench. If the laser wavelength is 1064 nm and the system uses a Mach-Zehnder interferometer, what is the minimum detectable phase shift in the fringe pattern caused by this fluctuation? How does this compare to the expected phase shift from a real gravitational wave event (≈ 2×10⁻²⁰ radians)?
