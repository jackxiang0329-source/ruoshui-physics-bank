# HFDPO-08299

A marine biologist studying humpback whale communication in the North Atlantic notices that vocalizations at 20 Hz—critical for long-range mating calls—become distorted and fade rapidly in certain underwater canyons, despite low ambient noise. Measurements show the water temperature varies from 12°C at the surface to 4°C at 800 m depth, creating a strong thermal inversion. The canyon’s seafloor has a sediment layer with a sound speed of 1,450 m/s, while the water column exhibits a sound speed gradient due to temperature and pressure. Determine whether the observed signal degradation is caused by sound bending away from the whales due to refraction, and calculate the minimum depth at which sound rays begin to refract upward, given that the sound speed at 12°C is 1,440 m/s and increases by 4 m/s per 100 m depth (due to pressure) but decreases by 2 m/s per 1°C drop in temperature.
