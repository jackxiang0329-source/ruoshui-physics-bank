# HFDPO-02189

During a high-altitude polar expedition, a team discovers a frozen lake whose ice layer is gradually thinning despite sub-zero temperatures. The ice, initially 1.8 m thick, is losing mass at a rate that suggests latent heat from geothermal flux beneath is overcoming radiative and convective cooling. Using thermal conductivity of ice (2.18 W/m·K), density (917 kg/m³), and latent heat of fusion (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg), estimate how long it would take for the ice to fully melt if the geothermal flux is 0.04 W/m² and the surface is exposed to -20°C air with a convective heat transfer coefficient of 25 W/m²·K. Assume steady-state heat transfer and uniform temperature profile across the ice.
