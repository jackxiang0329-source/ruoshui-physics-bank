# HFDPO-09872

During a cold snap in northern Canada, a research team discovers that a newly developed Arctic monitoring drone fails to deploy its solar array after being stored at -40°C for 12 hours. The array’s hinges are made from a polymer composite that becomes brittle below its glass transition temperature (Tg). The drone’s manual specifies that the polymer must be above Tg = -25°C to function safely, but the team has no access to thermometers or heating tools. They only have a small heat pack (initially at 25°C, mass 150 g, specific heat 2.1 J/g°C) and a calibrated thermal camera reading the drone’s surface temperature every 30 seconds. What is the minimum time needed to warm the hinge region (estimated mass 10 g) above Tg, assuming 70% of the heat pack’s energy transfers to the hinge and no significant heat loss to the environment?
