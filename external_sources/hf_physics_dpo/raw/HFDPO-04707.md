# HFDPO-04707

A biomedical engineer is designing a microfluidic device to separate circulating tumor cells (CTCs) from blood using inertial focusing in a narrow channel. The device operates at 37°C, with blood modeled as a Newtonian fluid of viscosity 3.5 mPa·s and density 1060 kg/m³. The channel is 100 μm wide and 50 μm high, with a flow rate of 0.5 μL/min. If the CTCs (diameter ~12 μm, density 1080 kg/m³) are to be focused to the channel centerline via secondary flows, determine the critical Reynolds number above which inertial effects dominate viscous forces, and explain whether the flow regime supports reliable cell focusing.
