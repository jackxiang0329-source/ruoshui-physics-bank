# HFDPO-03232

In a remote Arctic research station, a solar-powered DC microgrid maintains critical instruments during the 24-hour polar night using lithium-ion batteries and a 48V DC bus. During a sudden temperature drop to -40°C, the monitoring system records a 12% voltage drop across a 100-meter copper power cable (10 AWG) supplying a weather sensor node. The cable’s resistance increases due to temperature effects, but the load draws constant power. Engineers must determine the temperature-dependent resistance change in the cable, given that the cable's nominal resistance at 25°C is 0.25 Ω and the temperature coefficient of copper is 0.00393/°C.
