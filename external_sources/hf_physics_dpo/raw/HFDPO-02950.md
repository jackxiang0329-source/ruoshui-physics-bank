# HFDPO-02950

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a large vertical borehole to circulate fluid, but recent measurements show unexpected fluctuations in the local magnetic field near the drill site. The borehole is 3 km deep, lined with a conductive steel casing (resistivity ρ = 1.5 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m), and during operation, a 50 Hz alternating current of 200 A flows through the casing due to induced currents from Earth's magnetic field. The Earth’s field at this location is 55 μT, directed 65° below horizontal. What is the maximum induced electric field strength inside the casing at the borehole’s midpoint? How does this compare to the field required to cause measurable eddy current heating?
