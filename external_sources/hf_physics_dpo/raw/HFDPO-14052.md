# HFDPO-14052

A lunar rover's heat shield, made of a composite material with temperature-dependent thermal conductivity, experiences a sudden solar flare that increases incident radiation to 1300 W/m² over a 10-minute window. The shield, initially at 150 K, must not exceed 420 K at any point to prevent degradation of underlying electronics. Given the shield's 8 cm thickness, emissivity of 0.85, and a convective heat transfer coefficient of 0.25 W/m²K (due to thin lunar atmosphere), determine the minimum effective heat capacity (in J/kg·K) the material must have to remain within safe temperature limits. Assume the shield absorbs 95% of incident radiation and radiates only from its outer face.
