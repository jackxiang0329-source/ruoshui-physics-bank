# HFDPO-14152

A solar thermal power plant in the Mojave Desert uses a central receiver tower with a hemispherical receiver coating that absorbs 95% of incident solar radiation. During a clear day, solar irradiance reaches 1000 W/m² at normal incidence. The receiver, made of a high-thermal-conductivity ceramic composite, loses heat via convection (h = 25 W/m²·K) to ambient air at 30°C and radiates energy to the cold sky (effective temperature: 10°C). To maintain safe operating temperature, the receiver must not exceed 700°C. If the receiver has a radius of 2.5 m and the system must deliver 1.5 MW of useful thermal power, what is the minimum required emissivity of the coating to prevent overheating?
