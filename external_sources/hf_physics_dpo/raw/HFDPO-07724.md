# HFDPO-07724

A quantum dot solar cell uses a 2D array of nanoscale semiconductor particles (diameter ≈ 8 nm) arranged in a triangular lattice with spacing 32 nm. When illuminated by unpolarized light (λ = 600 nm), the device exhibits unexpected intensity modulations in the far-field diffraction pattern, reducing efficiency at certain angles. Engineers suspect plasmonic coupling between adjacent dots alters the effective refractive index, but the exact mechanism remains unclear. Estimate the angular deviation of the first-order diffraction peak from the direct transmission path due to lattice spacing and wavelength, and explain how a small structural disorder (±1.5 nm in spacing) could cause destructive interference at the intended output angle.
