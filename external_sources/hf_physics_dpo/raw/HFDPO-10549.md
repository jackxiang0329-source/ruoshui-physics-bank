# HFDPO-10549

A fusion reactor containment vessel uses superconducting magnets cooled by liquid helium at 4.2 K to maintain stable plasma confinement. During a sudden power surge, the magnet quench causes 15 kg of liquid helium to vaporize explosively in 3 seconds. The surrounding vacuum chamber is initially at 300 K and has a thermal capacity of 8.5 × 10⁴ J/K. Evaluate whether the resulting heat transfer to the chamber exceeds the critical threshold for structural integrity (1.2 × 10⁶ J), considering both the enthalpy of vaporization of helium and the second law constraint on heat flow direction.
