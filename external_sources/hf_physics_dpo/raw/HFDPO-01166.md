# HFDPO-01166

A Mars rover traversing a regolith-covered slope encounters unexpected slippage during ascent, despite its wheel design optimized for low rolling resistance. On closer inspection, fine dust particles (mean diameter 15 μm, density 2.6 g/cm³) are suspended in a thin, shearing layer between wheel and soil, creating a viscous drag force that reduces effective normal force. Given the wheel's contact area is 0.03 m² and the shear rate in the dust layer is 120 s⁻¹, determine the effective reduction in friction force due to drag, assuming a dust dynamic viscosity of 0.8 Pa·s. Compare this to the theoretical static friction limit to explain the slippage.
