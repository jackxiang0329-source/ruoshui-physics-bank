# HFDPO-08553

In a quantum computing lab, researchers use a 2D array of superconducting qubits cooled to 15 mK. Each qubit behaves as a two-level system with an energy splitting of 2.3 GHz and is coupled to a photonic mode in a cavity. Due to thermal fluctuations, the system occasionally occupies excited states even at ultra-low temperatures, leading to decoherence. The researchers model the distribution of occupation probabilities using a grand canonical ensemble, but notice a persistent deviation from predictions at low photon numbers. They hypothesize that the photon statistics are not purely thermal but are influenced by weak nonlinear interactions. Calculate the effective temperature of the photon field assuming a Bose-Einstein distribution with a chemical potential shift, and analyze how the deviation from ideal thermal behavior affects the qubit coherence time.
