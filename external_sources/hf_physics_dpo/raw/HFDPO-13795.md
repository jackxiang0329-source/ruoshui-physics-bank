# HFDPO-13795

A remote Arctic research station relies on a hybrid heat engine system combining a Stirling engine powered by a geothermal heat source (120°C) and a waste-heat recovery unit from diesel generators. During winter, the ambient temperature drops to –30°C, but the engine’s cold reservoir is maintained at –15°C using a buried heat sink. If the system must deliver 15 kW of net power while operating at 60% of the Carnot efficiency due to real-world irreversibilities, what is the minimum required heat input rate from the geothermal source? Consider that the waste-heat recovery unit adds an additional 8 kW of useful work via a secondary cycle, but its efficiency depends on the temperature difference between exhaust gases at 300°C and the −15°C sink. What is the total heat input needed to sustain the station’s energy demands?
