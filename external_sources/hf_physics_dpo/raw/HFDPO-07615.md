# HFDPO-07615

In a fusion reactor experiment, deuterium and tritium ions are heated to 150 million Kelvin, confined in a magnetic field, and must overcome the Coulomb barrier to fuse. The probability of tunneling through this barrier depends on the effective nuclear charge screened by the electron cloud. If the electron density near the nucleus in a plasma reaches 10²⁰ m⁻³, how does this screening reduce the effective barrier height for a 10 keV alpha particle attempting to tunnel into a tritium nucleus (Z = 1), assuming a classical Coulomb barrier of 100 keV?
