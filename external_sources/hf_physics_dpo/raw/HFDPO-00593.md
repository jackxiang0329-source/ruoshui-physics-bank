# HFDPO-00593

A space agency plans to deploy a solar-powered atmospheric drone on Venus, where the thick CO₂ atmosphere exerts 93 times Earth’s surface pressure. The drone must descend from a high-altitude balloon at 60 km altitude to 50 km to collect data, using its rotors to control descent speed despite drag forces that scale with atmospheric density and velocity squared. The drone’s mass is 12 kg, and it must maintain a steady descent of 1.5 m/s to avoid sensor turbulence. Given that the gravitational acceleration decreases slightly with altitude (from 8.87 m/s² at 60 km to 8.84 m/s² at 50 km), estimate the total work done by the drone’s rotors during the descent, considering both gravitational potential energy change and energy dissipated by atmospheric drag. Assume drag force is proportional to the square of velocity and increases with density, which follows an exponential decay with altitude.
