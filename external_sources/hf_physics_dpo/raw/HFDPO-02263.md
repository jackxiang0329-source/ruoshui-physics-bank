# HFDPO-02263

A research team at a deep-earth neutrino observatory detects a sudden spike in electron antineutrino events over a 12-hour period, coinciding with a seismic anomaly. The detector, located 2 km underground, primarily registers antineutrinos from nearby nuclear reactors (~50 km away) but also captures geoneutrinos from uranium and thorium decay in Earth’s mantle. Given the reactor antineutrino flux was stable at 1.2 × 10⁶ /cm²/s and the observed event rate increased by 28% above background, analyze whether this signal is more likely due to a transient geoneutrino burst (e.g., from a deep mantle plume) or reactor fluctuation. Assume geoneutrinos have an average energy of 3.5 MeV (vs. reactor ~2 MeV), and the detector has a 15% detection efficiency for geoneutrinos at this energy.
