# HFDPO-01259

At a remote Arctic research station, scientists detect a low-frequency rumble (15 Hz) through seismic sensors embedded in permafrost, but no surface tremors are recorded. The sound travels 120 km through layered ice and snow—each layer has distinct density and adiabatic index. The upper 30 m of snow (ρ₁ = 200 kg/m³, γ₁ = 1.3) lies over 70 m of firn (ρ₂ = 550 kg/m³, γ₂ = 1.35), beneath which is solid glacial ice (ρ₃ = 917 kg/m³, γ₃ = 1.33). The ambient temperature is –25°C. Determine the expected wave speed and phase velocity variation across each layer, and explain why the 15 Hz wave shows no attenuation over the distance—despite known absorption in snow. How might this phenomenon be exploited for non-invasive monitoring of subglacial lakes?
