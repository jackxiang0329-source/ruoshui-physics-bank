# HFDPO-06552

In a nanoscale semiconductor fabrication lab, a batch of 100 nm diameter silicon nanoparticles is being dispersed in a dielectric fluid for quantum dot solar cell assembly. Due to uneven surface charge distribution, some particles exhibit persistent clustering despite ultrasonic dispersion. Measurements show that 15% of the particles carry a net charge of +2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ C, while the rest are neutral. The particles are suspended in a fluid with dielectric constant εᵣ = 4.5, and the average separation between charged particles is 300 nm. Using electrostatic principles, determine whether the Coulomb repulsion between charged particles is sufficient to overcome the van der Waals attractive forces at this separation, and assess the feasibility of stable colloidal dispersion.
