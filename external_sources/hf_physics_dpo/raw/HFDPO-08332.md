# HFDPO-08332

In a microfluidic device designed for single-cell separation, a suspension of red blood cells (diameter ~8 μm, density 1100 kg/m³) flows through a narrow channel (width 100 μm, height 50 μm) at a mean velocity of 1.2 mm/s. The plasma (μ = 1.2 cP) exerts viscous drag, but cells also experience lift forces due to wall effects and shear gradients. The device relies on precise control of flow to isolate cells based on deformability. What is the ratio of viscous drag force to inertial force on a typical cell near the centerline, and how does this ratio affect the reliability of the separation over time if the flow rate drifts by ±5%?
