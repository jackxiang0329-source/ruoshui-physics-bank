# HFDPO-00111

A quantum computing chip operates at 15 mK in a dilution refrigerator, employing superconducting qubits coupled to a 3D microwave cavity with critical coupling. During calibration, researchers observe anomalous energy loss in the cavity—measured as a 3.2% increase in resonant linewidth—despite no apparent material defects. The cavity is shielded with a high-permeability mu-metal enclosure, but nearby power lines introduce a weak 60 Hz magnetic field fluctuation of 1.8 nT rms. Evaluate whether Maxwell’s equations, specifically Faraday’s law and Ampère-Maxwell law in time-varying, non-ideal media, can explain this loss, and determine if the observed decay is due to induced eddy currents in the enclosure or vacuum polarization at microwave frequencies.
