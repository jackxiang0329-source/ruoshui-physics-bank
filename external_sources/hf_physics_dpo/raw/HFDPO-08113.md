# HFDPO-08113

A quantum computing research team at a fusion energy startup discovers that their superconducting qubits, cooled to 15 mK in a dilution refrigerator, exhibit unexpected phase slips during gate operations. The qubits are fabricated from a thin film of niobium nitride (NbN), with a critical temperature of 16 K and a coherence length of 12 nm. During calibration, they observe that the critical current density drops by 40% after 300 hours of operation, despite no measurable temperature rise. The team suspects non-equilibrium quasiparticle poisoning due to residual microwave radiation from a nearby high-power plasma heating system. Calculate the minimum shielding effectiveness (in dB) required for the cryostat's magnetic shielding to prevent quasiparticle injection rates that exceed the qubit's relaxation rate of 5 × 10⁷ s⁻¹, assuming the quasiparticle generation rate scales with the square of the electromagnetic field intensity.
