# HFDPO-09096

A research team designing a next-generation neural implant for treating epilepsy faces a critical challenge: minimizing power dissipation in microelectrodes embedded in brain tissue while maintaining sufficient current density to stimulate neurons. The implant uses a platinum electrode with a radius of 5 μm and length 100 μm, connected via a 500 μm-long copper trace (resistivity 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m) to a microcontroller. Brain tissue has a resistivity of 200 Ω·m, and the target stimulation current is 50 nA. If the electrode's surface area is critical due to tissue impedance and heating limits, and the system must operate below 10 μW of total power dissipation in the electrode and trace, what is the maximum allowable resistance in the electrode-tissue interface, assuming all power loss occurs in the trace and electrode?
