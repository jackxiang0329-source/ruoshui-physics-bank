# HFDPO-13957

A geothermal energy plant in Iceland uses a network of submerged, inclined steel conduits to extract heat from underground magma chambers. One critical conduit, 35 meters long and weighing 8,200 N/m, is anchored at both ends and submerged in seawater at 4°C. The upper anchor point is fixed, but the lower point is on a flexible seabed bearing that allows slight vertical movement. As heat extraction increases, thermal expansion causes the lower end to rise by 1.2 cm. Calculate the axial force in the conduit due to this displacement, considering buoyancy, thermal expansion (α = 12 × 10⁻⁶/°C), and the structural stiffness of the system. Assume the conduit is perfectly straight initially and the water’s buoyant force is uniform.
