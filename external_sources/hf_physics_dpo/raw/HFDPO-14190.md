# HFDPO-14190

A new quantum sensing platform uses arrays of nanoscale grating structures to detect minute changes in refractive index from single biomolecules binding to the surface. Each grating has a period of 600 nm and is illuminated at 532 nm wavelength. When a target protein binds, it shifts the local refractive index by Δn = 1.5×10⁻⁴, causing a measurable shift in the first-order diffraction angle. However, the sensor operates in a turbid biological medium where scattering creates background interference. How can the system distinguish between the true diffraction shift due to molecular binding and a false shift caused by coherent scattering from particulates?
