# HFDPO-11562

A quantum dot array in a next-generation photovoltaic cell is designed with a periodic lattice spacing of 600 nm to enhance light absorption via constructive interference. However, the fabrication process introduces random nanoscale defects, shifting individual dot positions by up to ±15 nm. When illuminated by broadband sunlight (400–700 nm), the cell's efficiency drops by 18% at 550 nm. Model the combined effect of the intended diffraction pattern and defect-induced phase disorder to determine the effective coherence length responsible for the efficiency loss.
