# HFDPO-08064

A deep-space probe near Proxima Centauri detects a burst of high-energy neutrinos with a measured energy of 500 MeV in its rest frame. However, the neutrinos were emitted by a stellar flare on a star moving at 0.8c relative to the probe, and the emission direction was not aligned with the probe's line of sight. The probe’s onboard systems must determine the original neutrino energy in the star’s rest frame to assess the flare’s power output. What was the true energy emitted by the star, and why does the standard Doppler formula fail without considering relativistic aberration?
