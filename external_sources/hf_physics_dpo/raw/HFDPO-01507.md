# HFDPO-01507

A solar-powered weather station is mounted on a remote mountain ridge, secured by a custom tripod with legs made of carbon-fiber composite. Each leg is 2.4 m long, angled at 30° from vertical, and connected to a central spherical joint. The station’s solar array (mass 18 kg) is offset 0.6 m horizontally from the tripod’s centerline, creating a torque. The tripod must resist overturning during high-wind events (up to 35 m/s). Determine the minimum friction coefficient required between the tripod feet and the rocky soil to prevent sliding or tipping, assuming the soil can support the vertical load but fails under lateral forces exceeding 600 N per foot.
