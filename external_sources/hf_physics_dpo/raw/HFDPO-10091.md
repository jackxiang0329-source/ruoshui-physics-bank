# HFDPO-10091

During a deep-sea mining operation, a remotely operated vehicle (ROV) must extract polymetallic nodules from a sediment bed at 3,200 meters depth. The nodules are suspended in a stratified seabed layer where upward fluid flux from hydrothermal seepage creates a delicate balance between buoyancy and viscous drag. The ROV’s suction system generates a localized flow field that disturbs the sediment, but excessive shear stresses risk resuspensing fine particles into the water column, triggering turbidity plumes that could harm benthic ecosystems. Given a sediment layer with particle density 2,600 kg/m³, average diameter 3 mm, and a dynamic viscosity of seawater (1.2 × 10⁻³ Pa·s), what minimum suction velocity (relative to the sediment) must be maintained to initiate particle motion without inducing full erosion, considering the critical shear stress for incipient motion is 0.08 N/m² and the fluid is incompressible and laminar?
