# HFDPO-02553

A Mars rover’s rear wheel, with a radius of 0.35 m and mass 12 kg, begins to spin uncontrollably on loose regolith during a sand dune ascent. The coefficient of static friction between the tire and sand is 0.42, but dynamic friction drops to 0.28 once slipping starts. As the wheel spins at 8 rad/s, drag from the thin Martian atmosphere (density 0.020 kg/m³, viscosity ≈ 1.5×10⁻⁵ Pa·s) creates a torque opposing rotation. If the wheel’s moment of inertia is 0.45 kg·m², how long after slipping begins does it take for angular speed to drop to 2 rad/s?
