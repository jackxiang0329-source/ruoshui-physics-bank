# HFDPO-03594

During a high-altitude solar thermal power test, a parabolic trough collector in the Atacama Desert heats a synthetic oil flowing at 0.8 kg/s from 120°C to 380°C. The collector’s absorber tube (diameter 5 cm, emissivity 0.92) is exposed to solar irradiance of 1000 W/m² (normal incidence), but losses occur via convection (T_ambient = 25°C, h = 12 W/m²·K) and radiation. The tube operates in a vacuum envelope to minimize conduction. Given the oil’s specific heat capacity of 2200 J/kg·K, determine the required collector length needed to achieve the desired temperature rise, and then evaluate how a 5% reduction in solar irradiance due to dust accumulation would affect the outlet temperature if the length remains unchanged.
