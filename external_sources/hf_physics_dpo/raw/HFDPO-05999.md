# HFDPO-05999

A deep-space communications array on Mars receives a faint signal from a failing interstellar probe drifting near the Oort Cloud, 1.5 light-years away. The probe’s transmitter emits 50 W of power at 3.5 GHz, but its antenna is misaligned, radiating isotropically due to a gyro failure. The receiving array on Mars uses a parabolic dish with a 20-meter diameter, 78% efficiency, and operates at 3.5 GHz. Calculate the received signal power, accounting for path loss, antenna gain, and the probe’s isotropic radiation pattern. Then, determine the minimum detectable signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) needed for reliable decoding if the system noise temperature is 45 K and the bandwidth is 100 kHz.
