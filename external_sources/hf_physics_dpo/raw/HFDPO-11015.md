# HFDPO-11015

A tidal energy farm in the North Sea uses a rotating horseshoe-shaped superconducting loop (radius 12 m, resistance negligible) suspended vertically in the ocean, aligned with the Earth’s magnetic field (55 μT, dip angle 70°). As tides drive the loop to spin at 0.3 rad/s, the flux through it changes sinusoidally. Engineers observe a peak EMF of 1.4 V but must ensure the induced current doesn't trigger eddy current braking that slows the turbine. What is the minimum rotational inertia required in the loop to keep angular deceleration below 0.01 rad/s² when power is drawn?
