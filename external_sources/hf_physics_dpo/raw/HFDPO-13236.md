# HFDPO-13236

In a cryogenic fusion reactor design, a superconducting niobium-tin (Nb₃Sn) magnet must be cooled from 300 K to 77 K in under 15 minutes without triggering a normal-state transition due to localized hotspots. The material has a critical current density of 5×10⁹ A/m² at 77 K and a heat capacity of 120 J/kg·K. During cooldown, non-uniform helium-4 bath flow causes 1.8% of the magnet’s cross-section to heat at 0.4 K/s. Determine whether the local temperature rise exceeds the critical temperature for superconductivity (T_c ≈ 18 K) under these conditions, and evaluate the feasibility of this cooling protocol.
