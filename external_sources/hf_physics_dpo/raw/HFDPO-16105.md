# HFDPO-16105

In a quantum computing lab, researchers observe unexpected decoherence in a qubit based on a superconducting niobium Josephson junction operating at 15 mK. The qubit’s coherence time drops from 100 μs to 20 μs when exposed to a background magnetic field of 10 pT—far below typical shielding limits—despite using a mu-metal enclosure. The team suspects a coupling between ambient cosmic-ray-induced muons and localized electronic states in the niobium oxide layer at the junction interface. Given that the muon’s decay produces a relativistic electron with energy 10 MeV, and the effective mass of electrons in the oxide layer is 0.2 me, estimate the spatial extent of the electron wavefunction perturbation that could induce a local flux shift in the junction, assuming the perturbation couples to the superconducting phase via the Josephson energy.
