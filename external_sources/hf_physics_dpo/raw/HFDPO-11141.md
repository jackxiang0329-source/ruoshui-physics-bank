# HFDPO-11141

A deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystem thrives near a crack in the ocean floor, where superheated water (350°C, 30 bar) emerges from beneath Earth’s crust, mixing with cold seawater (2°C, 300 bar). Engineers propose a closed-loop osmotic heat engine using a selective membrane to exploit the enthalpy difference between hot vent fluid and cold ocean water, generating electricity for remote sensors. The system uses a working fluid with a heat capacity of 4.2 kJ/kg·K and operates in a thermodynamic cycle with 60% of the theoretical maximum efficiency. What is the minimum mass flow rate of vent fluid needed to produce 100 kW of net power, assuming all heat transfer occurs through the membrane and no losses beyond the cycle inefficiency?
