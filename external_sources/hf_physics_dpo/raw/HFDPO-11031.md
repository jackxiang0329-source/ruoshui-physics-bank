# HFDPO-11031

A lunar sample return mission uses a robotic arm to collect regolith from a 15° inclined slope on the Moon’s surface. The arm, modeled as a rigid, massless rod of length 2.4 m with a 12 kg scoop at the end, must lift the scoop from rest at the bottom of the slope to a vertical position above the landing module. Due to the Moon’s low gravity (1.62 m/s²) and lack of atmosphere, the system experiences negligible friction but significant inertial effects during acceleration. The arm is driven by a motor applying torque, but the controller uses a simple proportional response based on angle, not acceleration. When the arm accelerates too rapidly, the scoop oscillates, threatening sample contamination. What is the maximum constant angular acceleration the motor can apply without causing the scoop to oscillate beyond 3° from equilibrium during lift?
